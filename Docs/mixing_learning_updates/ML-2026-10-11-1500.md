### What was actually read

The authors measured a roughly 30 m tunnel with exponential sine sweeps at 48 kHz/24-bit. Six source orientations were aligned and summed to approximate an omnidirectional source; receivers were placed at approximately 8 m, 10 m and 25 m. Each IR was independently decomposed, then the three pole sets were merged into a common frequency basis so position-dependent residues and correction filters could be interpolated meaningfully.

The shared model contains 6,300 poles spanning 70 Hz–12 kHz and a 256-tap FIR correction per position. Reported time-domain normalised RMSE was below 3.5% at all three measured positions; the combined modal-plus-FIR fit reduced error two to three times versus the modal-only baseline. Runtime position movement is smoothed; residue/FIR arrays are recalculated every eighth sample and fractional delay uses cubic Lagrange interpolation. The “Aura” control stretches modes beyond the measured 12 kHz limit up to 20 kHz, so it is a creative synthesis control rather than measured-room reconstruction.

### Limited conclusion

Independent IR decompositions are not automatically safe to interpolate: corresponding modes need a common anchor or another explicitly verified correspondence. However, low reconstruction error and acceptable CPU use do not establish perceptual quality, transient preservation, stereo/mono safety or usefulness on rock drums. The paper reports no listener study, no production-mix test and no uncertainty around the fit metrics.

## Knowledge Cards

### KC-ML-20261011-1500-01 — align before interpolating measured rooms

When spatial movement is synthesized from several measured IRs, independently estimated modes can drift in frequency and damping. Direct residue interpolation is then ill-defined; a common basis or verified correspondence is required before movement is treated as coherent.

- **Confidence:** medium
- **Limit:** demonstrated for one tunnel and three measured positions; not independently replicated here

### KC-ML-20261011-1500-02 — fit error is not mix quality

Time-domain NRMSE and CPU use describe reconstruction and deployment cost, not drum attack, groove, mono compatibility, tail pumping or listener preference.

- **Confidence:** high
- **Citation check:** partial

### KC-ML-20261011-1500-03 — generated brilliance is not restoration

The source labels stretching modal frequencies beyond the measured 12 kHz range as an “Aura” creative control. Its output should not be described as recovered measured-room information.

- **Confidence:** high
