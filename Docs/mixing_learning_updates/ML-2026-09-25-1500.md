## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1500-01` — off-manifold synthesizer inversion

- **Title:** *Off-manifold robustness in synthesizer inversion with joint distribution flow matching*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29320>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.29320v1`
- **Publication date:** 2026-09-24
- **Discovery date:** 2026-09-25
- **Depth:** full HTML, including model variants, data, inference, metrics, ablations, tables and limitations
- **Project/demo:** <https://benhayes.net/synth-jdf/>

The task is to infer a plausible Surge XT or Dexed parameter set whose resynthesis approximates a reference. Because real recordings lie outside the synthesizer manifold and several parameter sets may sound alike, this is a projection problem rather than recovery of a uniquely true preset.

The models use 44.1-kHz stereo three-second clips and log-mel inputs. The off-manifold pool contains 617,016 training one-shots plus 10,000 validation and 10,000 test items from Freesound and a proprietary library. It is broad one-shot evidence, not a full-song, multitrack, effect-chain or rock-mix test.

On Surge real audio, JDF reduces MSS from 15.50 to 7.68 relative to the encoder-conditioned baseline, but SOT becomes worse: 0.317 versus 0.232. The paper itself treats SOT as a fine-spectral anchor. A metric panel is therefore necessary; average improvement cannot be converted into a listening or fidelity claim.

Most importantly, JDF-A with unpaired real audio is almost tied with JDF without it: off-manifold MSS is 7.69 versus 7.68. The strong result is joint-density/noisy-conditioning architecture, not demonstrated benefit from the real-audio marginal. The conditional baseline also uses a different AST-conditioned architecture, so attribution to the objective alone is incomplete.

Partial conditioning noise and CFG are inference sweeps. The favourable neighbourhood around `tau=0.1` is task/model-specific; guidance response changes by model and domain. Neither is a reusable production preset. No human evaluation or systematic failure taxonomy is reported.

**Bounded conclusion:** treat the output as a plausible synth-manifold projection. Separate objective, architecture and unpaired-data effects, and require multi-metric plus loudness-matched listening validation before any reference-matching use.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1500-02` — NoteSep

- **Title:** *On a Separate Note: Robust Score-Informed Note Separation with a Two-Stream TFC–TDF U-Net and Adaptive Set Ownership*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29071>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.29071v1`
- **Publication date:** 2026-09-24
- **Discovery date:** 2026-09-25
- **Depth:** full HTML, including architecture, compute, datasets, baselines, metrics, editing and real-transfer evaluation
- **Repository:** <https://github.com/ben2002chou/notesep>

NoteGrab requires aligned pitch, onset and offset for each note. Its harmonic and percussive streams are linked by cross-attention; Adaptive Set Ownership then reallocates mixture energy among concurrent note estimates. It is therefore score-informed separation, not transcription or automatic wrong-note detection.

SCNS-Train has 25,729 mixtures and 743,920 note targets; SCNS-Eval uses disjoint scores, libraries and 16 held-out instruments. NoteSep reaches median SI-SDR 7.39 dB versus 2.49 dB for score-informed NMF. The Melodyne comparison is not parity-controlled: Melodyne has no score and only 54.42% of scored notes are matched by the paper's pitch/onset rule.

The signal contract is 16-kHz mono with a 1,024-sample STFT and 128-sample hop. Editing uses stored note estimates rather than subtracting them from the input mixture; 13,892 synthetic edits are evaluated with a PEAQ-derived `2f` metric. No listener study establishes transparent editing.

The real-recording transfer aggregates estimates into instrument stems on four PHENICX-Anechoic pieces and ten Bach10 quartets. That is useful classical transfer evidence, but it does not cover full-band stereo rock, drums, distorted guitars, vocal bleed or room-heavy productions.

The paper says the artifact supplies code, datasets and checkpoints, but the pinned public repository contains only a README saying they are coming soon. No root licence is present. Reproduction is currently blocked.

**Bounded conclusion:** use only when a trustworthy aligned score exists. Audit independent-note sum inconsistency, octave leakage and preservation of unedited material before considering local pre-mix editing.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1500-03` — additive audio representations

- **Title:** *Do Audio Representations Compose Additively?*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27187>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.27187v1`
- **Publication date:** 2026-09-23
- **Discovery date:** 2026-09-25
- **Depth:** full HTML, including data, CCA, additive reconstruction, permutation/overlap baselines, metrics, results and limitations

The work tests frozen CLAP, HuBERT and Wav2Vec2 embeddings on FSD50K and CHiME-Home. It fits per-source contributions using source-label sets and predicts held-out combination means. Across larger repeated holdouts, CLAP beats both permuted and label-overlap baselines on FSD50K; the speech models do not consistently beat label overlap.

This supports partial additive structure in CLAP, not ideal decomposition. Every encoder retains reconstruction residuals. The labels are clip-level, unordered semantic tags; reverberation, masking, nonlinear encoding and scene structure can all remain in the residual.

The study does not vary or recover fader levels, pan, timing, dynamics, spectral balance, phase or mix quality. High embedding cosine therefore does not prove waveform additivity, source independence or a useful automatic-mixing objective.

**Bounded conclusion:** CLAP source-vector arithmetic may be a secondary diagnostic. It must never replace waveform conservation, calibrated loudness, phase, onset, peak and listening checks.

## Knowledge cards

1. Real-audio synth inversion returns a plausible projection onto the synthesizer manifold, not uniquely recovered ground truth.
2. In synth-JDF, joint-density training explains most of the reported gain; the added unpaired-real marginal is nearly tied with JDF alone.
3. Improved MSS/wMFCC/RMS can coexist with worse SOT; one aggregate metric cannot certify timbral match.
4. Conditioning noise near `tau=0.1` is a paper-specific sweep result, not a reusable setting.
5. CFG can help or hurt under domain shift; guidance must be swept per model and task.
6. One-shot inversion results do not validate full-song reference matching, effect automation or mix control.
7. NoteSep requires aligned pitch/onset/offset for every note and is not autonomous transcription or correction.
8. Independently estimated notes can violate mixture consistency; joint ownership can reduce, not eliminate, that risk.
9. A 16-kHz mono separation result does not validate full-band stereo rock editing.
10. Synthetic stored-estimate editing metrics are not listener evidence for transparent in-mix edits.
11. CLAP embeddings are partially, not perfectly, additive across semantic source labels.
12. Embedding additivity does not imply waveform additivity or invariance to reverb, masking, time, level, pan or dynamics.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1500-01` — joint training versus real-audio marginal

- **Baseline A:** pinned JDF model trained on paired synthesizer audio only.
- **Variant B:** identical JDF-A with the unpaired-real audio marginal enabled.
- **Only changed factor:** unpaired-real marginal training.
- **Controls:** same synth, architecture, parameter distribution, steps, seed set and test items; rights-cleared off-manifold rock one-shots; no commercial audio upload. Separate raw safety renders and BS.1770-matched blind copies.
- **Measure:** MSS, wMFCC, SOT, RMS-envelope similarity, onset/envelope error, sample/true peak, seed spread and blind match/preference.
- **Protect:** source identity, attack, decay, pitch, stereo and headroom.
- **Success:** consistent improvement beyond uncertainty without loss on SOT, attacks or preference.
- **Cancel:** near-tie, metric reversal, unstable seeds or loudness explains preference.
- **Blocker:** public code/weights and a free reproducible configuration are not available.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1500-02` — conditioning-noise sensitivity

- **Baseline A:** the same pinned inversion model with clean conditioning, `tau=1.0` in the paper's convention.
- **Variant B:** partial conditioning noise at `tau=0.1`.
- **Only changed factor:** conditioning noise level.
- **Controls:** identical reference, model, inferred-parameter sampling, CFG, seed and synthesizer render; raw outputs plus loudness-matched blind copies.
- **Measure:** metric panel, transient/envelope error, parameter stability, seed spread, sample/true peak and blind match.
- **Protect:** recognizable source detail and attacks.
- **Success:** robust off-manifold match without detail loss or metric reversal.
- **Cancel:** coarse approximation wins only MSS/RMS while SOT, attack or preference worsens.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1500-03` — independent notes versus ASO

- **Baseline A:** independently summed NoteGrab estimates.
- **Variant B:** the same estimates after Adaptive Set Ownership.
- **Only changed factor:** ASO ownership/reallocation stage.
- **Controls:** one rights-cleared, accurately score-aligned guitar/bass/keyboard phrase; same estimates, timing, gains and remix; no pitch/retime edit. Raw outputs and loudness-matched listening copies.
- **Measure:** unedited-sum null, target SI-SDR, off-target energy, octave confusion, onsets, phase/mono, sample/true peak and blind transparency.
- **Protect:** attacks, note tails, concurrent voices and unchanged material.
- **Success:** reduced duplication/leakage and better sum conservation without damaging attacks or ambience.
- **Cancel:** score mismatch, deleted notes, new artefacts or only loudness improvement.
- **Blocker:** public code/checkpoints are still coming soon.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1500-04` — CLAP additivity as a diagnostic

- **Baseline A:** embedding predicted by adding isolated source contributions.
- **Variant B:** embedding measured from the exact linear waveform sum.
- **Only changed factor:** additive embedding prediction versus observed mixed-waveform embedding.
- **Controls:** rights-cleared stems, fixed sample rate, encoder revision, preprocessing, gains, pan and timing; repeat with exactly one preregistered source mute.
- **Measure:** cosine, relative Frobenius error, CKA/retrieval where sample count permits, waveform null and explicit loudness/onset/phase checks.
- **Protect:** no acceptance or audio-processing decision is made from CLAP alone.
- **Success:** bounded, repeatable residual with correct directional response to the source mute.
- **Cancel:** residual is scene-dependent, sign/direction is wrong or CLAP conflicts with waveform truth.
