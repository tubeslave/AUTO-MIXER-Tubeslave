## Outcome

The pinned Capicola implementation was audited file-by-file and checked against an independent full primary dataset paper. The review changes the practical conclusion from “test this paper implementation at unity” to “first freeze a specific executable and prove that it is the same algorithm/mode being claimed.” Current Capicola 1.1.0 contains transient-driven multi-grain behaviour that its README explicitly excludes from the paper’s public baseline examples. The paper’s listening result therefore cannot validate the current firmware as a whole.

The public host tests are useful engineering evidence, but they do not establish a sample-null or transparent music round trip: the unity live-effect test uses a 220 Hz sine after warm-up with RMS and slew tolerances; it does not require equality. Tests were inspected, not executed. No CI workflow was found in the audited tree.

An independent time-scale-modification dataset paper was read in full. It confirms strong content and ratio-direction dependence and shows that objective measures available at the time were poor proxies for subjective quality. A later objective-measure paper was found, but only metadata and abstract were reliably read; its aggregate correlation is not promoted to a project acceptance threshold.

## Source Cards

### SRC-ML-20261013-1800-01 — A Time-Scale Modification Dataset with Subjective Quality Labels

- **Authors:** Timothy Roberts; Kuldip K. Paliwal
- **DOI:** `10.1121/10.0001567`
- **Venue/date:** Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 148(1), July 2020
- **URLs:** <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32752755/> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00848> · <https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/time-scale-modification-dataset-subjective-quality-labels>
- **Reading depth:** full 13-page paper
- **Discovered:** 2026-10-13
- **Citation check:** `partial`
- **Dataset licence:** CC BY 4.0 on the official project page

The dataset uses 108 short mono sources: 34 music, 37 solo-instrument and 37 voice clips, 16-bit/44.1 kHz, normalised to ±1, mean duration about 3.7 s. The training partition contains 88 sources processed by six algorithms at ten time-scale ratios (5,280 files); the test partition contains 20 sources, three unseen algorithms and four ratios (240 files).

The authors collected 42,529 ratings from 263 participants across 633 sessions. Forty-five sessions / 2,102 ratings (4.94%) were removed as outliers. Expert listeners supplied 52.36% of retained ratings; generalised inter-rater reliability increased from 0.871 to 0.909 after normalisation.

The bounded result relevant here is conditionality: slower-than-original processing was generally rated worse than the inverse faster condition, and PV/WSOLA preference changed with ratio direction. Artifacts include transient duplication/skipping, phasiness, smearing and metallic noise. No universal algorithm follows.

A known uTVS implementation error created discontinuities around ratios 0.9–1.1 and was retained in the subjective dataset, though corrected for a later evaluation subset. Those near-unity labels must not calibrate a project 1.0x null.

Transfer is limited by mono, short, level-normalised material; the study does not test stereo image, multitrack phase relations, project rock stems, raw gain behaviour or exact sample-null transparency.
