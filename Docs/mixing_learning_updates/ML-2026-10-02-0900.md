## Literature source cards

### SRC-ML-20261002-0900-01 — Group Delay Manipulation for Creative Sound Transformation with the Giant FFT

- **Stable ID:** `conference:dafx:2026:DAFx26_paper_02`.
- **Primary source:** Ted Apel, DAFx26, 1–4 September 2026; [open PDF](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_02.pdf), CC BY 4.0.
- **Reading depth:** complete six-page paper: abstract, background, method, all three transformations/results, limitations, future work and references.
- **Method:** one FFT spans the whole padded signal. Magnitude peaks are segmented; segments with similar magnitude-weighted group delay are grouped. A constant group-delay offset moves a group in time while retaining the magnitude spectrum; sinusoidal modulation produces repeated copies.
- **Evidence actually shown:** synthetic four-note reversal, a recorded glockenspiel sequence, a harpsichord-note displacement, and periodic-copy examples. The paper reports exact no-transform reconstruction to floating-point precision and qualitative audible/spectrogram examples, not a controlled listening test or a quantitative comparison with windowed methods.
- **Limits:** the method works best for pitched, temporally non-overlapping events whose components do not share frequency bins. Shared bins in sustained/polyphonic material represent amplitude-weighted mixtures and cannot be moved independently. Segment width trades residual energy against moving energy from other events; audible artifacts are acknowledged. No public implementation was verified or run.
- **Bounded conclusion:** useful as an offline creative transformation candidate for isolated events. It is not evidence for transparent edit repair, multichannel microphone phase alignment, bleed control or automatic mixing.
- **Independence:** single-author primary paper and same-author examples; no independent reproduction found in this search.
- **Citation check:** `partial`.

### SRC-ML-20261002-0900-02 — Perceptually Motivated Alignment and Interpolation of Pitch-Aligned Time-Frequency Representations

- **Stable ID:** `conference:dafx:2026:DAFx26_paper_20`.
- **Primary source:** Shahan Nercessian, Jeff Sontag and Alejandro Koretzky, DAFx26, 1–4 September 2026; [open PDF](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_20.pdf), CC BY 4.0.
- **Reading depth:** complete eight-page paper, including methods, experimental tables, conclusion and stated future work.
- **Method:** invertible extensions of the tonal interval vector, permutation search under perceptually weighted distances, circular optimal transport on the circle of fourths/fifths, and a learned geometric scale representation.
- **Evidence actually shown:** one synthetic musical alignment example plus 1024 randomly generated chromagram pairs; C-major-to-C-minor interpolation examples; and a learned representation trained for 20,000 steps on 106 internally constructed scale profiles.
- **Limits:** no listener study, no real-rock multitrack evaluation, no held-out external scale corpus and no evidence that lower representation distance predicts a better mix. The paper itself leaves alternative alignment objectives and OT integration for future work.
- **Bounded conclusion:** potentially useful for harmonic-layer or arrangement tooling. The word “alignment” here concerns pitch-class representations, not waveform timing, microphone phase or sample alignment; it creates no current mixing rule.
- **Independence:** all evaluation and demos are from the author team; `citation_check:partial`.

## Candidate rules

### RULE-ML-20261002-0900-01 — Offline group-delay creative transform gate

- **Source:** `SRC-ML-20261002-0900-01`.
- **When to apply:** only as a consciously creative offline audition on an isolated pitched event or a short sequence with little frequency overlap, after the ordinary arrangement/edit already works.
- **What to change:** uniformly displace exactly one preregistered coherent spectral group in time; keep the magnitude spectrum and every other processing stage fixed.
- **Candidate range / origin:** no transferable displacement range. The paper reports segment widths of 8,000–12,000 bins for its examples, but FFT length and signal duration make those counts nonportable; select locally and record the chosen value.
- **Protect:** onset identity, attack/decay, groove, pitch envelope, stereo/mono compatibility, boundaries and headroom.
- **Verify:** raw safety render plus randomized loudness-matched A/B; inspect onset/energy timing, magnitude delta, phase/group-delay continuity, sample/true peak, crest factor, boundary energy and stereo correlation.
- **Cancel:** low-level energy remains at the original position, adjacent events move, pre/post ringing or smearing appears, mono/stereo coherence regresses, or preference disappears after level matching.
- **Confidence:** `low_to_medium_single_primary_creative_method_no_listening_test`.
- **auto_apply:** `false`.

### RULE-ML-20261002-0900-02 — Keep harmonic and waveform alignment domains separate

- **Source:** `SRC-ML-20261002-0900-02`.
- **When to apply:** when cataloguing or prototyping arrangement/harmonic-layer matching, not when correcting multichannel timing or phase.
- **What to change:** tag PTFR/TIV outputs as harmonic-role candidates only; do not route their distances into mic-alignment, EQ, dynamics or gain decisions.
- **Candidate range / origin:** none; the paper's weights and optimizer settings are representation-specific research parameters, not audio settings.
- **Protect:** original key/voicing intent, chromatic colour, riff identity and section role.
- **Verify:** an independently defined real-music corpus and blind preference/role tests are required before any project use.
- **Cancel:** apparent improvement is only lower self-defined representation distance, or harmonic identity/arrangement intent changes.
- **Confidence:** `medium_for_domain_separation_low_for_mix_utility`.
- **auto_apply:** `false`.

- **Success:** B is consistently preferred for the intended creative motion after level matching and shows no protected-quality regression.
- **Cancel:** B wins only when louder, or any residual duplicate onset, adjacent-event motion, ringing, smearing, image/mono regression or boundary artifact appears.
