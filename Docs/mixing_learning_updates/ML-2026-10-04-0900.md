## Literature / scientific sources

### `SRC-ML-20261004-0900-01` — full text read

**Diagonal Complex-Valued State Space Models for System Identification and Modeling of Metal Plate Reverbs** — Matthias Bittner, Matthias Wess, Dominik Dallinger, Daniel Schnöll and Axel Jantsch, DAFx-26, CC BY 4.0.  
URL: https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_03.pdf

Reading depth: all seven pages, equations, experiment design, both result tables, limitations and references.

Bounded findings:

- The restricted diagonal complex-valued SSM is mathematically equivalent to a parallel bank of second-order all-pole modal filters; Matrix Pencil estimates are used to initialise poles before gradient optimisation.
- The reported benchmark is synthetic and in-sample: 50 randomly synthesised one-second plate IRs at 44.1 kHz, five state sizes (`H=128…2048`), 500 separately overfit models, 5,000 optimisation steps each and lowest-loss checkpoint selection.
- Matrix-Pencil initialisation reduced reconstruction error against the S5 initialisation at every tested state size. For example, normalised L2 was `0.155 vs 0.259` at `H=128` and `0.004 vs 0.034` at `H=2048`; normalised spectral-magnitude error was `0.624 vs 0.635` and `0.103 vs 0.449`, respectively.
- Better output reconstruction did not imply faithful parameter recovery: gain error grew strongly with state size (`Rw=1.124` at `H=128`, `14.149` at `H=2048` for Matrix Pencil), which the authors attribute to outliers and leave for regularisation/pruning work.

Limits: synthetic IRs only; no controlled listening test; no real measured plate set; no target-host real-time benchmark; no paper-specific licensed implementation found. This supports an engineering test strategy, not a preferred reverb sound or an automatic mixing rule.
