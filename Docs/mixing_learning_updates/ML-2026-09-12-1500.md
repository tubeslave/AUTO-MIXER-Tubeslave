# Mixing Learning Update — 2026-09-12 15:00 MSK

**Update ID:** `ML-2026-09-12-1500`  
**Deep topic:** bleed, gating/expansion, drum-room control, parallel room compression and multichannel debleeding  
**State:** research only; `candidate / auto_apply:false`; all audio experiments `not_run`

## What is genuinely new

1. A gate/expander and a multichannel debleeding model are not interchangeable. A gate changes the channel envelope from a detector; it cannot remove bleed that coincides with the wanted hit while the gate is open. A separator estimates sources jointly and can reduce coincident bleed, but can also alter transients, decay, phase and timbre.
2. Bleed is not globally an error. Room and inter-microphone pickup can supply kit cohesion, decay and section energy. The objective must be role-dependent: suppress distracting contamination while protecting intentional ambience and the overhead image.
3. Terrell et al. separated objectively optimised gate timing/threshold from the subjective attenuation amount. This supports exposing maximum reduction (`range/floor`) as an artistic control instead of defaulting to silence.
4. A 2026 permutation-equivariant debleeding study improves robustness to channel order and unseen simulated layouts, but its evidence is synthetic, fixed to five channels, string-heavy and SDR-only. It is a research lead, not a production-ready drum rule.
5. Room-mic compression and automation are section-level arrangement tools: bring energy into selected downbeats or choruses and reduce room during cluttered rolls, instead of assuming one static room level.
6. Parallel drum returns should preserve the spatial logic of the source kit unless a deliberate contrast survives mono and full-mix checks. A loudness jump in an online A/B is not proof of better processing.

## Literature and official documentation actually read

### SRC-ML-20260912-1500-01 — Pandey et al. (2026 preprint)

- **Title:** *Learning Input-Channel Permutation Equivariance for Multi-Channel Source Separation: Reducing Bleeding in Small Music Ensembles*
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16551
- **Publication date:** 2026-06-15; **discovered:** 2026-09-12
- **Depth:** full HTML manuscript read, including formulation, architecture, datasets, tables and limitations.
- **Method:** the same random permutation is applied to close-mic inputs and targets during training, encouraging channel-permutation equivariance without permutation-invariant training. Hybrid Demucs maps five mono inputs to five mono outputs; STFT window 4096, hop 1024; time-domain L1 loss.
- **Reported evidence:** on simulated URMP strings in unseen room/layout conditions, the magnitude-input model with permutation training reported median SDR improvement of 5.3 dB versus 0.3 dB without it. The numbers are paper results, not measurements on project audio.
- **Limits:** simulated RIR/bleed, fixed five-channel topology, string-heavy SynthSOD training, SDR on one-second frames, no subjective mixing test, no rock drums and no real clean close-mic ground truth. No public implementation was found in the available GitHub search.

### SRC-ML-20260912-1500-02 — Terrell, Reiss and Sandler

- **Title:** *Automatic Noise Gate Settings for Drum Recordings Containing Bleed from Secondary Sources*
- **DOI:** `10.1155/2010/465417`
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1155/2010/465417.pdf
- **Publication date:** 2011-02-16; **discovered:** 2026-09-12
- **Depth:** complete open PDF read.
- **Method:** estimate threshold, attack, release and hold to minimise artifacts/maximise source-to-artifact ratio under a required bleed-reduction constraint; leave gain/range as the user’s subjective control.
- **Useful boundary:** short release may close before later bleed but truncate kick decay; long release preserves decay but passes more bleed. Coincident bleed remains while the gate is open.
- **Limits:** only kick with secondary hi-hat/snare/cymbal/tom bleed; commercial BFD2 samples arranged into a simple 120 BPM pattern; manually exact onsets and a clean kick exemplar; no formal listening test. Values such as 1 ms minimum attack, about 40 ms hold and −60 dB requested reduction belong to that experiment and are not presets.

### SRC-ML-20260912-1500-03 — FabFilter Pro-G dynamic controls

- **URL:** https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-g/using/dynamiccontrols
- **Depth:** complete official page read.
- **Verified details:** threshold defines opening; ratio above roughly 5:1 behaves increasingly like gating; range/floor caps attenuation. The documented 4:1 and 20 dB examples describe this implementation, not universal starting settings.

### SRC-ML-20260912-1500-04 — FabFilter Pro-G time controls

- **URL:** https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-g/using/timecontrols
- **Depth:** complete official page read.
- **Verified details:** hysteresis reduces flutter near threshold; hold sets a minimum open time; release is programme-dependent; fast attack can preserve drum transients. Lookahead up to 10 ms can open before the transient but adds latency and is implementation-specific.

## Candidate rules

All rules are `auto_apply:false`.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-01 — choose_bleed_operator_explicitly

- **When:** unwanted cross-pickup obscures a drum or destabilises processing.
- **Change:** diagnose whether the task is envelope cleanup, partial expansion, manual editing or joint source estimation before selecting an operator.
- **Parameter range/origin:** no fixed range; compare partial attenuation with hard mute. The separation of timing/threshold from user-controlled gain comes from Terrell et al.
- **Protect:** wanted hit, decay, cymbal continuity, overhead image and groove.
- **Verify:** full-kit and full-mix A/B, mono, transient shape and inter-channel timing.
- **Undo:** if isolation improves but decay, image or musical glue worsens.
- **Confidence:** high.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-02 — partial_range_before_hard_gate

- **When:** hard gating sounds abrupt, chatters or makes the kit unnaturally dry.
- **Change:** test a finite range/floor before full attenuation; tune threshold only after the desired maximum reduction is audible in context.
- **Parameter range/origin:** project test grid `−6/−12/−24 dB/hard mute`; proposed experiment values, not source prescriptions.
- **Protect:** decay, room continuity and quiet ghost notes.
- **Verify:** loudness-matched section A/B and false-open/false-close count.
- **Undo:** if remaining bleed still masks the target or deeper attenuation causes audible holes.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-03 — gate_time_from_decay_and_events

- **When:** gating kick, snare or tom close microphones.
- **Change:** derive attack/lookahead, hold and release from wanted transient/decay and the next interfering onset, not a global preset.
- **Parameter range/origin:** no universal milliseconds; Pro-G’s 10 ms maximum lookahead is implementation-specific, while the paper’s 1 ms/40 ms examples are restricted to its synthetic kick test.
- **Protect:** leading edge, sustain, ghost notes and groove.
- **Verify:** onset alignment, envelope overlay, solo for diagnosis and full-kit acceptance.
- **Undo:** any transient loss, tail truncation, flutter or false trigger.
- **Confidence:** high.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-04 — automate_room_by_section_role

- **When:** static room level creates clutter in fills but the chorus lacks size or energy.
- **Change:** test sectional or hit-level room automation instead of globally reducing/removing room.
- **Parameter range/origin:** `−3/0/+3 dB` is a project experiment grid; the qualitative move comes from Barresi.
- **Protect:** groove, snare focus, cymbal decay and believable room continuity.
- **Verify:** blind sparse/fill/chorus comparisons at matched programme loudness.
- **Undo:** pumping, audible room discontinuity or masked lead role.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-05 — parallel_drum_image_coherence

- **When:** building a parallel drum or crushed-room return.
- **Change:** begin with pan relationships matching the source/overheads; change them only as a deliberate spatial effect.
- **Parameter range/origin:** no transferable width number.
- **Protect:** stable kick/snare centre, tom movement, cymbal image and mono compatibility.
- **Verify:** return solo for diagnosis, then full mix, mono and L/R image comparison.
- **Undo:** image pull, combing, cymbal splash or phasey movement.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-06 — compress_room_for_role_not_level

- **When:** room microphones should add density or excitement without replacing dry-kit definition.
- **Change:** compare no compression, one strong stage and two lighter stages; blend/automate the return in context.
- **Parameter range/origin:** the video’s approximately 20:1 room example is style-specific and requires visual verification; no automatic ratio/GR target.
- **Protect:** kick/snare attack, cymbal smoothness, low-frequency stability and section contrast.
- **Verify:** level-matched blind A/B; listen for pumping and cymbal modulation.
- **Undo:** louder-only preference, harsh wash or weakened impact.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### R-ML-20260912-1500-07 — debleed_metrics_need_perception

- **When:** evaluating automatic debleeding/separation.
- **Change:** combine SDR with transient, spectral, spatial and human ratings; do not promote a model from SDR alone.
- **Parameter range/origin:** none.
- **Protect:** instrument identity, microtiming, decay, ambience and stereo phase.
- **Verify:** legal multitrack A/B with matched loudness, hidden condition and Dmitry rating across multiple sections.
- **Undo:** metric improves without consistent listening benefit.
- **Confidence:** high.

## Handoff to mixing agents

Do not equate cleaner close microphones with a better drum sound. Select gate, expander, manual edit or joint debleeding by failure mode; preserve coincident hits, decay, overhead image and useful room. Begin with finite attenuation, verify in the whole kit and full mix, and reject any SDR gain that damages transients or space. All new rules remain advisory pending Dmitry’s loudness-matched blind evaluation.
