### SRC-ML-20261008-1800-01 — Clifford & Reiss, JAES 2013

- stable key: `aes:e-lib:17072`
- title: *Using Delay Estimation to Reduce Comb Filtering of Arbitrary Musical Sources*
- authors: Alice Clifford; Joshua D. Reiss
- publication: *Journal of the Audio Engineering Society*, 61(11), 917–927, November 2013
- free author-uploaded full text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287427573_Using_Delay_Estimation_to_Reduce_Comb_Filtering_of_Arbitrary_Musical_Sources
- AES record: https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=17072
- reading depth: `full_text_theory_simulation_real_recordings_results_conclusion_limitations`
- source type: `peer_reviewed_engineering_report`

The theoretical model explains that summing a direct and delayed copy produces comb filtering, and that GCC-PHAT delay accuracy depends on usable bandwidth and spectral leakage. The controlled simulation used ten-second filtered-noise signals, a fixed ten-sample delay at 44.1 kHz, seven window functions, a 2048-sample frame and an accuracy tolerance of ±2 samples. These are study parameters, not production presets.

The real-recording evaluation used two microphones at arbitrary distances from one loudspeaker in an acoustically treated room, 20 musical excerpts of 30 seconds and varied frame sizes. The study assumed a point source to focus on bandwidth rather than real instrument radiation. Across excerpts and frame sizes, mean reported accuracy was 90.52% for Hann, 86.24% for Blackman and 40.14% for rectangular windows. With Hann, the mean estimate for each tested instrument was within one sample of the known 50-sample delay, but perfect frame accuracy was not obtained in the recording environment.

Bounded conclusion: Hann or Blackman is a defensible estimator choice for this method, especially for lower-bandwidth musical signals, but no single result guarantees correct alignment in bleed, moving-source, multi-source, nonlinear-processing or live conditions. There was no listener preference test and no automatic mix-controller evaluation. The article states that MATLAB code and audio were free, but the cited project URL was inaccessible now; `reproducibility:partial_unverified_current_artifacts`.

### SRC-ML-20261008-1800-02 — Ableton Live 12 latency documentation

- URL: https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-audio-effect-reference/#external-audio-effect
- help: https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010545559-How-Latency-Works
- reading depth: `full_relevant_sections_external_audio_effect_latency_pdc_driver_error`
- source type: `official_product_documentation`

Live states that external effects add latency that it cannot automatically detect; the Hardware Latency control is the manual correction. Samples retain the same sample count across sample-rate changes, while milliseconds retain time; fine tuning may use samples. Global plug-in delay compensation aligns reported software paths, but interface reporting can be inaccurate and Driver Error Compensation affects recorded placement rather than real-time latency. This confirms that a DAW's existing compensation is not proof of zero residual external-path delay.

## Knowledge Cards

### KC-ML-20261008-1800-01 — distinguish transport delay from acoustic arrival

External insert round-trip delay and microphone arrival-time differences are not the same intervention. Transport residual may be calibrated and removed; microphone arrival can encode source geometry, room and artistic depth. Correct only the delay class named by the experiment. Confidence: `high_definition / medium_workflow`.

### KC-ML-20261008-1800-02 — GCC-PHAT needs a signal/window contract

A GCC-PHAT result is incomplete without sample rate, frame/window, signal class, search range, confidence/rejection rule and reference/target convention. The JAES experiment supports Hann/Blackman over rectangular windows in its tested design, but its percentages do not transfer directly to a live multi-source drum kit. Confidence: `high_for_study / medium_transfer`.

### KC-ML-20261008-1800-03 — nonlinear parallel processing breaks pure-delay equivalence

The reviewed GCC-PHAT study estimates delay between recordings of the same source. A compressed/saturated branch changes envelope and spectrum, so it is not simply a delayed copy. Calibrate transport latency with a bypass/unity path or dedicated DAW ping, then evaluate the processed blend separately. Confidence: `high_signal_model / medium_workflow`.
