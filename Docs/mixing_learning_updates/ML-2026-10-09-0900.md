## Scientific Source Card

### SRC-ML-20261009-0900-01 — PEACE: joint DSP-code/audio embeddings

- stable key: `arxiv:2610.03405`
- title: *PEACE: Joint Embeddings of DSP Effects Code and Audio*
- authors: David Braun; Adam Finkelstein
- publication status: ISMIR 2026 paper; arXiv v1 dated 2026-10-02
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03405v1
- code: https://github.com/DBraun/PEACE
- reading depth: `full_text_method_dataset_metrics_results_limitations_plus_repository_readme_and_license`
- paper licence: CC BY 4.0
- citation_check: `partial`

PEACE pairs an AFx-Rep audio encoder with either a fine-tuned T5 Faust-code encoder or a graph encoder over Faust block-diagram algebra. Training/evaluation use 21 Faust effects, series chains of length 1–3, and 200,000 training, 8,192 validation and 4,096 test audio-code pairs. Wet audio is normalized to -18 LUFS, so retrieval is deliberately prevented from exploiting gross loudness cues.

The headline aggregate retrieval result is not uniform evidence across processors. In the 1,024-item single-effect gallery, overall R@1 was 16.8% for BoxGraph and 18.1% for T5; R@10 was 44.3% and 45.2%. Time-based effects were much easier than several dynamics/nonlinear/spatial cases: BoxGraph R@1 was 2.5% for compressor, 4.6% for distortion, 0.3% for gate, 0.5% for limiter and 0.2% for stereo width, versus 43.5% for Freeverb and 45.1% for Zita reverb. The authors attribute part of this gap to log-mel features and note that limiter/gate/width can be observationally inactive for some inputs.

On the out-of-domain professional-plugin RIR benchmark, scratch BoxGraph achieved 53.5% R@10 versus 28.3% for pretrained AFx-Rep, but this evaluation covers reverb only. With all parameters masked, BoxGraph retrieved exact ordered two-effect topology at 17.4% R@1 and 61.4% R@10 over 420 ordered pairs; the supervised scratch classifier reached 21.4% and 65.9%. Seeds differed by as much as 4.9 R@1 points, and order-tolerant scoring is partly justified because LTI effects may commute.

Limitations reported by the authors: random rather than engineer-curated parameters, random source/preset pairing, some source audio not fully dry, no user study, out-of-domain testing limited to reverb, and large performance variance across effects. This work therefore supports a retrieval research candidate, not a quality score or automatic mixing rule.

## Knowledge Cards

### KC-ML-20261009-0900-01 — effect retrieval is not mix-quality assessment

A code/audio embedding can rank a known gallery without establishing musical preference, fidelity, production quality or correct processing for the song. PEACE had no listener study. Confidence: `high_definition / high_source_specific`.

### KC-ML-20261009-0900-02 — effect-family performance must be reported

Aggregate retrieval obscures large differences between reverb/delay and compressor/gate/limiter/stereo-width cases. Any project trial must report per-effect-family recall and inactive-effect failures, not one overall score. Confidence: `high_within_paper / medium_transfer`.

### KC-ML-20261009-0900-03 — parameter masking changes the task

Masking parameters tests topology retrieval, not parameter estimation. Order-tolerant scoring may be legitimate for commuting LTI effects but must not erase order differences for nonlinear or time-varying processors. Confidence: `high_task_definition / medium_chain_transfer`.

### KC-ML-20261009-0900-04 — preprocessing and version are part of the metric contract

Sample rate, excerpt policy, LUFS normalization, feature padding, encoder checkpoint and embedding head are part of the result. Proceedings and arXiv numbers differ for two evaluations because padding was recomputed. Confidence: `high_repository_documentation`.
