## Source Card

### SRC-ML-20261007-1200-01 — parameter automation in a dynamic range compressor

- title: *Parameter Automation in a Dynamic Range Compressor*
- authors: Dimitrios Giannoulis; Michael Massberg; Joshua D. Reiss
- publication: *Journal of the Audio Engineering Society* 61(10), 716–726; publication date `2013-10-06`
- DOI: none listed by AES
- official metadata and correction notice: https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=16965
- legal author-hosted full text: https://joshreiss.github.io/documents/2013/Giannoulis%20Massberg%20Reiss%20-%20dynamic%20range%20compression%20automation%20-%20JAES%202013.pdf
- reading depth: complete 11-page paper, including topology, equations, feature mappings, listening method, results, limitations and conclusion
- status: `studied_full_text_peer_reviewed_journal`

The design leaves threshold under user control and automates the remaining parameters from side-chain features. Attack/release are derived either from a 200 ms crest-factor estimate or normalized spectral flux; the published maxima are 80 ms and 1 s. Ratio is fixed at infinity and knee width adapts with gain reduction plus spectral-flux side information. Make-up gain is estimated either from average control voltage or a loudness difference. These values and mappings are paper-specific implementation choices, not rock presets.

The evaluation used nine expert mixing engineers and seven experienced amateurs. Each received a VST, instructions and four short isolated tracks: drums, slap bass, soft vocal and acoustic guitar. This was a method-of-adjustment task in each participant's own listening environment: one parameter was tuned while the others were fixed, often to deliberately obvious compression. Static human choices were then compared with grouped or averaged values from adaptive automation. It was not a randomized, loudness-matched A/B preference test of complete compressor chains.

The paper reports that spectral-flux attack/release generally followed participant choices better than the crest-factor alternative; knee automation improved when spectral-flux context was included; and loudness-based make-up gain was closer to participant medians than mean gain reduction, although drums remained about 3 dB high. These are within-paper results, not independent replications.

The authors explicitly limit generalization: uncontrolled listening environments, few subjects and signals, isolated tracks rather than signals fitted into a mix, and a mismatch between static human settings and adaptive automation. Empirical thresholds in the knee formulas were fitted to the same user results. The fixed infinite ratio also makes the topology limiter-like, and the authors themselves suggest tempo dependence and user-controllable meta-parameters as future work.

## Knowledge Cards

### KC-ML-20261007-1200-01 — parameter automation preserves a human intent control

The paper does not remove artistic intent: threshold remains the user's amount-of-compression control. A project controller should keep at least one explicit, bounded intent control and must not infer that signal features alone define the desired density.

Confidence: `high` for the paper design; `low` for universal preference.

### KC-ML-20261007-1200-02 — static preference and adaptive trajectories are different objects

Averaging time-varying attack/release/knee values to compare them with one chosen static value can hide trajectory timing, modulation and section dependence. Validation must inspect event-aligned control paths and audio, not only average parameter agreement.

Confidence: `high` as a measurement limitation.

### KC-ML-20261007-1200-03 — published corrections are part of DSP provenance

Paper title and equation number are not enough for reproducibility. Store publication version, erratum state and reference-code identity; block parity claims if an official correction exists but its exact content has not been verified.

Confidence: `high`.
