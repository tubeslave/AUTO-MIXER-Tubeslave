## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260925-0900-01` — RESTORE

- **Title:** *RESTORE: REal-time Steerable Music resTORation and bandwidth Extension via stem disentanglement*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28683>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28683v1`
- **Publication date:** 2026-09-23
- **Discovery date:** 2026-09-25
- **DOI:** not located
- **Depth:** full HTML text, including architecture, synthetic degradation model, metrics, tables, real-data evaluation and limitations
- **Project page:** <https://melissachen15.github.io/restore-audio-demo/>
- **Artifact:** <https://github.com/MelissaChen15/restore> — linked repository exists but is empty as checked on 2026-09-25; code, checkpoints and runnable configuration are unavailable

RESTORE adapts HTDemucs to return vocals, music, broadband hiss, transient noise, residual and HFE stems. Mixture consistency makes the decomposition additive, and HFE is isolated from the denoising/removal stems. This is a useful control topology, but additivity does not prove that energy has been assigned to the correct semantic stem.

Training degradations are simulated from clean material: randomized piecewise EQ/low-pass bandwidth loss, sampled gramophone noise, clicks and repeating thumps. Distortion and groove echo are omitted after preliminary experiments. The reference test set contains 30 synthetically degraded excerpts. Real historical recordings have no clean target and are evaluated with FAD-like distribution metrics rather than fidelity to an original master.

The paper's HFE ablation is not uniformly favourable: enabling HFE improves one reported CLAP-FAD category but worsens another. The authors explicitly leave listening evaluation for future work. The reported approximately 50-times-real-time throughput is measured on a single A100 and does not establish CPU, plug-in or low-latency deployment.

**Bounded conclusion:** mixture-consistent semantic stems and a separately gainable HFE stem are promising research controls. HFE is not a restoration fact or mastering preset; it must remain opt-in and be tested for hallucinated harmonics, altered attacks, stereo/mono damage and peak growth.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-0900-02` — SSE

- **Title:** *Spot, Separate, and Enhance: Fully Generative Approach for Audio Mixing*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29169>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.29169v1`
- **Publication date:** 2026-09-24
- **Discovery date:** 2026-09-25
- **DOI:** not located
- **Depth:** full HTML text, including data construction, flow-matching model, metrics, ablations, listening comparison and limitations
- **Project page:** <https://sse-ai.notion.site/> — unavailable during this run; no public code, weights or split manifest was verified

SSE conditions a generative audio model on video and text to rebalance or replace speech, music, effects, noise and reverberation in ten-second consumer/movie clips. It is not a conventional music-mixing system and can synthesize replacement content.

DegradedMix is derived from professional movie mixes that are first decomposed into pseudo-stems by a pretrained separator and then degraded with gain changes, noise and reverberation. Text instructions are generated from the clean target by Qwen2-Audio. Consequently, training targets can inherit separator artefacts, and the instruction channel can contain cleaner target knowledge than a real user's ambiguous prompt.

The large model has about three billion parameters and was trained on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs. Objective evaluation relies mainly on embedding/distribution, semantic and audiovisual synchronization metrics. Those metrics do not establish source preservation, phase coherence, transient integrity, true-peak safety or loudness-controlled preference.

The subjective study uses 10 participants and 10 UGC videos with random pairwise order. The paper does not document loudness matching, listener screening or equivalence bounds. Preference supports the tested UGC task but does not establish safe music-remix transfer.

**Bounded conclusion:** SSE is a generative UGC enhancement candidate, not evidence for autonomous music mixing. Any music evaluation must separate conservative enhancement from content generation and include a no-generation/codec baseline, identity residuals and loudness-matched blind preference.

## Knowledge cards

1. Exact additive reconstruction constrains the sum of estimated stems but does not prove semantic correctness of each stem.
2. Generated high-frequency extension should be isolated from denoising and exposed as an opt-in control.
3. Reference-free FAD on historical recordings measures distribution similarity, not archival fidelity or listener preference.
4. RESTORE's HFE condition is not uniformly better across its own reported CLAP-FAD categories.
5. A single-A100 real-time factor is not a CPU, plug-in, memory or low-latency guarantee.
6. A linked but empty repository blocks reproducibility despite a public demo page.
7. SSE addresses generative UGC/movie enhancement rather than multitrack music mixing.
8. Pseudo-stems produced by a separator can transfer leakage and artefacts into both targets and evaluation.
9. Instructions generated from the clean target can be oracle-like relative to real ambiguous user prompts.
10. Embedding and distribution metrics do not protect waveform identity, attacks, phase, loudness or true peak.
11. A small pairwise preference test without documented loudness alignment or listener screening is limited task evidence.
12. Generative replacement and conservative enhancement require separate baselines and acceptance criteria.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-0900-01` — isolated HFE contribution

- **Baseline A:** RESTORE render with HFE stem gain `0`.
- **Variant B:** identical render with HFE stem gain `1`.
- **Only changed factor:** HFE stem gain, matching the paper's off/on ablation endpoints.
- **Controls:** same rights-cleared degraded rock excerpt, checkpoint, separator estimates, gain and render path; retain raw outputs and create separate BS.1770 loudness-matched blind copies.
- **Measure:** above-cutoff spectral error against a held-out clean target, harmonic/onset consistency, sample/true peak, stereo correlation, mono residual, artefact count and blind preference.
- **Protect:** instrument identity, drum attacks, guitar texture, sibilance, stereo image and headroom.
- **Success:** HFE improves perceived bandwidth without hallucinated events, transient loss, mono damage or peak failure.
- **Cancel:** invented harmonics/noise, brittle HF, image instability or loudness advantage explains preference.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-0900-02` — additive reconstruction and semantic leakage

- **Baseline A:** original degraded input.
- **Variant B:** sum of all RESTORE estimates with HFE disabled.
- **Only changed factor:** reconstruction through the separated additive path.
- **Controls:** identical sample rate, length, gain and no downstream processing.
- **Measure:** null residual, sample alignment, gain ratio, finite output, sample/true peak, music bleed into noise stems and mono/stereo parity.
- **Protect:** the complete input signal and timing.
- **Success:** numerical reconstruction is stable and semantic leakage remains below a preregistered audible-review threshold.
- **Cancel:** scale drift, missing samples, non-finite values or wanted musical events appear mainly in removal stems.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-0900-03` — generative-path identity cost

- **Baseline A:** unchanged-input/codec-only reconstruction.
- **Variant B:** SSE generation with a fixed enhancement instruction.
- **Only changed factor:** generative enhancement path.
- **Controls:** same 10-second rights-cleared clip, video, text, seed, codec and output gain; raw safety copies plus loudness-matched blind copies.
- **Measure:** residual identity, onset timing, transient envelope, audiovisual sync, loudness, sample/true peak, mono compatibility and blind preference.
- **Protect:** performance, words, effects timing, music identity and headroom.
- **Success:** intended cleanup improves without material replacement or timing/peak failures.
- **Cancel:** generated content, loudness or codec differences explain preference.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-0900-04` — prompt provenance robustness

- **Baseline A:** caption automatically derived from the clean target.
- **Variant B:** human-written ambiguous prompt expressing the same intent.
- **Only changed factor:** prompt provenance/wording.
- **Controls:** identical degraded input, video, model, checkpoint, seed and gain.
- **Measure:** control compliance, source retention, residual identity, prompt sensitivity, peaks and listener preference.
- **Protect:** performance identity and non-target ambience.
- **Success:** comparable safe behaviour across realistic paraphrases without privileged target knowledge.
- **Cancel:** results collapse or content replacement rises under ordinary human wording.
