## Source Cards

### SRC-ML-20261014-0900-01 — Evaluating Dynamic Range Compressor Models Using Control-Voltage Measurements: An Approach and Dataset

- **Authors:** Benjamin R. Thompson; Michael C. Heilemann
- **Canonical IDs:** `arXiv:2606.18573`; `DAFx26_paper_30`
- **Venue/date:** DAFx26, 1–4 September 2026; arXiv submitted 17 June 2026
- **URLs:** <https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_30.pdf> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18573>
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0
- **Discovered:** 2026-10-14
- **Reading depth:** full eight-page paper
- **Citation check:** `partial`

The work argues that the primary behavioural quantity for a compressor model is its time-varying gain-reduction trajectory. Output-waveform L1 and multi-resolution short-time-energy loss can be dominated or blurred by phase, filtering, noise and capture effects that are not the intended broadband gain control.

The controlled comparison modifies `torchcomp` to use a peak detector and one shared stereo gain signal. Threshold, ratio, attack, release and makeup gain are optimised. Ten independent models per loss regime are fitted for 3,000 steps, each to one 30-second stereo example; direct gain-reduction L1, waveform L1 and MSTE are compared.

Mean direct gain-reduction error was 0.0869 dB for the model trained on the measured control trajectory, versus 0.1493 dB for waveform-L1 training and 0.1615 dB for MSTE training. The worst-to-best spread was 85.9% under direct gain error, while waveform L1 and MSTE spreads were only 2.8% and 13.3%. Under this protocol, waveform proxies compressed differences that the measured control signal exposed.

The paper explicitly does **not** test generalisation: every model is fitted and evaluated on the same example to isolate the training-objective question. There is no listening test, no comparison of final mix preference and no proof that lower control error preserves the hardware’s desirable colour. The simplified model also omits the SSL’s gradual knee and program-dependent AUTO release and restricts training to 10:1 ratio plus fixed release settings.
