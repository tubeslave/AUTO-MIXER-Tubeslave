## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260924-1500-01` — Herglotz echo detection

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28068>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28068v1`
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-23
- **Depth:** full text; method, simulations, measured validation, limitations and conclusion
- **Artifact status:** no public companion code or checkpoint found in targeted search

The method estimates a Herglotz localization function from higher-order spherical-microphone-array data, fits nonnegative spherical Gaussians, and selects the number of echoes with an empirically chosen AIC-like stopping criterion. The large simulation uses equal-amplitude echoes, a minimum `35°` separation and 250 configurations for each echo count from 1 to 12. Those controls are useful for comparison but do not represent arbitrary real reflection density or level range.

Measured validation uses one EM64 configuration in IRCAM's ESPRO hall. Reference directions are not direct measurements: they are computed with an Image Source Method from known geometry. In a 10 ms frame containing direct sound and the first reflection, all four methods detect both arrivals; MUSIC has the best direct-sound angular error, while Herglotz has the best first-reflection and relative-presence errors. In a 42 ms frame with 13 later reflections, Herglotz detects 70%, versus 31% SRP, 62% maxWDI and 38% MUSIC.

**Bounded conclusion:** Herglotz is a promising diagnostic for overlapping early-reflection directions in higher-order SRIRs. It does not yet validate cross-frame tracking, false-positive rejection, time-of-arrival or spectral reconstruction, resynthesis, listening quality, mono safety, or a musical reverb rule.

### `SRC-ML-20260924-1500-02` — Neural FoV binaural matching

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28343>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28343v1`
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-23
- **License:** CC BY 4.0
- **Depth:** full text; architecture, data, metrics, objective results, MUSHRA design and conclusion
- **Artifact status:** no public companion implementation or weights found in targeted search

FoV-BSM-Net predicts a signal-dependent spatial weighting mask for binaural signal matching from a wearable microphone array. The 12,000-scene dataset is simulated, single-source speech. Objective evaluation uses 1,200 test scenes. Its binaural NMSE keeps complex error below 1.5 kHz but magnitude-only error above 1.5 kHz; this is a method-specific evaluation contract, not a universal stereo metric.

The MUSHRA experiment has 12 normal-hearing listeners and only two simulated speech scenes. In the low-DRR scene, adaptive FoV is not significantly different from the reference, but it is also not significantly better than fixed FoV. In the high-DRR scene it is better than fixed FoV yet remains 23.7 points below the reference. The paper itself limits the evidence to simulated single-source conditions and leaves real recordings and multiple sources for future work.

**Bounded conclusion:** adaptive FoV is a research candidate for binaural reproduction from sparse wearable arrays. It is not evidence for pan, width or spatial-processing choices in a conventional rock mix and should not be transferred to music, multiple sources, personalized HRTFs or moving listeners without new tests.

## Knowledge cards

1. Herglotz improves dense-frame direction/presence estimation in the reported SMA conditions, not reverb rendering quality.
2. The measured Herglotz reference directions are ISM-derived, so they are not an independent physical ground truth.
3. Equal-amplitude echoes and a 35° minimum separation bound the large simulation.
4. An empirically chosen AIC-like criterion needs false-positive and alternative-criterion checks before unattended use.
5. Single-frame detection does not solve cross-frame echo association, time-of-arrival or reconstruction.
6. FoV-BSM-Net evidence is simulated, single-source and speech-based.
7. Magnitude-only NMSE above 1.5 kHz is a study contract, not a general music-spatial metric.
8. Twelve listeners and two scenes support a narrow perceptual comparison, not broad music generalization.
9. Failure to differ from the reference at low DRR does not prove equivalence, especially with a small test.
10. The high-DRR adaptive system still differs substantially from the reference despite beating fixed FoV.
11. CLA's unlinked parallel bus is an engineer preference whose stereo and centre behavior must be measured.
12. Spoken compressor/EQ values describe one chain and do not define a portable preset without processor and gain-reduction parity.
