## Sources actually read

### Updated `SRC-ML-20260924-1500-01` — Herglotz echo detection

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28068>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28068v1`
- **Depth this run:** full text re-read, including equations 12–15, stopping logic, metric definitions, tables I–IV, measured validation and limitations
- **Artifact status:** no public implementation was found in the targeted search; the equation/algorithm ambiguity therefore could not be resolved against code

#### Revisions

- The model fits nonnegative spherical Gaussians by minimizing a positive weighted least-squares error `L`.
- The printed selection score is `C = -(ln L + P)`. Reducing `L` increases `-ln L`, while increasing `P` decreases `C`; this is incompatible with the accompanying statement that `C` initially decreases as fit improves and later increases, with termination at `C(i+1) > C(i)`. The echo-count rule is therefore marked `verification_required`, not implementation-ready.
- The criterion is described as AIC-inspired, not as standard AIC, and was selected empirically on preliminary simulations. No coefficient sweep, BIC result or held-out criterion-selection set is reported.
- “Detection rate” is explicitly `number detected / number synthesized`. It is a count ratio, can exceed 100% under over-segmentation, and does not identify true positives or false positives.
- Angular and relative-presence errors are calculated only for correctly matched detected reflections. Misses and extra peaks are omitted from those error summaries, so low conditional error does not imply complete detection.
- The 42 ms ESPRO evaluation uses 13 reference directions computed by a second-order image-source model. Those labels are model-assisted and can omit higher-order, scattered or diffracted arrivals in the measured room.

**Revised bounded conclusion:** the paper supports Herglotz as a promising localization-map candidate under its tested SMA conditions. The published echo-count logic and reported count-ratio metric are insufficient for unattended detection claims until the sign/stopping convention and full TP/FP/FN accounting are independently verified.

### Existing related source `S-ML-20260916-1500-02` — parametric SRIR resynthesis

- **Primary paper:** <https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_08.pdf>
- **Official companion:** <https://dafx26-eac.ircam.fr/>
- **Depth this run:** full eight-page paper re-read, including early-reflection reconstruction, late-field covariance, evaluation, discussion and appendix; official companion structure and notes checked
- **Relationship:** same research group and same Herglotz analysis family; downstream evidence, not independent replication

This DAFx paper corrects one over-broad sentence in the 15:00 card: Herglotz detection has in fact been placed inside an end-to-end SRIR resynthesis chain by the same group. The chain uses 2.6 ms overlapping frames for early analysis, reconstructs detected arrivals as filtered plane waves, models array scattering/aliasing, and synthesizes the late field with DFDNs plus covariance matching.

The evidence remains bounded. Detailed paper results are one EM32 measurement in the IRCAM Espro hall, evaluated with spherical correlation and binaural EDR-difference figures. There is no controlled listening test, released implementation, multi-room statistical table, CPU/latency benchmark or independent reproduction. The paper explicitly notes missed reflections from finite array order, possible adjacent-beam energy overestimation and single-slope late-decay limits. The official companion offers listenable examples across several rooms but no formal randomization, listener sample or inferential analysis.

**Revised bounded conclusion:** objective end-to-end resynthesis evidence exists, but it is same-family and does not repair the echo-count ambiguity or establish perceptual superiority, musical transfer or safe deployment.

### Updated `SRC-ML-20260924-1500-02` — Neural FoV-BSM

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28343>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28343v1`
- **Depth this run:** full text re-read, including loss, split description, baseline selection, direct-cue evaluation and listening protocol
- **Artifact status:** no public code, weights, training manifest or split manifest was found in the targeted search

#### Revisions

- The “avoids explicit source estimation” claim applies to inference architecture, not supervision. The composite training loss contains an explicit ground-truth DOA term, and the result section reports approximately `1.3°` mean center-direction error.
- All 12,000 scenarios use the same three room geometries and LibriSpeech `train-clean-100`. The paper says the scenarios are partitioned 9600/1200/1200 with balanced conditions, but does not state speaker-disjoint, utterance-disjoint or room-geometry-disjoint splits.
- Training directly optimizes DOA, ILD/ITD and NMSE terms that also frame the evaluation. IPD/ICLD and listening provide partial checks, but the main objective metrics are not independent of the training targets.
- The fixed-FoV baseline is chosen by an ad-hoc search on 50 high-DRR scenarios; the paper does not identify whether those scenarios belong to train, validation or test. This is an unreported provenance issue, not proof of leakage.
- The study calls its listening test MUSHRA, but each screen contains a hidden reference and the three algorithms; no dedicated low-quality anchor is described. This is a MUSHRA-like comparison with an incomplete stated anchor protocol.
- “Statistically indistinguishable from the reference” in one low-DRR scene is not an equivalence result.

**Revised bounded conclusion:** FoV-BSM-Net is an explicitly DOA-supervised adaptive renderer evaluated on simulated single-source speech. Its apparent localization-free character and broad perceptual generality should not be overstated.

### New `SRC-ML-20260924-1800-01` — fixed FoV-BSM comparator

- **Title:** *Field-of-View Informed Binaural Signal Matching for Head-Worn Arrays*
- **Author-uploaded full manuscript:** <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/410601217_Field-of-View_Informed_Binaural_Signal_Matching_for_Head-Worn_Arrays>
- **Conference:** AES 2026 International Conference on Audio for Virtual and Augmented Reality and Immersive Games, 30 June–3 July 2026
- **Stable ID:** `normalized_title:field-of-view-informed-binaural-signal-matching-for-head-worn-arrays|aes-avarig-2026`
- **DOI:** not located
- **Depth:** full manuscript; formulation, anechoic parameter study, 200-scenario Monte Carlo evaluation, listening method, statistics and limitations
- **Review status:** the manuscript states that only the abstract and 750-word precis were peer reviewed; the full manuscript was not peer reviewed

This is the closest fixed-FoV primary comparator cited by the neural paper. It uses measured Project Aria array transfer functions and KU100 HRTFs, but all acoustic scenes are simulated at 16 kHz. The Monte Carlo part covers 200 simulated sets: one to three TIMIT speakers plus diffuse babble, four DRR/room conditions, and inside-versus-outside-FoV groupings. The signal-dependent COMPASS-BSM comparator receives ground-truth DOAs and ATFs, making it an oracle best-case baseline rather than deployable parity.

The listening test is stronger than the neural paper’s: 16 participants, four simulated speech/DRR conditions, a hidden HOA reference, BSM as the lower anchor, BS.1770-2 loudness equalization and reported repeated-measures statistics. It nevertheless tests only single simulated talkers inside the target FoV, static binaural playback, no head tracking and no real recording or music. It shares authors, BSM formulation, HRTF/array assumptions and research lineage with FoV-BSM-Net, so it is supporting family evidence, not an independent replication.

**Bounded conclusion:** fixed spatial weighting can reallocate error toward a chosen frontal region in this simulated wearable-array domain. It does not independently validate neural adaptation, multi-source target selection, moving listeners or conventional stereo-mix width/pan choices.

## Revised knowledge cards

1. The printed Herglotz information criterion and stopping direction are mutually inconsistent unless a sign or optimization convention is missing.
2. A source-count ratio is not recall; values above 100% directly demonstrate the need for explicit false-positive accounting.
3. Conditional angular/presence errors over matched detections must be reported alongside misses and extra peaks.
4. ISM-derived directions in a measured room are model-assisted labels, not direct physical ground truth.
5. Same-group downstream resynthesis is useful extension evidence but not an independent replication of the detector.
6. Spherical correlation and EDR fit do not establish perceptual quality, peak safety or musical suitability.
7. FoV-BSM-Net is explicitly DOA-supervised even though it omits a separate source-localization block at inference.
8. Random scenario splitting within three fixed simulated rooms does not establish speaker-, utterance- or room-geometry generalization.
9. Training on NMSE/ILD/ITD makes those metrics partially coupled to the objective; held-out cues and listening must remain separate evidence.
10. A fixed baseline selected on an unreported 50-scenario provenance needs a declared split before fair benchmarking.
11. A MUSHRA label does not replace a described low-quality anchor, screening criteria and predefined contrasts.
12. The fixed and neural FoV papers form one BSM/FoV evidence family and must not be counted as two independent confirmations.

### `EXP-ML-20260924-1800-01` — information-criterion parity gate

- **Baseline A:** literal published `C = -(ln L + P)` with the stated stop when `C(i+1) > C(i)`.
- **Variant B:** a preregistered internally consistent alternative selected before inspection of outcomes, with the same optimizer and maps.
- **Only changed factor:** criterion/sign/stop convention.
- **Controls:** identical synthetic spherical maps, initialization, grid, Gaussian fitter, noise, echo counts, separations and level ranges; log every `L`, `P` and `C`.
- **Measure:** selected count, TP/FP/FN after one-to-one angular matching, precision, recall, F1 and stability across seeds.
- **Protect:** identical localization map and fitting path; no audio or renderer changes.
- **Success:** one unambiguous convention reproduces known counts without systematic under/over-segmentation and is documented well enough for independent replay.
- **Cancel:** the literal rule is algebraically non-monotonic relative to the prose, results depend on unstated clipping/initialization, or no convention is robust across held-out noise/dynamic-range conditions.

### `EXP-ML-20260924-1800-02` — detector-to-resynthesis causal contribution

- **Baseline A:** early-reflection resynthesis from manually reviewed DoA/ToA/count labels.
- **Variant B:** the identical renderer driven by blind Herglotz detections.
- **Only changed factor:** reflection detector/count source.
- **Controls:** same rights-cleared measured SRIRs, split time, spectra, plane-wave renderer, scattering/aliasing model, late DFDN, gain and binaural decoder. Keep raw renders; make separate BS.1770 loudness-matched blind copies.
- **Measure:** TP/FP/FN, onset/ToA error, early-energy ratio, spherical correlation, EDR, sample/true peak, mono residual and blind preference by transient-rich and sustained sources.
- **Protect:** direct-sound identity, drum attacks, room continuity, spatial stability and headroom.
- **Success:** detector-driven resynthesis approaches the manual-label baseline without salient missed/extra reflections or new peak/mono failures.
- **Cancel:** a missed salient reflection, duplicated beam energy, wrong onset or loudness advantage explains the result.
