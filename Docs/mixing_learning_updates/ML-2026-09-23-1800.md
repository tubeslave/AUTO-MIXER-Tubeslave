## Deep-review result

The main source from the 15:00 search, [SRF-SVB](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25610), was re-read against its primary comparator, the peer-reviewed ACL 2022 paper [Learning the Beauty in Songs: Neural Singing Voice Beautifier](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.549/) (DOI `10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.549`), its [official project](https://neuralsvb.github.io/) and the pinned [NeuralSVB repository](https://github.com/MoonInTheRiver/NeuralSVB/commit/7a80bc51806103f09c7448b0daf93f49615ce52f).

The central revision is that NSVB is not independent confirmation of SRF-SVB. SRF-SVB reuses NSVB's 617-pair English PopBuTFy test set and compares against NSVB on it. Both systems also require a target/template pitch source; SRF-SVB additionally requires a same-content professional performance for pitch and DTW timing. The comparison therefore supports a bounded within-benchmark result, not general superiority over conventional vocal editing or unguided automatic tuning.

NSVB's own evidence is stronger than a metadata-only baseline: 60 subjective test samples were rated by at least ten vocal-music majors, and the paper reports MOS/CMOS confidence intervals. It is still limited by synthetic task construction: trained vocalists deliberately produced paired “amateur” and “professional” takes. No ordinary rock-session, stereo, phase, true-peak, consonant, boundary or full-song test is reported.

The public implementation adds important reproducibility limits. The split code assigns the same held-out list to both `valid` and `test`; the published config selects two speaker prefixes but does not provide a separate validation set. Raw-input inference is explicitly `WIP`; only packed-test inference is documented. The English config is public, while an equivalent Chinese training config is absent. Multi-speaker embedding examples are randomly shuffled during binarization without a saved selection manifest, and no explicit `torch.manual_seed` call was found. The linked checkpoint has no repository-recorded checksum or separate weight licence.

GeoAtt remains a second-priority source. An exact-title and method-name GitHub search on 2026-09-23 found no public code repository, consistent with the paper's “upon acceptance” release statement. Its magnitude-only evidence boundary from the 15:00 report is unchanged.

### Added `SRC-ML-20260923-1800-01` — Neural Singing Voice Beautifier

- Stable ID: DOI [`10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.549`](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.549); arXiv `2202.13277`; ACL 2022 long paper.
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete`, including methods, data, tables, ablations and appendices.
- Method: CVAE mel resynthesis at 22.05 kHz; Shape-Aware DTW aligns amateur content to a template pitch curve; latent mapping converts the learned “amateur tone” representation toward the paired “professional tone”.
- Data: PopBuTFy contains 99 Chinese songs/12 singers and 443 English songs/22 singers, recorded in a studio by trained vocalists performing both roles. The paper selects 274 Chinese and 617 English pieces for validation/test and 60 subjective samples.
- Subjective test: each sample was rated by at least ten testers majoring in vocal music; MOS-Q/MOS-V and CMOS were separated.
- Reported evidence: SADTW PAA is 79.45% Chinese and 78.64% English; NSVB F0 RMSE is 6.96/7.29 Hz. English NSVB MOS-Q is `3.84±0.06` versus `3.96±0.11` professional mel reconstruction; MOS-V is `3.63±0.18` versus `3.96±0.18`.
- Limits: constructed amateur/professional roles; target pitch/template dependency; no conventional-edit baseline; no ordinary rock-session, stereo, phase, peak, boundary or full-song evaluation; no inferential significance test reported for most pairwise claims.
- Status: `peer_reviewed_research_candidate`, not a production rule.
