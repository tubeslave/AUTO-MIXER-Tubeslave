## Updated scientific source card

### SRC-ML-20261001-0900-01 — DDM acoustic-instrument resynthesis

- **Stable IDs:** `conference:dafx:2026:DAFx26_paper_34`, `dafx_archive:UNNytCTePoNgsd28yb9K7A`
- **URLs:** https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/UNNytCTePoNgsd28yb9K7A and https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_34.pdf
- **Reading depth:** `full_text_audit`; all eight pages, equations, tables, figures, experimental protocol, statistical results, conclusion and references read; figures on model settings and listening results visually inspected. No code reproduction and no independent scoring of companion audio.
- **Implementation context:** all models used 44.1 kHz; a zero-phase non-causal Hann window of `M=6*T0*fs` with 50% overlap; FFT size `N=24M`; maximum 100 partials; DDM order `Q=3`. SM+/DDM peak-selection values were `-110 dB` absolute, `-80 dB` relative, `7 dB` average peak-to-trough and a Hann-lobe-derived width threshold. These are source-specific resynthesis settings, not mixing parameters.
- **Material:** 39 monophonic VSL/SOL instrument samples, 13 each sustained, percussive and modulated, all forte/fortissimo. This is not a rock-mix corpus.
- **Objective protocol:** median frame-wise RMS log-spectral measure and Itakura-Saito divergence plus whole-waveform signal-to-residual ratio (SRR). SRR is highly phase/time-estimation sensitive; it is not an onset-punch or preference measure.
- **Objective correction:** DDM had the strongest SRR for all listed sounds except marimba, while eaQHM often had lower spectral distance on percussive/modulated sounds. The paper attributes much of the DDM/SM+ versus eaQHM spectral difference to different peak selection; eaQHM also failed to converge for the glockenspiel. Therefore the comparison does not isolate estimator family as the only cause.
- **Listening protocol:** online volunteer MUSHRA-style test; example page and self-adjusted level; 18 sounds (six per technique); randomized page and condition order; hidden reference, degraded anchor and three models. Thirteen completed, eight were excluded under the cited hidden-reference/anchor rule, leaving five. One Celesta hidden-reference rating was removed as an extreme outlier.
- **Statistical correction:** the reported 30 evaluations per model×technique cell equal five retained listeners × six sounds, not 30 independent listeners. The ANOVA degrees of freedom confirm five subjects. The paper reports Shapiro-Wilk `W=0.995, p=0.092`; it says sphericity was checked but gives no statistic or correction. Model×technique interaction was significant (`F(8,32)=10.94, p=2.83e-7`). DDM versus reference remained significant after Holm-Bonferroni (`p=0.0091`).
- **Revised conclusion:** DDM was preferred to eaQHM and SM+ in this small screened test, but “almost perceptually indistinguishable” is descriptive, not a demonstrated equivalence/non-inferiority result. `DDM technique effect p=0.075` is also not proof of technique-invariance.
- **Rights:** the article is CC BY 4.0. That licence does not by itself establish redistribution or ML-use rights for VSL/SOL source samples or companion audio; none was downloaded or added.
- **Limits:** isolated mono acoustic samples, strong dynamics only, no mix bus, no compression/limiting intervention, model and peak-selection differences partly coupled, online self-adjusted listening, five retained listeners, one removed rating, unreported sphericity result, no public raw-rating table or independent replication found.

## Knowledge-card changes

- **KC-ML-20261001-1200-01:** distinguish listener count, stimulus count and repeated ratings; repeated cells do not increase independent `n`.
- **KC-ML-20261001-1200-02:** a significant model×material interaction requires section/role-stratified reporting; an overall main effect is not a universal rule.
- **KC-ML-20261001-1200-03:** “almost indistinguishable” must not be stored as equivalence when the reference comparison is significant and no equivalence margin/test was pre-registered.
- **KC-ML-20261001-1200-04:** shared nominal analysis settings do not remove attribution confounding when peak-selection policies differ or a comparator fails to converge.
- **KC-ML-20261001-1200-05:** article licence and linked-audio rights are separate; do not ingest companion stimuli without an explicit audio licence.

## Limited rule candidate

### RULE-ML-20261001-1200-01 — Repeated-listening evidence gate

- **Sources:** `SRC-ML-20261001-0900-01`, `SRC-ML-20261001-1200-01`
- **When to apply:** any candidate processing rule is supported by repeated ratings across excerpts, sections or takes.
- **What to change:** report unique listeners separately from trials; stratify by section/material when interaction is plausible; use “equivalent/indistinguishable” only with an explicit equivalence design.
- **Setting range:** none; evaluation protocol only.
- **Protect:** transients, groove, cymbal texture, pick definition, bass articulation, lead intelligibility, stereo/mono and true-peak headroom.
- **Verify:** randomized loudness-matched copies, raw safety renders, listener-level summaries and section-level results; retain no-processing/current-approved baseline.
- **Cancel:** cell repetitions are presented as independent listeners, a headline contradicts its reference comparison, or screening/level matching is undocumented.
- **Confidence:** `high_for_reporting_structure`; `low_for_direct_mixing_transfer`.
- **auto_apply:** `false`

`RULE-ML-20261001-0900-01` remains a candidate and is narrowed: SRR is a waveform residual metric, not a direct onset-quality endpoint. Use explicit onset/event measures plus listening rather than relabelling SRR as “transient preservation.”

- **Cancel:** any non-attack setting differs, loudness matching is unverifiable, preference flips with level matching, raw true peak/headroom regresses, pumping or protected-quality damage appears, or repeat choices are inconsistent.
