## Sources actually reviewed

### Update `SRC-ML-20261010-0900-01` — RF-SPC sequence editor

- **Stable keys:** `arxiv:2610.11524v1`; `doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.11524`.
- **Depth:** complete five-page PDF re-read, including equations, data split, baselines, ablations, objective and subjective results.
- **Architecture:** SOME note transcription and RMVPE frame F0 feed a frozen MusicBERT encoder with singing adapters; separate heads estimate edit necessity and one of six non-zero offsets; a soft input-derived tonal prior reranks `0, ±1, ±2, ±3`; NSF-HiFi-GAN reconstructs audio.
- **Training:** MIDI extracted from 16,000 professional recordings (about 1,060 h); 20% of notes receive synthetic errors, 70% independently and 30% in two-to-four-note spans. The real paired set is private and used only for validation/test.
- **Objective result inside this protocol:** input accuracy `73.25%`; proposed model `83.43%`; repair `46.81%`; harm to initially correct notes `3.19%`.
- **Risk trade-off exposed by ablations:** the flat edit head reaches `39.32%` repair / `1.93%` harm; removing tonal reranking reaches `35.76%` repair / `1.77%` harm. The full model's extra repair therefore comes with extra harm. Maximising accuracy is not a sufficient project objective.
- **Tonal prior:** validation peaks at paper-specific `lambda=2.5` and then degrades. This number is not transferable. The prior is estimated from the same possibly detuned vocal and can be unreliable for short, chromatic, modal, borrowed-note or intentionally unstable passages.
- **Subjective test:** 30 vocally trained participants each rated five random 8–12 s excerpts across four methods. Only means are reported; the visible paper gives no confidence intervals, subjective inferential tests, participant demographics, listening environment or sufficient randomisation/blinding detail. These means remain descriptive.
- **Pipeline interpretation:** all subjective methods share SOME transcription and NSF-HiFi-GAN reconstruction. This usefully isolates the symbolic edit map within the paper, but does not compare complete original end-to-end systems and does not prove that resynthesis preserves production-ready vocal detail.
- **Artifact status:** the paper links `https://dongb001.github.io/RF-SPC/`, but the page was not retrievable in this run; no public implementation, checkpoint or split/identity manifest was found. No execution was attempted.

### Comparator update `SRC-ML-20260915-1200-01` — BERT-APC v3

- **Stable key:** `arxiv:2511.20006v3`; `doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.20006`.
- **Depth:** full HTML sections for data, target prediction, correction backend, MOS, ablations, segmentation sensitivity and limitations rechecked.
- **Different pipeline:** BERT-APC has its own audio-conditioned note segmentator, stationary-pitch estimator, context note predictor and TD-PSOLA backend; the new paper instead uses common SOME/RMVPE/NSF-HiFi-GAN components around the compared symbolic editor.
- **Different data/metrics:** BERT-APC reports 12,287 samples / 509.67 h and RPA on its own moderate/high-detuning splits. Its headline `94.95/89.24% RPA` cannot be compared with the new paper's `83.43% note accuracy`.
- **Different listening protocol:** BERT-APC reports 34 listeners × 24 excerpts, separate pitch/expression tasks, 95% intervals and pitch-accuracy inferential tests. The new paper's `3.94` overall mean and BERT-APC's `4.32` pitch-accuracy MOS are different questions and are not comparable scores.
- **Baseline status in the new paper:** the reported BERT-APC row is an independent reimplementation trained on the new authors' corpus, not the original released end-to-end result and not an independent replication of the MusicBERT premise.
