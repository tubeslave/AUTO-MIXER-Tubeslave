## Sources actually read

### SRC-ML-20261013-1200-01 — BERT-APC

- **Title:** *BERT-APC: A Reference-free Framework for Automatic Pitch Correction via Musical Context Inference*
- **Canonical ID:** `arXiv:2511.20006v3`
- **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2511.20006`
- **State/date:** arXiv v3, 2026-04-28; paper states acceptance in IEEE/ACM TASLP
- **URLs:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20006>, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20006>, <https://joshua-1995.github.io/BERT-APC-Demo/>
- **Reading depth:** full 14-page PDF + full public demo page + public demo-repository root, HTML and licence
- **Citation check:** `partial`

The three-stage pipeline segments notes and estimates stationary pitch, predicts intended discrete note pitches with a MusicBERT-derived context model, then applies one uniform note-level shift through TD-PSOLA. Applying the same offset to every frame preserves the relative contour numerically; it does not prove that a renderer preserves timbre, boundaries or naturalness.

Training/evaluation data comprise 12,287 samples / 509.67 h at 22.05 kHz. The paper reports a 9,828/1,229/1,230 sample split and defines low/middle/high detuning by ranked average pitch error. It does not state that singers or songs are disjoint across splits, so identity/song leakage cannot be excluded. The learned detuner is trained from the highly detuned distribution, creating distribution coupling even if the documented train/test split is respected.

On the test subsets, RPA is 94.95%/89.24% for moderate/high detuning versus 89.60%/78.75% for ROSVOT and 85.07%/58.02% for frame rounding. Ablation on high detuning falls to 71.25% without the context-aware predictor, while removal of augmentation or interpolated pitch embedding produces much smaller drops (87.41% and 88.18%). This supports contextual target-note selection under this dataset and metric; it does not validate automatic rock-vocal correction.

The listener test used three amateur Korean singers, 34 listeners and 24 ratings per listener. Pitch-accuracy MOS was 4.32±0.15 for BERT-APC, 3.22±0.18 for Auto-Tune and 3.08±0.18 for Melodyne; expression-preservation means were 3.80±0.17, 3.81±0.17 and 3.85±0.17. The paper reports one-way ANOVA only for pitch accuracy, `F(2,405)=60.46`, `p<.001`, `η²=.23`, with Bonferroni comparisons. Because listener and excerpt are repeated/crossed factors but are not modelled in that analysis, the inferential strength may be overstated. No inferential test is reported for expression preservation; similar means are not equivalence evidence.

Fixed commercial settings and different render paths also prevent treating the MOS result as a clean algorithm-only comparison. Boundary perturbation tests show dependence on segmentation: RPA drops from 94.95/89.24 to 85.85/81.74 at ±160 ms. The authors also warn that atypical musical patterns may degrade context-based inference.

### SRC-ML-20261013-1200-02 — objective/perceptual metric validity

- **Title:** *Toward Expressive Singing Voice Correction: On Perceptual Validity of Evaluation Metrics for Vocal Melody Extraction*
- **Canonical ID:** `arXiv:2010.12196v1`
- **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2010.12196`
- **State/date:** ICASSP 2021 submission; arXiv 2020-10-23
- **URLs:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12196>, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12196>
- **Reading depth:** full five-page paper
- **Citation check:** `partial`

The study built a reference-based correction pipeline on six 30-second MIR-1K amateur clips and professional target performances. It compared eight source-separation/melody-extraction combinations using 115 subjects in 2AFC tests across six criteria, then compared perceptual rankings with OA/RPA/RCA/VR/VFA computed over 1,000 MIR-1K mixtures. A discrete extractor scored best on several standard pitch metrics, while continuous WORLD contours were perceptually preferred; OA/RPA even correlated negatively with pitch/rhythm judgments in this small method-level comparison.

This is independent evidence that thresholded pitch accuracy alone is not a sufficient proxy for natural correction. Limits are substantial: only six source songs in the listening study, method-level correlation over few systems, a reference-based WORLD/vocoder pipeline, older separation models and no dense-rock-mix test. It supports a measurement requirement, not a processor choice.

### RULE-ML-20261013-0900-01 — `vocal_pitch_sparse_edit_shadow_only` (revision)

- **When:** project-owned exposed vocal; role/section declared; one suspected gross note error with a manually sealed target; no intentional scoop, blue note, growl or vibrato target.
