### Method and results actually read

The renderer maps one normalized distance control to five correlated cues: direct level, reverb wet level, high-frequency shelf gain/frequency, pre-delay, and a fixed low-mid compensation peak. The published range maps `d=0..1` to 1–20 m; the implementation table gives exact author-chosen values, including a deliberately hyper-real high-frequency attenuation that exceeds the physical atmospheric-absorption prediction. These values describe the tested renderer and are not general mixing presets.

The signal path applies gain and spectral shaping before a parallel dry/reverberant topology, uses Freeverb for the wet branch, smooths mapped targets, and optionally convolves the direct path with KEMAR HRTFs for headphone externalization. The authors report a remote headphone test with 18 participants (nine audio engineers, four musicians, five non-experts) on one shared three-source music scene. Relative to their uncoupled manual baseline, the coupled renderer reduced mean absolute placement error from 15.3 to 7.3 slider points, increased mean Spearman correlation from 0.56 to 0.86, and increased correct near-to-far ordering from 50% to 89%. All 18 participants preferred it over the baseline on the paper's composite spatial-quality score.

### Limits

- This is a three-page demo paper; the baseline construction, randomization, repeat structure, individual ratings, confidence intervals, and inferential statistics are not reported in the paper.
- `N=18`, one shared scene, remote headphones, and an author-built comparator limit generalization to other arrangements, rooms, loudspeakers, and genres.
- Exact mappings combine physical, psychoacoustic, and explicitly artistic choices; the paper itself calls the HF attenuation hyper-real.
- The optional HRTF stage is direct-path-only and headphone-oriented; it is not a direction-resolved room renderer.
- No independent replication or correction was located. `citation_check:partial`.

### Bounded conclusion

Correlated distance cues are a credible production hypothesis and the small in-study comparison favors the coupled renderer over the authors' manual baseline. The work does not establish that its exact mappings, 1–20 m labels, or preference result transfer to rock arrangements, loudspeaker playback, other reverbs, or other listeners.

## Knowledge Cards added

1. **Distance is a correlated-cue problem.** Reverb amount alone is not equivalent to a coherent distance move; direct level, DRR, spectral tilt, and onset timing can interact.
2. **A macro can be the single experimental factor.** A deterministic distance macro may vary several derived parameters while remaining one preregistered treatment, provided mappings and all other processing are frozen.
3. **Author mappings are implementation facts, not presets.** The paper's table includes deliberately exaggerated production choices and must not be generalized automatically.
4. **Small-scene preference is bounded evidence.** The reported 18/18 preference concerns one author-controlled scene and baseline, not a universal listener or genre rule.
