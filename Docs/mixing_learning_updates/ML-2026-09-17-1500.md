## Fully read source

### `S-ML-20260917-1500-01` — Stability Analysis of Time-Varying Virtual Analog Filters

Read the complete eight-page [DAFx-26 paper](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_28.pdf) and its [official metadata](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/nzadVise8gmeLmPAFmfe2g). Author: Russell McClellan. Published in the DAFx-26 proceedings, 1–4 September 2026; discovered 17 September 2026. The paper is CC BY 4.0.

Supported conclusions:

- A sequence of individually stable frozen LTI filters can still form an unstable switched/time-varying system. Pole checks at isolated parameter values are therefore insufficient for automation, envelope, LFO or abrupt preset changes.
- The paper proves that a common quadratic Lyapunov function for a bounded continuous-time filter family is preserved by the stated trapezoidal discretisation when `g[n]` stays in a bounded positive interval and the remaining state-space matrices are uniformly bounded.
- Arbitrary cutoff modulation is proven BIBO-stable for trapezoidally discretised stable continuous-time minimal filters under the theorem's assumptions. The state-variable filter is also proven stable for bounded positive resonance parameter `R`, and Sallen–Key for time-varying `k < 2`.
- For the paper's normalised **linearised** Moog ladder, fixed resonance with varying cutoff is stable for `k ∈ [0,4)`, while simultaneous arbitrary variation is proven only on `k ∈ [0,2.88]`. An alternating `0 ↔ 2.89` counterexample at constant `g=1.3764` has a two-step eigenvalue magnitude above one and unbounded output.
- For the equal-capacitance **linearised** diode ladder, fixed resonance with varying cutoff is stable for `k ∈ [0,901/49)`, while simultaneous arbitrary variation is proven on `k ∈ [0,8.90]`; alternating `0 ↔ 8.91` at `g=1.8161` supplies an instability counterexample.

Limits and interpretation:

- The numerical `k` limits are normalised proof coordinates for the paper's specified linear state-space models, not mix settings, hardware knob positions or safe ranges for arbitrary implementations.
- The work establishes BIBO stability, not absence of zipper noise, aliasing, coefficient interpolation artefacts, CPU overload or listener preference.
- The counterexamples deliberately use sample-to-sample alternating parameters. The paper explicitly leaves rigorous slow-modulation guarantees in musical contexts to future work.
- Nonlinear saturation, component tolerances and actual pedal/synth circuitry are outside these proofs. Discretisation, parameter mapping, precision and coefficient/input/output bounds must match before a guarantee is transferred.
