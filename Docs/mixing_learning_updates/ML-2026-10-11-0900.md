## What was actually read

### SRC-ML-20261011-0900-01 — Restore, Separate, Restore

- **Stable IDs:** `arxiv:2610.08284v1`; `doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.08284`.
- **Title/authors:** *Restore, Separate, Restore: A Modular Framework for Music Source Restoration* — Tobias Morocutti, Emmanouil Karystinaios, Gerhard Widmer.
- **Publication / discovery:** arXiv v1, 2026-10-06 / discovered 2026-10-11.
- **Reading depth:** complete arXiv HTML paper, including formulation, data, training, metrics, tables and limitations.
- **Evidence class:** fresh preprint; not peer-reviewed in the source read.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08284v1

The paper separates music-source restoration into mixture restoration, eight-stem separation and source-specific stem restoration. The training/evaluation chain uses RawStems, MoisesDB, Slakh2100 and MUSDB25-style extensions for training, MSRBench for validation, and the MSR Challenge 2025 test set. The authors explicitly warn that validation and test are independent collections with a distribution mismatch.

The clean-stem formulation is not an exact inverse of a real production session. It models a sum of stem-level processes and explicitly does **not** model bus-level processing. Therefore the output target must not be described as the original project multitrack of a commercial master.

Stage 1 is the strongest transferable result: with the separator held fixed, mixture restoration before separation moved aggregate test MMSNR from 3.980 to 4.047, Zimtohrli from 0.015 to 0.014 and FAD from 0.300 to 0.293. This is benchmark evidence for a controlled preconditioning ablation, not evidence for an automatic mixing rule.

Stage 3 exposes a selection failure. Five stem restorers passed validation MMSNR, but only vocals, guitars and synthesizers improved MMSNR on test. Across all Stage-3 outputs, Table 2 reports 4.047 to 3.972 MMSNR, while the authors' selected final system is 4.050. For the selected system, aggregate MMSNR rises only slightly while FAD worsens from 0.274 to 0.279 and Zimtohrli is essentially unchanged. The two numbers refer to different policies—unconditional Stage 3 versus validation-selected final experts—and are retained separately.

No subjective listening test was conducted in this work. The cited challenge-level Spearman correlation with MOS came from a separate blind test set and does not replace a project-level listening pass.

## Limited knowledge updates

### KC-ML-20261011-0900-01 — pre-separation restoration is an ablation candidate

On this benchmark, mixture restoration before a fixed separator improved all three reported objective metrics. The evidence is specific to the paper's models, degradation distribution and 44.1 kHz stereo protocol. It supports testing an on/off preconditioner, not enabling one automatically.

### KC-ML-20261011-0900-02 — validation-selected restoration does not guarantee transfer

Per-stem experts chosen by one validation metric did not transfer consistently to the independent test collection; the metrics also disagree. A stage cannot be promoted from one metric or one validation collection.

### KC-ML-20261011-0900-03 — restored stems are not project originals

The paper's target omits bus-level processing and estimates clean-source surrogates from a mastered/degraded observation. Generated stems may be alternate evidence for analysis; they are not replacements for licensed/project-owned raw tracks.

### KC-ML-20261011-0900-04 — availability claims require live artifact verification

The paper advertises code/models, but the linked repository was empty when checked. Reproducibility remains blocked until a versioned source tree, license, weights and checkpoint/config hashes are available.
