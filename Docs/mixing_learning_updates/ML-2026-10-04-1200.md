## Source-card updates

### `SRC-ML-20261004-0900-02` — upgraded to full text

**Modal Structure of Plate Boundaries and Klein Bottle Reverberation** — Jin Woo Lee and Mark Rau, DAFx-26, CC BY 4.0.  
Paper: https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_11.pdf  
Project: https://kleinreverb.github.io/  
Browser demo: https://jnwoo.com/assets/demos/kleinreverb.html

Reading depth: all eight pages, equations, boundary tables, FDTD method, objective results, discussion, conclusion and references; official project page and browser-demo implementation inspected. Audio examples were not downloaded, played or analysed.

Bounded findings:

- The paper derives closed-form modal solutions for Dirichlet/Neumann rectangles, torus, Möbius strip, Klein bottle and real projective plane by quotient-space boundary identification.
- Topology mainly changes which modes are allowed/non-zero, their shapes and receiver-position nulls. Similar eigenfrequency formulas do not imply similar responses; the Klein-bottle identification introduces anisotropic/inharmonic structure.
- The finite-difference check uses a uniform `h=0.01 m` grid, an explicit five-point stencil and `cT=h/sqrt(2)` (about `20.6 us`, nominal Nyquist `24.3 kHz` at `c=343 m/s`). The displayed validation is limited to `0–1 kHz`, and the authors acknowledge numerical-dispersion anisotropy.
- Reported analytic-versus-FDTD mean relative frequency errors are `0.02–0.03%`; reported MAC values are `0.875` Neumann, `0.944` Dirichlet, `0.944` Möbius, `0.834` torus, `0.932` Klein and `0.929` real projective plane. These compare two implementations of the same idealised model and boundary assumptions, not an independent physical measurement.
- The authors note that the `50 Hz` main-lobe width introduced by their Hann-windowed spectra contributes to MAC differences. Therefore MAC cannot be interpreted as a listening-quality or real-plate score.
- The work contains no controlled listener study despite the project-page heading “Listening Study”. It supplies rendered examples only; the text's statement that the real-projective-plane case is “audibly distinguished” is not a formal preference or discrimination result.
- Demo damping is a creative Rayleigh-damping choice; it is not perceptually validated. The model remains an idealised two-dimensional scalar wave equation with constant wave speed and flat quotient spaces, not a measured three-dimensional plate or room.

Evidence confidence: high for the analytic derivations and reported analytic/FDTD agreement within the stated model; low for preferred sound, physical-room transfer or rock-mix utility.

### `SRC-ML-20261004-0900-01` — conclusion narrowed

Related source, read in full: **Non-Iterative Modal Parameter Estimation for Plate Reverbs via Matrix-Pencil-Guided State Space Model Initialization** — Matthias Bittner and Axel Jantsch, DAFx-26 challenge paper.  
URL: https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_challenge_86.pdf  
Stable ID: `dafx:2026:DAFx26_challenge_86`

Reading depth: all four pages, method, synthetic protocol, result table, compute statement, limitations and references. This is the same first-author/method lineage as `DAFx26_paper_03`, so it is a version/extension, not independent confirmation.

- The extension adds a mode-count estimator, low-frequency Matrix Pencil poles, polynomial decay extrapolation and least-squares gain estimation, with no gradient optimisation.
- Its count estimator was fitted on `128` synthetic IRs from the same hyperparameter distribution and evaluated on `17` self-generated synthetic IRs. The official challenge's hidden `16`-item ground truth/result is not published in the paper.
- The proposed method improves total error `1.96 -> 0.78`, frequency error `0.98 -> 0.27`, decay error `0.98 -> 0.77`, count error `3239 -> 335`, but gain error remains nearly unchanged at `0.98 -> 0.97`.
- Thus the strongest support is for mode-count/frequency initialisation. It does not solve gain recovery, prove measured-plate transfer or establish perceptual quality.
- Matrix Pencil processing is reported at about `30 s` per five-second 44.1-kHz IR; the `16` challenge files took about `42 min` on dual Xeon Gold 5118 CPUs. The authors report that a GPU state scan above about `2,000` states exceeded memory. This is research-compute context, not a target-host benchmark.
