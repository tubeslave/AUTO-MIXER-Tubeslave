## Source Card update — `SRC-ML-20261003-1500-01`

### Fast Parametric Matrices for Lossless Feedback Delay Networks

- **Stable ID:** `conference:dafx:2026:DAFx26_paper_04`
- **Author:** Andrea Coppola / Arturia
- **Primary source:** https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_04.pdf
- **Reading depth:** `full_text_methods_equations_benchmarks_applications_limitations_references`
- **Status:** `reviewed_full_text`
- **Evidence type:** peer-reviewed conference paper; author-produced objective plots and companion demonstrations
- **Independence:** no independent replication found; `citation_check:partial`

### Method and results actually read

The method constructs a `2^M × 2^M` orthogonal feedback matrix from `M` rotation/reflection kernels. An in-place iterative Kronecker transform applies it in `O(N log2 N)` time and stores `O(log2 N)` kernel parameters rather than a dense `O(N^2)` matrix. Orthogonality is preserved while kernel angles change.

The implementation benchmark used C++17/MSVC on an Intel Core Ultra 9 185H, 5,000,000 timed iterations after 500,000 warm-up iterations. For a fixed matrix the proposed method was slower at `N=4`, approximately tied at `N=8–16`, and faster at `N=32–64`. When one angle was updated at every sample, it strongly outperformed the two dense baselines because those baselines rebuilt the full matrix. The paper did not benchmark a specialized FWHT or another optimized structured parametric transform.

Creative demonstrations use:

- the outer kernel angle for stereo cross-coupling;
- the inner angle for even/odd network decoupling and selective freeze;
- intermediate-angle modulation to move modes and reduce fixed-frequency buildup.

The modulation examples use `2–15% of π` at `0.1–1.0 Hz`, plus aggressive demonstrations up to `π` and `2 Hz`. These are author demonstration settings, not perceptual thresholds or mix presets.

### Limitations and bounded conclusion

- Instantaneous orthogonality/losslessness does not prove perceptual transparency, absence of modulation artifacts, preferred stereo width or better musical results.
- IACC and pole-distribution histograms describe selected technical properties; they do not establish listener preference.
- The claim of smoother/artifact-free topology transitions was not tested with ABX/MUSHRA or a rock-mix corpus.
- The companion repository contains 18 WAV demonstrations, plots, HTML and the paper, but no implementation code, automated tests or license file. The paper's CC BY 4.0 license does not establish rights for the companion audio.
- Applicable conclusion: the matrix family is a credible DSP design candidate for controlled creative reverb experiments, not a production rule.

## Source Card update — `SRC-ML-20261003-1500-02`

### A Unified Framework for Real-Time Concatenation-Driven Convolution

- **Stable ID:** `conference:dafx:2026:DAFx26_paper_01`
- **Authors:** Niccolo Abate; Brian Hansen
- **Primary source:** https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_01.pdf
- **Reading depth:** `full_text_methods_equations_evaluation_raw_csv_repository_limitations_references`
- **Status:** `reviewed_full_text_and_public_data`
- **Evidence type:** peer-reviewed conference paper; author implementation and objective evaluation
- **Independence:** no independent replication found; `citation_check:partial`

### Method and results actually read

The system analyzes corpus grains with RMS, ZCR, centroid, flatness, spectral slope/intercept, energy ratio, bandwidth and f0; maps them to an `80 × 76` SOM; synthesizes a continuously changing IR; and updates a non-uniform partitioned FFT convolution engine.

The key comparison is:

- baseline: two independent convolution engines whose outputs crossfade, with the incoming engine starting from zero state;
- proposed: Cartesian linear interpolation of the active FFT-domain kernels inside one engine, keeping one convolution history.

Tests used 44.1 kHz, block size 512, head/tail partitions 512/1024 samples, 3–4 second Church/Hall/Spring IRs and four update regimes from about 2 seconds to continuous updates every about 0.16 seconds. On one Apple M4 Pro the paper reports mean CPU under roughly 150 µs per block for the proposed engine during continuous interpolation. Raw CSVs support inspection of the recorded time series, but not end-to-end reproduction.

The public SOM results cover three corpora: 27,665 struck-metal grains, 22,085 IR grains and 18,063 chime grains. Low quantization/topographic errors show that the trained maps preserve the selected normalized descriptors. They do not prove perceptual similarity between neighboring cells.

### Limitations and bounded conclusion

- The CPU result is a mean on one host and one block/partition configuration; no p95/p99/max deadline analysis, xruns, multi-instance test or independent reproduction is reported.
- The baseline is a particular zero-state dual-engine output crossfade. More advanced filter-transition baselines cited by the paper were not benchmarked, so superiority cannot be generalized to all dynamic-convolution designs.
- Cartesian interpolation can reduce energy for near-antiphase bins. Polar interpolation is implemented but explicitly underexplored.
- RMS continuity does not establish inaudibility of timbral, spatial, phase or transient artifacts. The paper explicitly says formal listening tests are future work.
- The public repository contains data and examples but no plugin/source/build scripts. Corpus provenance and licenses are not documented at repository level.
- Applicable conclusion: single-state kernel interpolation is a credible implementation hypothesis for rapid IR updates, requiring a matched implementation and perceptual/real-time validation before adoption.

### `RULE-ML-20261003-1800-01` — dynamic IR transition policy

- **Source:** `conference:dafx:2026:DAFx26_paper_01`.
- **When:** a mix or creative effect requires frequent IR changes rather than a static convolution reverb.
- **Change:** compare one explicitly defined transition topology at a time; single-engine Cartesian kernel interpolation is only a candidate.
- **Do not damage:** transient clarity, tonal continuity, stereo position, mono compatibility, decay continuity and real-time safety.
- **Check:** raw transition envelope/spectrogram/correlation and CPU tails first; then randomized BS.1770-matched listening copies.
- **Cancel:** near-antiphase cancellation, zipper/click artifacts, tone/pan jumps, missed deadlines, or no repeatable preference.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **auto_apply:** false.

## Planned experiment `EXP-ML-20261003-1800-01`

- **Status:** `not_run`
- **Blocked by:** no public CDC implementation or licensed corpus; a small independently implemented offline harness is required before audio testing.
- **Question:** does single-engine Cartesian kernel interpolation outperform the specified zero-state dual-engine output crossfade under continuous IR changes without adding audible artifacts?
- **A:** dual-engine output crossfade matching the paper baseline.
- **B:** one-engine Cartesian FFT-kernel interpolation.
- **Single changing factor:** `ir_transition_topology`.
- **Fixed:** input, two IRs, 44.1/48 kHz test pair, block and partition sizes, update schedule, interpolation duration, dry/wet, render start, pre-roll policy and all downstream processing.
- **Raw measurements:** transition RMS envelope, band-limited energy, spectrogram difference, stereo correlation, sample/true peak, click/zipper detector, median/p95/p99/max callback time and deadline misses.
- **Listening copies:** separately match integrated BS.1770 loudness to within 0.1 LU; randomize A/B; evaluate tonal continuity, spatial stability, attack clarity and preference across drums, piano and one dense rock section.
- **Success:** B reduces transition-energy loss and CPU tail at high update occupancy while retaining or improving blinded preference without new artifacts.
- **Cancel:** advantage disappears with a fair pre-roll/warm-up policy, phase cancellation or timbral jumps increase, real-time deadlines are missed, or rights/provenance cannot be established.
