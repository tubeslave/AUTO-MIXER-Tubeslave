## Scope and novelty check

This run extends the existing spatial, stereo-width and heavy-guitar material with a narrower question: how a lead-guitar role should be detected and placed when it competes with rhythm guitars, vocal or another foreground instrument. Existing cards already separate dry position, width, depth and ambience; reject global width/correlation targets; and require section-aware kick/bass/guitar roles. New material below therefore concentrates on time-varying lead role, level-only baselines, psychoacoustic de-masking and the boundary between binaural research and ordinary stereo mixing.

## Literature and reproducible sources actually read

### SRC-ML-20260913-1800-01 — Webb et al., 2026

- Title: *Effects of HRTF Augmentation on Predicted Spatial Release from Masking in Music*.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28422v1
- Publication date: 2026-08-28. Discovered: 2026-09-13.
- Reading depth: full open HTML.
- What was studied: 2-second mixtures from MusicLarity and MedleyDB, one target plus three maskers, seven binaural rendering conditions, two target geometries, four target categories and 25 individual HRTFs. The analysis used two auditory models rather than human listening.
- Result: stronger, frequency-dependent interaural cues increased model-predicted spatial release from masking; the benefit depended on target location, spectrum and simulated hearing profile. The authors explicitly note that temporal modulation, pitch, harmonic structure, listener adaptation and other higher-level processes are omitted.
- Boundary for Mixing Learning: this supports testing spatial separation as a spectrum- and listener-dependent cue. It does **not** justify an HRTF preset, a pan angle, or a claim that wider/stronger cues universally improve a loudspeaker rock mix.

### SRC-ML-20260913-1800-02 — Ou, Takahashi & Wang, 2025

- Title: *Lead Instrument Detection from Multitrack Music*.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03232v1
- Publication date: 2025-03-05; accepted at ICASSP 2025. Discovered: 2026-09-13.
- Reading depth: full open HTML; linked repository was not available through the connected GitHub reader in this run, so code was not claimed as inspected.
- What was studied: frame-level lead labels in two expert-annotated multitrack datasets (MJN 7.10 h and MedleyDB 5.57 h). Lead changes averaged 23.05 s and 29.33 s in the two datasets. The proposed model compared each track with the mixture and outperformed mixture-only, averaged-track, CRNN and guitar-solo SVM baselines on the reported splits.
- Important limitations: the task assumes one lead at each time step; that annotation convention is not a universal law of arrangement. Datasets are imbalanced toward vocals, MJN contains substantial bleed, audio was normalized and converted to mono, and the reported metrics evaluate detection—not whether a mix decision is musically beneficial.
- Boundary for Mixing Learning: “lead” should be represented as a time-varying role or confidence, not a permanent privilege attached to a track name. Detection output must not directly raise level, brightness or width.

### SRC-ML-20260913-1800-03 — Presti et al., 2024

- Title: *Real-time Psychoacoustic Frequency Masking Compensation for Audio Signals with Overlapping Spectra*.
- DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14361929.
- Publisher PDF: https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/648a665b-e5ca-4aeb-abbb-79d139b8b0b0/SMC2024_paper_id126.pdf
- Publication date: 2024. Discovered: 2026-09-13.
- Reading depth: full six-page open-access paper.
- What was studied: a 32-band Bark-scale masking estimate drives dynamic EQ on the masked source from a side-chain masker. The prototype used empirically chosen smoothing and intervention limits. In a headphone test, 13 participants compared baseline, the processor and a +3 dB level version for distorted-guitar/voice and noise/voice stimuli; three listeners failed the identity check, leaving 10.
- Result: intelligibility medians improved from 2 to 4 for both dynamic de-masking and +3 dB level, with no difference between those two conditions. The processed version was judged closer to the baseline mix level than the +3 dB version. Naturalness did not differ significantly.
- Limitations: very small retained sample, two stimulus pairs, voice always the masked target, no lead guitar target, fixed prototype architecture and empirically tuned parameters. The reported ±12 dB soft limit and 80/250 ms smoothing belong to this prototype and are **not** adopted as recommended mix ranges.
- Boundary for Mixing Learning: a level-only condition is mandatory. Psychoacoustic dynamic EQ is a candidate when level solves audibility but damages balance; it still needs full-mix, section-specific, loudness-matched listening.

### RULE-ML-20260913-1800-01 — Sectional lead-role map

- Source: `SRC-ML-20260913-1800-02`.
- When: a guitar alternates between accompaniment, hook, fill and solo.
- Change: create a phrase/section role map before proposing processing; allow multiple or uncertain foreground roles in the project representation.
- Suggested range: categorical role plus confidence; no automatic dB/EQ values.
- Protect: intended hierarchy, shared hooks, rhythm foundation and transitions.
- Verify: compare the role map with Dmitry’s annotations while listening to the full arrangement.
- Undo when: the model repeatedly treats activity or loudness as musical leadership, or the one-lead assumption hides intentional co-leads.
- Confidence: high for the time-varying representation; medium for automated detection on project material.

### RULE-ML-20260913-1800-02 — Detection is advisory

- Source: `SRC-ML-20260913-1800-02`.
- When: an analysis model labels a track as lead.
- Change: expose the label as evidence only; require a separate decision for level, EQ, pan, width, ambience or automation.
- Suggested range: none.
- Protect: creative intent, vocal/instrument balance, dynamics and section contrast.
- Verify: blind full-mix comparison against no-action and human-role-map baselines.
- Undo when: a validated project-specific policy links a narrowly defined role to a reviewed action.
- Confidence: high.

### RULE-ML-20260913-1800-03 — Level-first de-masking baseline

- Source: `SRC-ML-20260913-1800-03`.
- When: a foreground phrase is audible in solo but masked in the full mix.
- Change: test the smallest section-local level move before spectral compensation; keep no-processing as another candidate.
- Suggested range: sweep by ear around the current balance; the paper’s +3 dB condition is a study baseline, not a project default.
- Protect: perceived mix balance, rhythm-guitar mass, vocal/instrument hierarchy and headroom.
- Verify: loudness-matched full-mix A/B for intelligibility, naturalness and balance similarity.
- Undo when: the level move makes the phrase oversized, collapses section contrast or shifts limiter behaviour.
- Confidence: medium-high.

### RULE-ML-20260913-1800-04 — Bounded side-chain de-masking candidate

- Source: `SRC-ML-20260913-1800-03`.
- When: a level lift restores audibility but causes an unacceptable balance change and separate masker/target tracks exist.
- Change: compare a limited, collision-triggered dynamic-EQ treatment on the masked source with level-only and complementary masker-dip variants.
- Suggested range: start at zero and increase only to the first stable preference; do not transplant the prototype’s ±12 dB or 80/250 ms values.
- Protect: guitar identity, pick attack, vocal naturalness, rhythm body, automation continuity and headroom.
- Verify: section-specific blind A/B with equal final loudness; listen to full mix, target solo, masker solo and bypass transitions.
- Undo when: tone becomes hyped, modulation is audible, silent bands rise, or intelligibility is no better than the simpler level move.
- Confidence: medium.

### RULE-ML-20260913-1800-05 — Spatial unmasking stays playback-specific

- Source: `SRC-ML-20260913-1800-01`.
- When: considering pan, interaural processing or HRTF-derived placement to separate a lead from maskers.
- Change: label the proposed operator and playback domain; test ordinary stereo pan separately from headphone/binaural HRTF processing.
- Suggested range: no source-backed pan angle or HRTF strength for rock mixing.
- Protect: timbre, localization, mono, centre stability, externalization and accessibility across listeners.
- Verify: separate headphone and loudspeaker sessions plus mono fold-down; collect human ratings rather than model score alone.
- Undo when: separation depends on one playback path, colouration appears, or centre/mono translation worsens.
- Confidence: high for the boundary; low-medium for benefit in a conventional rock mix.

### RULE-ML-20260913-1800-06 — Align spatial analysis with target spectrum

- Source: `SRC-ML-20260913-1800-01`.
- When: a spatial cue change is proposed as a clarity intervention.
- Change: inspect whether cue change occurs where the target actually carries useful energy; do not reduce the result to broadband width/correlation.
- Suggested range: reference-relative, frequency- and section-resolved; no global target.
- Protect: lead tone, low-frequency foundation, transient localization and mono.
- Verify: bandwise diagnostics plus matched-loudness listening; test target at multiple sections and positions.
- Undo when: apparent clarity comes only from coloration, level change or a narrower competing source.
- Confidence: medium-high for analysis design; medium for mix benefit.

## Contradictions and boundaries

- A “lead” label can be useful for analysis while still being insufficient for a mix action. The detection paper optimizes classification metrics, not musical preference.
- The psychoacoustic processor boosts masked components of the target; many engineering workflows instead dip the masker. Neither direction is universal, so the experiment includes both.
- HRTF augmentation can increase model-predicted separability while also changing timbre and remaining untested behaviourally. It is not a stronger version of ordinary pan.
- Prior Mixing Learning already warns that brightening a lead can worsen competition. The new material does not overturn that; it adds a role- and baseline-first decision layer.
