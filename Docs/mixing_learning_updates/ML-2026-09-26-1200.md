### `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-01` — MSS Bake-Off, deep-review correction

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06917v3>
- **Published DOI:** `10.1109/WASPAA66052.2025.11230942`
- **Version family:** `arXiv:2507.06917v3` / WASPAA 2025
- **Depth:** full PDF/HTML re-read, released README/CSV audit and endpoint cross-check

Table 2 resolves the endpoints: `SI-SAR` versus `SI-SIR` is `0.246/0.007` for vocals, `0.240/0.017` for drums, `0.116/0.084` for bass, `0.271/0.174` for other and `0.218/0.071` on the displayed average. Thus the artifact-only endpoint is higher for every stem in this pool. Section 4.2's claim that correlation decreases from interference-only to artifact-only is inconsistent with those endpoints; the Figure 3 caption's first clause instead says it decreases from artifact toward interference, but its final clause incorrectly calls interference more predictive. The bounded conclusion is now: artifact error is the stronger published endpoint, while the plotted interior sweep still requires a pinned recomputation.

The quality-control implementation differs from the paper. The paper specifies a hidden-reference/anchor difference greater than 10 points. The Zenodo README says the violation is `reference - anchor < 5`, and the CSV flags match `<5` exactly. Implementing the paper literally as a violation at `≤10` would retain 5,368 rather than 5,433 rows under the permissive policy and 2,281 rather than 2,371 under the strict policy. This sensitivity is small in percentage terms but large enough that “unchanged findings” needs a reproducible correlation rerun, not an assertion.

The 5,889 rows are repeated evaluations from 209 sessions and 50 tracks. The paper averages per-row Kendall values but provides no cluster interval, model/track holdout or per-system conditional result. Its coefficients describe rank agreement within this study design, not independent deployment accuracy.

**Revised conclusion:** use the published endpoint ordering only as dataset-specific evidence. Do not set interference/artifact weights or acceptance thresholds until the exact estimates, metric code, quality-control threshold and participant/track uncertainty are pinned.

### `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-02` — Spacing Out, deep-review correction

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25919>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2607.25919v1`
- **Depth:** full PDF re-read plus public branch/code/demo audit

Bi-SCNet is trained on Binaural-MUSDB with scaling and channel-flip augmentations disabled, whereas the comparator is the stereo SCNet recipe/model. Domain and augmentation policy therefore change together. The observed advantage cannot be attributed solely to binaural training without a same-recipe control.

The 10-second stimuli were chosen from peak-RMS regions and all clips were normalized to `-12 LUFS`. This improves audibility control but removes output-level fidelity from preference judgments and concentrates the test on high-energy passages. Transfer to full songs, quiet tails, measured rooms, moving sources or ordinary stereo-rock mixes remains untested.

The final sample has 26 participants, 96 pairwise comparisons and 132 localization observations with at least three responses per condition. Figure 1 reports `N=96` across instrument or position conditions, but the paper does not clearly state whether the chi-square test uses conditions, majority outcomes or individual responses. Likert comparisons use Mann–Whitney tests, with no described participant/clip repeated-measures model, effect intervals or correction across multiple displayed tests. These results remain useful exploratory evidence, not calibrated population estimates.

**Revised conclusion:** retain the metric failure findings, especially bass zero-lag collapse, but downgrade causal Bi-SCNet superiority and significance claims until domain, augmentation and repeated-measures effects are separated.

### `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-03` — Binaural-MUSDB predecessor, implementation correction

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00155v1>
- **Evidence family:** same authors/dataset/metric lineage as `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-02`
- **Depth:** full text retained; synthesis and evaluation code inspected more deeply

The public synthesis script mono-averages each MUSDB stem, convolves it with one static D1 HRIR, writes the stems, then divides only the summed mixture by its own peak before writing `mixture.wav`. Unless that peak is exactly one, `mixture.wav != sum(stems)`. This breaks the ordinary additive dataset contract and can confound training, source-level metrics and gain interpretation. It also reinforces that this is a synthetic spatial benchmark, not a raw mix reference.

### `SRC-ML-20260926-1200-01` — MERT intrusive metric comparator

- **Title:** *Embedding-Based Intrusive Evaluation Metrics for Musical Source Separation Using MERT Representations*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20270v1>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2604.20270v1`
- **Publication:** presented at DAGA 2026; preprint submitted `2026-04-22`
- **Discovery date:** `2026-09-26`
- **Depth:** full paper, equations, both datasets, correlation tables, limitations and public-code path
- **Independence:** independent authors and metric method; bake-off listening data are reused, so this is not an independent listener replication

The paper computes time-aligned MSE and per-song intrusive FAD on layer-12 `MERT-v1-95M` embeddings. On 800 median/aggregate points from four models × four stems × 50 songs, MERT-MSE Spearman correlations are `0.65` bass, `0.61` drums, `0.73` other and `0.78` vocals; MERT-FAD reports `0.66`, `0.65`, `0.69` and `0.73`. These exceed the recomputed energy baselines under the same pooled protocol.

The gain is meaningful but narrower than “reliable proxy.” The analysis pools model identities and songs, so between-model quality tiers can drive correlation. It reports point coefficients and ordinary p-values without model/song grouped intervals, leave-one-model-out validation or within-model correlations. The second dataset independently supports vocals across discriminative/generative systems, but does not independently replicate the four-stem result.

`FAD_song2song` is not ordinary corpus FAD: the target and estimate frames from one aligned clip act as the two empirical distributions. For five-second audio the paper states 374 samples in 768 embedding dimensions, making covariance rank-deficient by construction. The implementation uses a matrix square root/eigendecomposition with only limited numerical fallback; sample-length and conditioning sensitivity need explicit testing.

**Bounded conclusion:** MERT-MSE/FAD are useful secondary intrusive candidates. Pin the model revision and preprocessing, test within-model and grouped generalization, report numerical stability, and retain waveform/spatial/peak/listening gates.

12. A dataset mixture normalized independently of its stems fails the additive null and can confound separator training.
