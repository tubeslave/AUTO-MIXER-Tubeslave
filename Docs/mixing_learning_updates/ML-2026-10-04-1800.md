## Source-card deep review

### `SRC-ML-20261004-1500-01` — Count-Density Networks

Paper: https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_challenge_87.pdf  
Stable ID: `dafx:2026:DAFx26_challenge_87`

Reading depth this run: preprocessing and absolute-scale contract; count/density target construction; B1/B2 architecture and training; local comparison table; official hidden table and response plots; artifact/license metadata.

Protocol findings:

- The `100`-IR comparison set is separate from the `327,680` training IRs, but both use the same disclosed simulator and default ranges. It is a held-out seed inside one generator distribution, not a physical-plate or model-mismatch test.
- Local versus official hidden totals are close: B1 `0.3180` versus `0.328`; B2 `0.3301` versus `0.337`. This supports matched-distribution repeatability across a participant-controlled 100-IR set and the organiser-controlled 16-IR set.
- It does not create independent scientific confirmation: the participant paper and organiser report share the challenge generator, task, output convention and submissions.
- Model selection is validation-driven, but the paper does not publish exact split seeds, checkpoint hashes, local-comparison IR manifest or confidence intervals. Sixteen hidden IRs are too few to characterise rare failure tails.

Architecture/metric findings:

- B1 is approximately `3.1 M` parameters and explicitly conditions on `log(gx)` plus a global modal-gain scale. B2 is approximately `99.6 M` real-valued parameter equivalents, applies per-IR RMS normalization to complex spectra and directly regresses signed gain.
- Officially B1 is better on total error (`0.328` vs `0.337`), gain (`0.847` vs `0.925`) and reconstructed full-band response (`0.882`, rank 3 vs `2.983`, rank 10). B2 is better on decay (`0.023` vs `0.058`). The published benchmark provides no evidence that B2's extra complexity is worthwhile overall.
- The organiser plot shows B2 about `30 dB` below the target on one representative case while retaining spectral shape. The saturated per-mode metric hides much of that bulk-gain failure. A causal attribution to RMS normalization or missing explicit scale conditioning is plausible but not proven; it is an ablation hypothesis only.
- Matching modes solely by log-frequency and clipping component errors at `1` is useful for set comparison but can reward correct density without unique high-overlap pole recovery. Reconstructed response, bulk gain and resolvable/overlap bands remain mandatory companions.
- Neural inference timings (`1/3 ms`) are A100-only batch-one values; classical timings use a 24-core EPYC system. No CPU/Mac/target-host conclusion follows, and the reported `24/27 h` A100 training is not a project requirement or authorised compute plan.

Bounded conclusion: B1 is the strongest of the two count-density variants on this matched synthetic benchmark. Neither method establishes measured-plate transfer, listener preference, real-time target-host performance or a mixing rule.
