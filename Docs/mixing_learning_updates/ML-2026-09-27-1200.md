## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260927-0900-01` — adaptive room EQ, updated

- **Title:** *A DDSP Framework for Adaptive Room Equalization*
- **Primary:** <https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_48.pdf>
- **Stable ID:** `DAFx-26:paper_48`
- **Depth:** full proceedings PDF reread; methods, target construction, initialization, Table 1, ablation, compute and limitations checked against code

The paper's main Table 1 covers transition times of 1, 15 and 30 seconds and includes no-EQ, Fx-LMS, Fx-FDAF, SGD, Adam, iHAM/Newton variants. The repository's bundled table covers only 1 and 3 seconds and SGD, GHAM-1 and Adam. It is a different experiment, not a public reproduction of Table 1.

The paper describes midpoint initialization for bounded parameters and zero initial EQ gains. The public code instead samples all 21 normalized parameters from a uniform random distribution, which maps shelf and peaking frequencies, Q values and gains across their full configured ranges. Any convergence or safety claim must therefore record the actual initial state and separate algorithm quality from initialization luck.

The paper assumes perfect transfer-function knowledge at time zero. In the public controller, the physical forward path starts from the measured RIR, but the frequency response used for the first optimizer gradient is initialized from the target response. With `use_true_LEM:false`, the first update therefore is not the paper's stated perfect-RIR initialization.

The target builder converts its magnitude design to minimum phase, prepends the initial RIR's peak delay and then divides the waveform by two. That exact `-6.02 dB` amplitude factor is not documented in the paper. A fixed 300-sample delay is added again to the desired reference during metric evaluation, without a published derivation or per-RIR alignment. Negative SI-SDR values around those reported in the paper cannot establish “minimal change” or causal phase preservation without an alignment sweep, raw nulls and a separately specified target.

The paper's informal listening claim has no listener count, protocol, stimuli, loudness matching, statistics or results table. It cannot support perceptual superiority. The oracle-transfer ablation is more useful: it shows that estimator quality materially affects transitions, while remaining instability under oracle conditions indicates the optimizer/controller also needs independent safety checks.

### `SRC-ML-20260927-0900-02` — time-varying all-pass clipping guard, updated

- **Title:** *A Clipping Prevention Method for All-Pass Digital Filters with Time-Varying Coefficients*
- **Primary:** <https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_45.pdf>
- **Stable ID:** `DAFx-26:paper_45`
- **Depth:** full proceedings PDF reread; assumptions, coefficient paths, Direct Form I scope, second-order allocation, music example and runtime table checked

The derived bound assumes an already sample-bounded input and protects the discrete output equation of first- or second-order Direct Form I. It does not prove reconstructed true-peak safety or internal-state safety in another realization. The second-order equal split of available headroom is a sufficient triangle-inequality construction, not an optimum; the authors explicitly leave better allocation open.

The guard continues modifying coefficients after a dangerous command until the applied trajectory catches up. Preventing a sample overload is therefore not perceptual transparency: coefficient error, phase, group delay, transient shape and stereo motion must be evaluated.

The reported C++ throughput—approximately 3.8 ms for first order and 4.9 ms for second order over a ten-second 48-kHz input with `gcc -O3 -ffast-math`—is an offline batch measurement, not worst-case plugin callback latency. The evidence includes a near-full-scale sinusoid and one music excerpt inspected visually, but no controlled listening test or broad corpus.

## Revised conclusions

- **Corrected:** serialization peak normalization invalidates gain/LUFS evidence, but SI-SDR is scale invariant and must not be rejected solely for that reason.
- **Strengthened:** the bundled adaptive-EQ metrics are `provenance_contaminated`; a reused directory plus glob aggregation explains the two-versus-ten-file mismatch.
- **Parity failure:** public defaults do not match the paper's Table 1, initialization, first-step transfer estimate or target/evaluation contract.
- **Causal correction:** minimum-phase target construction plus fixed delay does not justify calling the outcome phase-preserving or interpreting very negative SI-SDR as minimal change.
- **Evidence limit:** undocumented informal listening is not a listening study.
- **Narrowed:** the all-pass guard establishes topology-specific sample safety, not true peak, arbitrary-state safety, optimal second-order allocation or transparent automation.
- **Independence correction:** the predecessor thesis is same-family and abstract-only, not an independent confirmation.

## Knowledge cards

1. Independent peak normalization destroys gain-dependent evidence but not scale-invariant SI-SDR by scaling alone.
2. Raw gain-preserving outputs and loudness-matched listening copies serve different evaluation purposes.
3. Reused result directories plus wildcard aggregation can contaminate experiment cardinality and rankings.
4. A bundled result subset with different conditions is not a reproduction of a paper's main table.
5. Random filter initialization is not equivalent to midpoint parameters with zero gains.
6. A target-response gradient-path initialization is not perfect initial-RIR knowledge.
7. An undocumented target factor of `1/2` is a controller-level gain choice, not neutral serialization.
8. A fixed reference delay needs derivation or an alignment sensitivity sweep for every RIR.
9. Negative SI-SDR cannot by itself prove minimal change or phase preservation.
10. An informal listening statement without protocol is not perceptual evidence.
11. A Direct Form I discrete-sample bound does not generalize to reconstructed true peak or arbitrary internal states.
12. A same-author predecessor abstract establishes lineage, not independent replication.
