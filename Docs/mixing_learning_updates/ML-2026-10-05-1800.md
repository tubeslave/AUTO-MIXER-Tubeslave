### SRC-ML-20261005-1500-02 — All About That Bass Drum?

- stable IDs: DOI 10.1111/nyas.70306; PMCID PMC13390590; PMID 42301156
- full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13390590/
- published: 2026-06-16; Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences 1560(1), e70306
- reading depth: full article — introduction, both methods, stimuli, modelling, results, discussion, limitations and conclusion
- status: studied_full_text_peer_reviewed
- conflicts: authors declare no conflicts
- correction/retraction check: none found in the accessible PMC/PubMed record; citation_check remains partial

Experiment 1 used 20 musically trained participants, 15 acoustic-kick compounds at 0 and ±20/±40 ms P-center-adjusted asynchronies, 100 bpm presentation and 899 retained observations. A linear mixed model found later compound P-centres when the kick lagged by 20 or 40 ms; kick-leading and synchronous comparisons were not significant. Variability modelling required a reduced model after the fuller model failed to converge.

Experiment 2 used 10 male semi-professional or professional musicians, six pairings of fast- and slow-attack sounds, seven conditions from 0 to ±20/±40/±80 ms and 837 retained observations. Fast attacks usually anchored fast-slow compounds, while fast-fast percussion could produce bimodal click placements at larger offsets. Only two trials per stimulus prevented a corresponding variability analysis.

Important limitations: small and expertise-heavy samples, an all-male second sample, fixed artificial pairs rather than full mixes, relative loudness matched by two experimenters by ear, unpublished pilot P-centres for two sounds, coarse offset steps, and no direct preference, groove or mix-quality test. Bimodality is not proof of two heard events because that question was not asked.

Bounded conclusion: acoustic onset is not identical to perceived timing. Attack shape, energy and role must be considered when auditing kick, bass or percussion alignment, but 20/40/80 ms conditions are experimental stimuli, not production settings.

Confidence: moderate for laboratory P-center effects; low for direct rock-mix editing transfer.

### SRC-ML-20261005-1500-03 — Separate-and-Detect

- stable ID: arxiv:2608.01093v1
- full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01093
- accepted-paper listing: https://ismir2026.ismir.net/accepted-papers/
- authors: Wei-Han Hsu, Chih-Cheng Chang, Bo-Yu Chen, Li Su, Yi-Hsuan Yang
- submitted: 2026-08-02; accepted at ISMIR 2026
- reading depth: complete open HTML — method, data, training, metrics, tables, results, ablations and limitations
- status: studied_full_text_conference_preprint
- citation_check: partial

Pipeline: a fixed Demucs front end creates a drum-dominant signal; a five-stem latent-diffusion separator produces kick, snare, toms, hi-hats and cymbals; a fixed onset detector produces symbolic events. The separator was trained on more than 1200 hours of synthetic StemGMD and 2.1 hours of IDMT-SMT-Drums. Audio was mono 16 kHz, with 64-bin mel features and 10 ms hops. Training used one RTX 6000 Ada GPU; the paper reports 305.17M trainable U-Net parameters plus a frozen 128.12M VAE.

The onset auxiliary branch used a training-only focal-loss onset map; the timbre branch predicted seven DrumGAN descriptors. They are discarded at inference. Evaluation used unseen MDB Drums and the official ENST partition. Separation was scored with mel-MSE and VGGish FAD; transcription used one-to-one event matching with a 50 ms tolerance. The 50 ms value is an evaluation tolerance, not an edit range.

Reported overall F1:
- MDB Drums: ADTOF 0.795; LarsNet 0.613; MSG-LD+OB 0.707.
- ENST: ADTOF 0.709; LarsNet 0.493; MSG-LD+OB 0.640.

MSG-LD+OB was stronger than ADTOF for kick and snare, but weaker overall because hi-hat and cymbal results were substantially lower. The paper reports Wilcoxon signed-rank significance for +OB versus LarsNet, not versus ADTOF. In the ablation, +OB had the best mean overall F1, while the best mel-MSE, mean FAD and per-class FAD were split across variants. No single variant dominated all metrics.

Limitations: 16 kHz bandwidth constrains hats and cymbals; diffusion took about 25 seconds per 10-second clip on the stated GPU; broadband event confusion and latent compression remain; there was no formal listener study of stem fidelity, editability or full-mix preference; no independent reproduction was found.

Bounded conclusion: stem generation plus transcription is a promising interpretable pipeline, but event accuracy, perceptual stem quality, class leakage, bandwidth and full-mix preference must be tested separately. The word editable in the task description is a capability claim, not perceptual validation.

Confidence: medium for the reported benchmark comparison; low for production-stem transfer.

## Candidate rules

### RULE-ML-20261005-1800-01 — perceptual timing audit before cross-instrument correction

- source: SRC-ML-20261005-1500-02
- apply when: considering timing edits between rhythmic roles such as kick-bass, kick-hi-hat or snare-percussion within a named section
- change: use waveform onset as a measurement aid, classify the competing attack roles, and audition the unedited event against one event-specific grouped correction in the full mix
- proposed range and origin: no universal milliseconds; values must come from the measured event and creative brief. The source's 20/40/80 ms values are laboratory conditions only.
- protect: intended push/pull, groove, flams, kick-bass pocket, attack character, multi-mic phase, room image and cymbal tails
- verify: raw onset and attack-envelope diagnostics followed by randomized full-mix BS.1770-matched repeated preference
- cancel when: the edit splits perceptual anchors, removes intended feel, changes the multi-mic image or loses preference after matching
- confidence: moderate_psychoacoustic_low_direct_mix_transfer
- auto_apply: false

### RULE-ML-20261005-1800-02 — qualify generated drum stems on separate axes

- source: SRC-ML-20261005-1500-03 and ART-ML-20261005-1800-01
- apply when: evaluating generated or separated drum stems for editing, replacement or transcription
- change: require class-specific onset accuracy, audible stem quality, bandwidth, leakage and full-mix preference; retain unchanged and conventional-separator baselines
