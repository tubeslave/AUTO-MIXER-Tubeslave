### Methods and results actually read

- The authors assembled roughly 1,500 room impulse responses and retained **1,151** after normalized-RMSE screening against the linear, time-invariant modal model. The collection combines measured rooms with commercial/bundled convolution-reverb material; this does not establish redistribution rights for the underlying IR audio.
- Decomposition used a 256-tap FIR for direct/early content and a modal late-reverberation model over 40–12,000 Hz at 44.1 kHz. Strong saturation, fast-attack compression, spectral shaping, spring, and plate responses were excluded because they violate or poorly fit that model class.
- The interface exposes six normalized controls: decay, warmth, brightness, room size, diffusion, and early/late balance. Correlated descriptors were robustly standardized and PCA-rotated before ten per-band IRLS regressions.
- Reported robust R² is stronger for damping (0.747–0.937) than density (0.497–0.717). The generated examples occupy the corpus cloud, but extreme control corners extrapolate beyond the 5th–95th percentile training region.
- The paper reports a real-time MATLAB prototype, but provides no formal listening test. It therefore supports a reproducible parameterization hypothesis, not perceptual superiority over FDNs or a preferred sound.

### Bounded conclusion

Use the work as a design reference for a project-owned synthetic-reverb experiment. Do not treat its six controls as independent causal perceptual dimensions, do not transfer its normalized limits as production presets, and do not claim perceptual improvement without a level-matched listening test.

## Knowledge Cards added

1. **Modal-model domain boundary.** A linear modal late-reverb model should reject or separately handle IRs dominated by saturation, compression, severe spectral shaping, spring, or plate behaviour.
2. **Controls are statistically coupled.** Room-size, decay, diffusion, and early/late descriptors may be correlated; a user-facing slider is not automatically an independent causal dimension.
3. **Band-specific uncertainty.** Density prediction is materially weaker than damping prediction in the reported corpus, especially toward spectral extremes; confidence must remain band- and target-dependent.
4. **No-listener-test boundary.** Corpus placement and regression fit do not establish audible preference, realism, or superiority.
5. **Detector-exposure principle.** EQ/compressor order changes what reaches the compressor detector; the order should be tested as a single controlled factor when low-frequency EQ changes pumping.
6. **Parallel-routing preference is contextual.** Whole-subgroup and per-channel sends are alternative creative controls, not universal rules; the cited presenter calls the whole-subgroup routing specific to this mix.
