## Fully read source update

### `S-ML-20260917-1500-02` — Keyframe Time Stretching via Extrema Sampling

Read the complete eight-page [DAFx-26 paper](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_21.pdf) and inspected the current [Capicola companion repository](https://github.com/heavylight-industries/capicola) at commit [`f2b74150`](https://github.com/heavylight-industries/capicola/commit/f2b7415000ac2f6742d36ebe6210a2350f3a1bb2), including its AGPL-3.0 licence, README, build path and core file layout. Code was inspected, not built, flashed or run.

Supported conclusions:

- The algorithm stores timestamped extrema rather than a uniform waveform, resamples them in time and reconstructs with nonuniform cubic Hermite interpolation. The paper's evaluation used an extrema threshold `ε=0.001` (`−60 dB`), but this is a source-test value, not a recommended preset.
- At unity, the sparse representation itself was not transparent: the ten-item mean was STOI `0.91`, log-attack-time deviation `0.09` and spectral-contrast loss `3.08 dB`. Glockenspiel, singing voice and castanet/violin examples showed larger deviations; a noise-like haze between partials is reported.
- A 1 kHz sine reconstruction produced odd-harmonic THD of `−38.1 dB`; a sine sweep also exposed harmonics and aliasing from the spline reconstruction. These are method diagnostics, not musical-quality scores.
- The listening study used 15 listeners, five items and three stretch ratios, but omitted the hidden reference required for full MUSHRA conformity. WSOLA ranked highest on four of five items. Keyframe scores were `51.1`, `42.5`, `48.8`, `31.1` and `46.8`; the solo singing-voice result was weakest.
- Transient performance depended on ratio: keyframe LAT was worst at `0.5×`, close to WSOLA at `1.2×`, and best among the compared methods at `1.8×`. Compression and expansion must therefore not be pooled into one claim.
- Very sparse regions before a transient can produce long splices and repeated material. A maximum splice duration of a few hundred milliseconds is suggested as mitigation, not validated as a universal value.
- The paper estimates roughly 34 Cortex-M7 cycles per output sample offline and 61 live, versus much larger estimates for WSOLA/PV. These are operation/cycle estimates under stated assumptions, not measured cross-platform end-to-end benchmarks.

Limits and revised strength:

- The test corpus contains ten short clips, the listening panel is small, and no dense full-rock mix or editing workflow was evaluated.
- The method's perceptual rating includes both time-stretch behaviour and lossy sparse reconstruction. A unity sparse-passthrough control is mandatory before attributing colour to stretching.
- Singing voice and spectrally sparse/harmonic material are clear risk cases; preservation of consonants, pitch stability and partial clarity is unproven.
- Capicola is a broader stereo hardware implementation with transient-driven behaviour. Its repository demonstrates an implementation path, not reproduction of every paper result.

### `S-ML-20260917-1500-04` — neural audio under realistic DAW contention

Read the complete eight-page [DAFx-26 paper](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_16.pdf) and inspected the current [neural-audio-bench repository](https://github.com/dharanipathirk/neural-audio-bench) at commit [`72b9330c`](https://github.com/dharanipathirk/neural-audio-bench/commit/72b9330ce1aa276ac274b8388bab67204525169e). The GPL-3.0-or-later repository provides pinned dependencies, seeded model export, configuration schemas, run manifests, smoke/full modes and result schemas. Code was not executed.

Supported conclusions:

- The study compares LSTM, TCN and WaveNet families across small/medium/large sizes and six buffers. Random weights are suitable for timing because the tested networks have no conditional early exits; they do not validate audio quality.
- Isolated tests cover 270 configurations with three repetitions and report through p99.9. Contention tests use Tracktion Engine, Core Audio through BlackHole, a 36-track rock/pop-style session, neural-instance scaling and serial insert depth.
- At 128 samples, RTNeural-XSIMD TCN-L appeared safe in isolation with RTF `0.211`, yet its zero-additional-contention host result reached p99 utilization `158.5%` with 355 xruns. BNNSGraph TCN-L remained below `20%` p99 with zero xruns across that scenario.
- Asynchronous wrappers can make the audio-thread callback look cheap while missing inference deadlines. At 16 TCN-L instances, anira-LibTorch recorded 14,022 inference underruns; its output policy can return zeros when a fresh result is unavailable. Xruns alone are therefore insufficient.
- Backend ranking is architecture- and size-dependent. The paper does not justify a universal “BNNS is best” rule; small/medium LSTM isolated results can favour RTNeural.
- The current repository materially improves reproducibility through pinned LibTorch/ONNX/JUCE/Tracktion/RTNeural/anira versions, fixed-seed exports, `resolved.json`, `run_manifest.json` and documented CSV schemas.

Limits and revised strength:

- Results come from one fanless M3 MacBook Air, mono 48 kHz processing, a BlackHole virtual device, a partly flattened bus graph and approximately 15-second measured runs. Temperature was not logged and memory was not measured.
- ONNX TCN/WaveNet paths were effectively stateless in this implementation, so their timing cannot be treated as a behaviourally equivalent substitute for stateful streaming models.
- A 100-call RTSan check on a minimal BNNS loop is useful evidence, not a formal proof of real-time safety across every configuration.
- The anomalous RTNeural TCN distribution under increasing contention is explicitly unexplained. It should not be converted into a scheduling rule.
- Transfer to the project's M3 Max, physical interface, stereo processing, actual routing and longer sessions requires a new target-host benchmark.

## New knowledge and bounded rule candidates

Added **2 upgraded Source Cards**, **2 Code Cards**, **11 Knowledge Cards**, **8 rule candidates** and **4 experiment plans**. Added Video Source Cards: **0**; Reference Cards: **0**.

All rules are `auto_apply:false`:

1. Separate sparse-representation colour from time-stretch colour with an unchanged baseline and a unity sparse-passthrough control.
2. Treat compression and expansion ratios as separate conditions; do not generalise the favourable `1.8×` transient result to `0.5×` compression.
3. Use keyframe stretching only as a creative candidate on material whose role tolerates haze/repetition; protect vocal intelligibility, harmonic clarity, cymbal texture, attack and groove.
4. Stress-test silence-to-transient boundaries and reject audible repeated segments or overly long splices.
5. Gate neural deployment on target-host p99/p99.9/max callback utilization and xruns, not isolated RTF alone.
6. For asynchronous inference, log worker underruns and any zero/stale-output substitution separately from host xruns.
7. Preserve equivalent streaming state, weights, topology and buffering when comparing inference backends; stateless substitutions are invalid comparisons.
8. Benchmark each architecture, size, buffer, instance count and real routing on the target hardware; do not install a universal backend ranking.
