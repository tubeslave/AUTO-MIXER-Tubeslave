### Actually read

1. Marc Daniel Nelson: searches roughly `800 Hz–1 kHz` for articulation when the specific bass has weight but lacks definition, auditions compressor character, and judges saturation in the full arrangement.
2. Cameron Webb: divides the Motörhead bass role between distorted identity and clean low-frequency weight, checks polarity when combining sources, and notes that heavy distortion already reduces peak movement.
3. Brad Wood: blends the attack/noise/attitude of the original amp with a rounder reamp, uses only light compression, and preserves note-to-note low-end movement when it serves the arrangement.
4. Richard Furch: level-matches processed and bypassed signals, uses targeted EQ for disproportionately heavy notes, and adds mid/high information only when the part needs translation.
5. Bob Marlette: separates DI, sub, amp and distorted functions, checks polarity before judging the blend, omits layers that do not help, and balances kick and bass as complementary identities.

### Bounded conclusion

Bass processing should follow the part's role in the section. A clean/low layer, articulation layer and distorted-character layer are optional functions, not a mandatory topology. Multiple layers require timing/polarity checks before EQ or dynamics judgments. Processor comparisons require output-loudness matching. No-compression and fewer-layer baselines remain valid.

### Limits

- no controlled listener study, randomisation, statistics or independent replication;
- examples are selected excerpts mediated by one publisher;
- `800 Hz–1 kHz`, `60–100 Hz`, named processors and the article's approximate `3 dB` gain-reduction example are source-specific, not transferable presets;
- the article does not supply raw audio, exact full-chain manifests or enough information to reproduce the paid-course sessions;
- the 2025 article *Mixing the Bass: Five Approaches* links the same YouTube ID and is treated as the same content/version family, not independent evidence.

## Knowledge Cards

### `KC-ML-20261012-0900-01` — mix the bass role, not the label

Decide whether the bass supplies weight, rhythmic attack, melodic movement, distortion, width or a changing combination by section before selecting processing. **Confidence:** medium; professional editorial evidence, not controlled research.

### `KC-ML-20261012-0900-02` — layer functions are optional and separable

Clean/low, articulation and distorted-character paths can be made independently controllable, but extra layers are not automatically beneficial. Preserve an unchanged/fewer-layer baseline. **Confidence:** medium.

### `KC-ML-20261012-0900-03` — alignment precedes tone judgment

When DI, amps, reamps or parallel returns are combined, verify sample timing, polarity and mono behaviour before attributing thinness to EQ or compression. Polarity audition is not a substitute for measured time alignment. **Confidence:** medium-high.

### `KC-ML-20261012-0900-04` — loudness and existing saturation confound compression judgment

Match output loudness when comparing processing, and account for peak control already produced by overdrive or limiting. Keep no-compression as a first-class baseline. **Confidence:** medium-high.
