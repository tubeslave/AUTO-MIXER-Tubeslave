### SRC-ML-20261005-1500-01 — Drum Synthesis from Expressive Drum Grids via Neural Audio Codecs

- stable ID: arxiv:2605.10281; DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2605.10281
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10281
- project URL disclosed by the paper: https://github.com/kostantinos-soiledis/midigroove_poc
- authors: Konstantinos Soiledis, Maximos Kaliakatsos-Papakostas, Dimos Makris, Konstantinos Tsamis
- submitted: 2026-05-11
- reading depth: full relevant open HTML — introduction, representation, method, dataset, metrics, results, conclusion and limitations
- status: studied_full_relevant_text; preprint
- data: E-GMD, 444 hours of aligned drum audio/MIDI and 43 kits; four-beat segments
- representation: expressive drum grid retains event timing, velocity and lane identity; beat-aligned cache; BPM and drummer conditioning, with optional kit conditioning
- model/evaluation: non-autoregressive Transformer predicts neural-codec tokens; EnCodec, DAC and X-Codec compared; common-window evaluation; 1,748 windows for the single-kit subset and up to 68,180 for all kits
- metric caveat: onset matching used ±50 ms and reference velocity above 0.30. These are benchmark settings, not recommended edit limits.
- reported result: EnCodec gave the authors' best overall trade-off; DAC had lower sample error but worse FAD/token predictability. Larger models degraded and showed training instability.
- limitations: no human listening study, no formal statistical tests, no external live-multitrack test, no bleed or multi-mic phase-alignment evaluation, and no evidence that objective scores preserve perceived groove in a dense mix
- bounded conclusion: keep approved event timing and velocity explicit in replacement/rendering workflows, then validate transients, tails, multi-mic coherence and listener preference separately
- confidence: medium for test design; low for direct production transfer

## Candidate rule

### RULE-ML-20261005-1500-01 — preserve event expression in drum replacement/rendering

- source: SRC-ML-20261005-1500-01
- apply when: replacing, rendering or humanizing kick/snare/tom events from an approved performance before mixing
- change: preserve each approved event's onset, velocity and lane/role; if timing is edited, move the selected multi-mic drum event as a group rather than aligning one close mic independently
- proposed range: no universal numeric correction. Derive any move from the measured performance and creative brief. The paper's ±50 ms window is evaluation provenance only.
- protect: intentional groove, flams, ghost notes, kick–snare relation, overhead/room phase, cymbal continuity, transient shape and bass pocket
- verify: event-level onset error, transient/tail and envelope diagnostics, within-kit inter-mic lag/polarity/coherence, full-mix randomized loudness-matched preference
- cancel when: groove stiffens, intentional flams or ghost notes are lost, overhead/room image changes, cymbal tails break, transients weaken, or preference disappears after matching
- confidence: medium_preprint_objective_only
- auto_apply: false
