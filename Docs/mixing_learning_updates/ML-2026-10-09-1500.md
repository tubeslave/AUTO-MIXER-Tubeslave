## Literature and artifact Source Cards

### SRC-ML-20261009-1500-01 — perceptual noise masking through music-spectrum adaptation

- **Stable keys:** `doi:10.1109/ICASSP49660.2025.10888409`, `arxiv:2502.17527`.
- **Title:** *Perceptual Noise-Masking with Music through Deep Spectral Envelope Shaping*.
- **Authors:** Clémentine Berger, Roland Badeau, Slim Essid.
- **Publication:** peer-reviewed ICASSP 2025 contribution; arXiv version dated 2025-02-24; discovered 2026-10-09.
- **URLs:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17527 ; https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP49660.2025.10888409 ; https://clementineberger.github.io/DPNMM/
- **Reading depth:** full arXiv HTML, methods/results/limitations plus official companion results and repository source/config/example audit.
- **Task:** predict a time-varying spectral envelope for music so that the music masks environmental noise while controlling deviation from the original music level.
- **Signal path:** 44.1 kHz, STFT 2048 samples with 75% overlap, 26 Bark bands up to about 16.5 kHz; a U-Net/GRU predicts band gains from music/noise PSDs and a Johnston-style masking model; gains are smoothed.
- **Data:** FMA music and DNS Challenge noise; 50 h training, 20 h validation, 10 h test; ten-second mono examples; three headphone response conditions.
- **Evaluation:** objective Noise-to-Mask Ratio and Gain-Level Deviation only; no listener study. The no-power variant can improve masking partly by raising music energy. The 2 dBA and 1 dBA constraints provide a more useful masking/level trade-off than the baseline in the reported tests; the strict 0.5 dBA condition can regress in low frequencies.
- **Bounded conclusion:** the study supports an explicit trade-off between ambient-noise masking and preservation of playback level/spectral identity under its simulated headphone conditions. It does not establish a universal mixing EQ curve, stem-demasking rule, or perceived-quality improvement.
- **Independence:** paper, website and repository are the same-author primary family, not independent confirmation.
- **Limitations:** simulated headphone/noise mixtures; mono; objective metrics only; no musical preference test; environment/SNR/headphone response are causal context; citation follow-up incomplete.

## Knowledge Cards

### KC-ML-20261009-1500-01 — playback masking is not multitrack de-masking

Changing a final playback spectrum to mask external noise and separating competing stems inside a mix are different causal tasks. DPNMM must not be used as evidence for automatic cross-track EQ. Confidence: `high_task_boundary`.

### KC-ML-20261009-1500-02 — objective masking metrics do not establish musical preference

NMR and GLD quantify the paper's masking/level objectives. Without a listener study they do not prove improved mix quality, reduced fatigue, preserved intent or preference. Confidence: `high_method_limit`.

### KC-ML-20261009-1500-03 — power preservation is an explicit trade-off

Relaxed level constraints can improve masking partly through added energy; a stricter level bound can reduce gains and may regress low-frequency masking. A candidate must therefore report both masking and actual-level deviation. Confidence: `high_full_text`.

### KC-ML-20261009-1500-04 — playback context is part of the model input

Ambient-noise spectrum/SNR and headphone passive response materially change the task. Results cannot be transferred while omitting those fields. Confidence: `high_full_text`.

### KC-ML-20261009-1500-05 — aggressive bass can depend more on attack than distortion amount

The Scheps demonstration supports a bounded engineering hypothesis: in a dense rock arrangement, a clearer DI layer can expose pick attack while distorted amps/guitars carry density. This is a style/session observation, not a universal rule. Confidence: `medium_engineer_demonstration`.

## Candidate Rules

### RULE-ML-20261009-1500-01 — environment-conditioned playback adaptation only

- **Apply when:** an offline, non-production study has an approved final mix, a measured/approved ambient-noise sample, a declared headphone response and a fixed listening level.
- **Change:** one frozen, smoothed spectral envelope on the final playback signal.
- **Do not apply to:** multitrack balancing, automatic stem EQ, production defaults, stereo operation without a declared stereo contract, or commercial redistribution without licence clearance.
- **Range provenance:** no transferable numeric gain range is adopted; any band cap must come from a pinned, inspected implementation and be predeclared.
- **Protect:** tonal identity, bass audibility, transients, stereo width/direction, chosen listening level and true-peak headroom.
- **Verify:** NMR, GLD, integrated/short-term loudness, true peak, band deltas, crest, raw-level and BS.1770-matched listening, and blinded preference/intelligibility judgement.
- **Cancel:** benefit occurs only by getting louder; low-frequency collapse, harshness, width loss, identity regression, unpinned code/weights, or unresolved rights.
- **Confidence:** `medium_playback_research / low_mix_transfer`.
- **auto_apply:** `false`.

### RULE-ML-20261009-1500-02 — articulation-first bass hypothesis

- **Apply when:** a dense rock section has adequate low-end weight but bass-note attack is obscured, and an aligned DI exists.
- **Change:** audition one fixed DI contribution before adding more distortion or broad EQ.
- **Do not assume:** the amps are phase-aligned, the bus should be mono, or the transcript's 60/700/1000 Hz values fit another recording.
- **Protect:** kick/bass separation, low-end weight, note envelope, guitar aggression, mono compatibility, headroom and the intended section hierarchy.
- **Verify:** polarity/lag screen first; raw and BS.1770-matched A/B; short-term loudness, true peak, crest, low-band energy, and Dmitry's blinded articulation/weight preference.
- **Cancel:** thinner low end, comb filtering, click dominance, loss of body, or preference only at the louder level.
- **Confidence:** `medium_engineer_demonstration`.
- **auto_apply:** `false`.
