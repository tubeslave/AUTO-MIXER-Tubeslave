## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260927-1500-01` — Differentiable RIR fitting (`DAFx-26:paper_10`)

- **Primary source:** https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_10.pdf
- **Depth this run:** full PDF re-read: topology, configurations, initialisation, nine-term loss, training, dataset, baselines, ablations, costs and limitations.
- **Paper contract:** the safer `FDN (fixed)` configuration freezes co-prime delays, a normalised Hadamard matrix and ER taps; filters and gains remain trainable. `FDN (fixed delay, train mix)` is a distinct row.
- **Initialisation contract:** 16 delays are listed explicitly from 487 to 3361 samples (about 10–70 ms at 48 kHz); attenuation shelves start at 100 Hz and 12 kHz; the trainable bandpass starts at 100 Hz and 18 kHz; the tone PEQ is inside Branch A before the branch sum.
- **Loss attribution:** the noise-shaped baseline improves from T30 MAE `1.71` to `0.31 s`, MRSTFT `684` to `80`, and EDR `13.3` to `7.8 dB` when moved to the composite loss. Architecture-only attribution is therefore unsupported.
- **Selection boundary:** the nine loss weights were selected by isolated sweeps on one held-out RIR, but the paper does not identify that RIR or report a multi-room weight-sensitivity analysis.
- **Generalisation boundary:** each RIR is fitted independently from one impulse/target pair; the nine test RIRs were manually selected for low noise and no truncation. There is no formal listening test.

### `SRC-ML-20260927-1500-02` — POLAR (`DAFx-26:paper_13`)

- **Primary source:** https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_13.pdf
- **Accepted manuscript:** https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/conferenceProceeding/Perceptual-Optimisation-of-Loudspeaker-Based-Reproduction/991143996602346
- **Depth this run:** full manuscript re-read: all four tasks, simulation, optimisation/evaluation positions, loss weights, results and limitations.
- **Evaluation boundary:** the multi-attribute experiment optimises 30 virtual listeners but reports only three excluded positions. At low frequencies the authors expect some spatial overlap with nearby optimised points.
- **Circularity boundary:** localisation, uncertainty and coloration are evaluated with the same model family used in optimisation. The authors explicitly call this circular and reserve perceptual confirmation for future formal listening tests.
- **Weight boundary:** single/combined loss weights (`1e3`; `1e-1`; `1e-2`; combined `2e2/1e-2/5e-2`) were chosen by trial and error, not established as universal perceptual ratios.
- **Programme boundary:** the experiments optimise FIR filters in ideal free field for a single virtual source. They do not test rock programme dynamics, measured-room reflections, source competition, head motion, true peak or renderer headroom.

### Paper/code parity findings

1. **Fixed baseline is not exposed exactly.** `ReverbConfig.fixed()` only sets `train_delays=False`; the default remains `mixing_type="orthogonal"` and `train_mixing=True`. ER positions/gains are always trainable in the public high-level path. Thus `fit.py --fixed-delays` maps to the paper's `fixed delay, train mix`, not `FDN (fixed)`.
2. **Delay initialisation differs.** Public `delay_range_scale=0.6` scales the nominal 10–70 ms range to 6–42 ms instead of the paper's listed 487–3361 samples.
3. **Attenuation initialisation differs.** Public shared attenuation PEQ initialises shelves at 50 Hz and 16 kHz; the paper specifies 100 Hz and 12 kHz. The separate public tone PEQ does use 100 Hz and 12 kHz.
4. **Bandpass initialisation differs.** Public code constructs 20 Hz / 20 kHz, while the paper specifies 100 Hz / 18 kHz.
5. **Tone topology differs.** The paper places tone PEQ inside Branch A before the sum. Public code applies `(Branch A + Branch B) -> tone -> bandpass`, so it also colours the direct/early-reflection branch.
6. **Validation is not held-out generalisation.** The Lightning data module uses the identical impulse/target pair for train and validation. Validation is clean while training may receive matched noise, but early stopping still monitors the fitted RIR itself.
7. **Reproduction checks are partial.** `EXPECTED.md` covers one studio target and selected metrics, not Table 1 across nine rooms/configurations. `smoke_test.py` explicitly checks only finite output, gradient flow and loss decrease on a synthetic target.
8. **Saved output loses absolute gain.** Target, initial and optimised WAVs are independently peak-normalised. This is acceptable for some shape/decay comparisons but cannot validate absolute gain or headroom.
9. **Environment is not fully locked.** The repository has two commits; most dependencies are lower-bounded, while `pyfar` and `pyrato` are pinned. Coloured training noise and GPU nondeterminism prevent bit-exact reproduction.

### Corrected conclusion

The public artifact is useful and inspectable, but its high-level defaults are not an exact paper-configuration manifest. Before reproducing Table 1 or the metallicity A/B, pin every trainable parameter, exact delay vector, filter initialisation, tone topology, noise policy, seed/device and saved-output scaling. Do not label same-RIR clean validation as held-out validation.

### `EXP-ML-20260927-1800-01` — delay-range parity A/B (next)

- **Baseline A:** exact paper delay vector, 487–3361 samples (10–70 ms at 48 kHz).
- **Variant B:** public default scaled vector (6–42 ms); delay initialisation is the sole factor.
- **Controls:** fixed normalised Hadamard matrix, frozen ER taps, identical PEQ/bandpass/tone topology, target RIR, loss, seed, epochs and gain.
- **Measure:** delay GCD/ratios, modal and spectral peaks, EDP/EDR/T30, late-field smoothness, raw sample/true peak, seed spread and blind preference.
- **Loudness:** raw renders for metrics/safety; separate BS.1770-matched randomised copies for listening.
- **Protect:** decay, non-metallic timbre, mixing time, attacks and headroom.
- **Success:** one range improves preregistered decay/reflection-density endpoints without ringing or preference loss across rooms.
- **Cancel:** the exact fixed-Hadamard/fixed-ER configuration cannot be instantiated and verified by manifest.

### `EXP-ML-20260927-1800-02` — tone-filter placement A/B

- **Baseline A:** tone PEQ inside Branch A before the branch sum, matching the paper.
- **Variant B:** identical tone PEQ after the branch sum, matching public code; placement is the sole factor.
- **Controls:** identical coefficients, RIR, delay/matrix/ER state, loss, gain and renderer.
- **Measure:** direct/ER null residual, early-energy ratios, spectrum, EDR, peaks and blind timbre preference.
- **Protect:** direct sound, early reflections, late decay, transients and headroom.
- **Cancel:** coefficients or any other topology differ.

### `EXP-ML-20260927-1800-03` — fixed-Hadamard versus train-mix A/B

- **Baseline A:** fixed delays, fixed normalised Hadamard matrix and fixed ER taps.
- **Variant B:** identical model with only the orthogonal mixing matrix trainable.
- **Controls:** exact delay vector, filters, ER taps, loss, seed set, epochs, target and output scaling.
- **Measure:** paper metrics, matrix orthogonality/colouration, modal peaks, raw peak/headroom, seed spread and blind preference.
- **Protect:** decay/coloration separation, late-field smoothness and headroom.
- **Cancel:** ER or delay state changes, or matrix state is not logged.

### `EXP-ML-20260927-1800-04` — POLAR independent-endpoint check

- **Baseline A:** rank fixed filters with the same perceptual models used in optimisation.
- **Variant B:** rank the identical filters with an independent listener/model endpoint; evaluator is the sole factor.
- **Controls:** identical filters, programme, geometry, level, source positions and room transfer functions.
- **Measure:** localisation, uncertainty, coloration, IPD, preference, rank correlation/disagreement and true peak.
- **Protect:** evaluator independence, timbre, transients and headroom.
- **Cancel:** no independent endpoint or measured-room programme is available.
