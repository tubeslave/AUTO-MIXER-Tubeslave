## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-01` — Musical Source Separation Bake-Off

- **Title:** *Musical Source Separation Bake-Off: Comparing Objective Metrics with Human Perception*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06917v3>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2507.06917v3`
- **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2507.06917`
- **Publication/version dates:** v1 `2025-07-09`; v3 `2025-09-30`; WASPAA 2025
- **Discovery date:** `2026-09-26`
- **Depth:** full HTML v3, including listener protocol, quality controls, metric equations, tables, limitations and public-data link

The study evaluates 10-second excerpts from all 50 MUSDB18 test tracks. It collected 5,889 stem-rating rows through seven webMUSHRA batches from non-expert listeners using their own headphones. Four per-rating quality checks were used; ratings with at most two violations were retained, dropping about 8%. This is a useful large listening dataset, but the 5,889 rows are repeated observations from 209 sessions and 50 tracks, not 5,889 independent listeners.

Metric alignment is source-dependent. Traditional SDR had the highest reported vocal Kendall `τ=0.316`; SI-SAR had the strongest reported average among the scale-dependent/invariant panel (`τ=0.218`) and exceeded SDR for drums in that table. CLAP-LAION-music FAD reached `τ=0.253` for drums, `0.188` for bass and `0.000` for vocals. These are correlations for this model, excerpt and listener pool—not universal acceptance thresholds. FAD is also a distributional embedding measure and does not replace per-example waveform, level, phase, transient or listener checks.

The IRM1 oracle mask did not perceptually outperform the strongest learned systems, illustrating that an oracle mask is not necessarily a perceptual upper bound. Conversely, no objective metric achieved strong enough agreement to stand alone.

There is an internal direction conflict in the current v3. The Figure 3 caption and Section 4.2 say Kendall's tau decreases as the sweep moves from interference-only to artifact-only and explicitly interpret this as interference error being more predictive. The abstract/conclusion say artifact errors are more predictive. The equation also uses a weight whose direction must be mapped carefully. The error-weight result is therefore recorded as `verification_required`, not as evidence to prefer one error term.

### `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-02` — Spacing Out

- **Title:** *Spacing Out: On the Reliability of Binaural Music Source Separation Metrics*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25919>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2607.25919v1`
- **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2607.25919`
- **Publication date:** `2026-07-28`; accepted for ISMIR 2026
- **Discovery date:** `2026-09-26`
- **Depth:** full PDF, including model training, listening protocol, metric definitions, position/instrument analyses, GCC-PHAT variants, noise stress test and limitations

The authors retrained small SCNet on Binaural-MUSDB (`Bi-SCNet`) and compared it with the stereo-trained counterpart. Binaural training preserved the MUSDB split, held out ten training tracks for validation, disabled scale/channel-flip augmentations, and used 44.1-kHz 11-second segments. The public report does not turn this into an automatic mixing result; it is a separator/metric study on synthesized binaural scenes.

The perceptual study recruited 30 people and retained 26 after screening. It yielded 96 pairwise comparisons and 132 localization observations, with at least three responses per condition. Ten-second excerpts were chosen from peak-RMS regions, normalized to `-12 LUFS`, and covered bass, drums and vocals at left, center and right positions. Bi-SCNet was preferred more often overall, but not significantly for centered sources. `ΔILD` and SRR agreed with pairwise judgments more often than `ΔITD` and SSR, yet the authors emphasize that level/residual metrics do not directly certify spatial-position preservation.

The strongest implementation warning concerns GCC-PHAT `ΔITD`. For many estimated bass stems it returned `0 µs` regardless of azimuth. In the synthetic noise stress test the bass calculation failed around `45 dB` SNR, while vocals and drums were more stable. Setting PHAT-β to `0` improved robustness on estimated bass but reduced agreement with the HRIR-derived ground truth; β=`1` was most accurate on the clean HRIR reference and least robust to artifacts. Masking low-energy frequency bins showed the same trade-off. No fixed β or mask threshold should be reused as a production setting.

### `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-03` — same-family predecessor

- **Title:** *Do Music Source Separation Models Preserve Spatial Information in Binaural Audio?*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00155v1>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2507.00155v1`
- **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2507.00155`
- **Publication date:** `2025-06-30`; ISMIR 2025
- **Discovery date:** `2026-09-26`
- **Depth:** full HTML, including dataset synthesis, metrics, models, results, limitations and companion-artifact links
- **Independence:** same lead author, dataset and metric lineage as `SRC-ML-20260926-0900-02`; foundational context, not independent confirmation

Binaural-MUSDB is synthesized from MUSDB18-HQ by averaging each original stereo stem to mono and convolving it with a single SADIE II subject's HRIR at one static azimuth per source. Angles use 10° increments from `-90°` to `+90°`, with one random non-overlapping placement set per song. It is therefore a controlled, single-HRTF, static-horizontal-plane benchmark—not a measured live-rock multitrack or general room/spatial reference.

The predecessor had only preliminary listening by the authors and used objective `ΔITD`, `ΔILD`, SSR and SRR. The 2026 perceptual paper directly revises the confidence that can be placed in those metrics, particularly `ΔITD`. The two papers form one evolving evidence family and must not be counted as two independent confirmations.

## Revised conclusions

- **Corrected:** use no single objective metric as a separator acceptance gate; choose a panel by stem and expected failure mode, then retain blinded listening.
- **Verification required:** the bake-off's reweighted interference/artifact conclusion is internally inconsistent and must be recomputed from a pinned implementation before use.
- **Narrowed:** CLAP-FAD showed modest alignment for drums/bass in one separation pool and none for vocals; it is not a general fidelity score.
- **Narrowed:** `ΔILD` and SRR can be useful partial diagnostics but mainly expose level balance and residual error, not localization fidelity.
- **Corrected:** GCC-PHAT `ΔITD` is unsuitable as a sole bass/spatial gate under separation artifacts; robustification trades away reference accuracy.
- **Independence:** the 2025 and 2026 binaural studies are one author/dataset/method family, not independent replications.

## Knowledge cards

1. Separation-metric validity is stem- and failure-mode-specific; a global average can conceal vocal, bass or drum reversals.
2. Traditional SDR can outperform scale-invariant metrics when gain and spatial fidelity are perceptually relevant, as in this vocal test pool.
3. SI-SAR can better follow instrument-stem judgments, but its reported advantage is not a universal production threshold.
4. CLAP-FAD is distributional and reached only modest instrument-stem rank agreement while giving zero positive vocal correlation here.
5. Metric weights selected or interpreted from an internally contradictory sweep remain `verification_required`.
6. Repeated rating rows must be analysed with participant- and track-level dependence preserved, not as independent listeners.
7. An oracle ratio mask is not necessarily a perceptual upper bound when filtering artefacts dominate preference.
8. Binaural metric agreement changes with source position; centered sources can be perceptually harder to distinguish.
9. Full-whitening GCC-PHAT can collapse narrow-band bass ITD to the centre under low-energy artifacts.
10. Reducing PHAT whitening improves robustness but can reduce accuracy against HRIR ground truth.
11. `ΔILD` and SRR agreement can reflect level/residual differences without proving spatial-position preservation.
12. Single-HRTF static synthetic scenes do not validate measured rooms, moving sources, loudspeakers or ordinary stereo-rock mix controls.

### `EXP-ML-20260926-0900-01` — stem-specific separator acceptance

- **Baseline A:** a pinned rights-cleared reference stem and output from separator A.
- **Variant B:** output from separator B; separator is the only changed factor.
- **Controls:** identical mixture, stems, excerpt, model output gain policy and sample alignment. Preserve raw outputs for gain/phase/peak checks; create separate BS.1770-matched, randomized blind copies for preference.

- **Protect:** identity, words, bass pitch/body, drum attacks, ambience and headroom.
- **Success:** B improves the preregistered stem panel beyond uncertainty and is not rejected in blind listening.
- **Cancel:** an aggregate gain hides a stem reversal, loudness drives preference, or FAD conflicts with waveform/listener evidence.

### `EXP-ML-20260926-0900-03` — PHAT robustness versus accuracy

- **Baseline A:** GCC-PHAT-β with `β=1`, the paper's fully whitened endpoint.
- **Variant B:** `β=0`, ordinary GCC; only β changes.
- **Controls:** identical clean HRIR, bass signal, azimuth, sample rate, window, lag range and a preregistered uncorrelated-noise sweep. Audio is unchanged for any listening copy; loudness matching is not applicable to the metric-only comparison.
- **Measure:** ITD error against HRIR truth, zero-lag collapse rate, variance by SNR/azimuth and continuity across frames.
- **Protect:** accurate off-centre localization rather than merely stable numbers.
- **Success:** a preregistered operating region improves robustness without unacceptable truth drift.
- **Cancel:** the apparent gain is only centering, or accuracy and robustness cannot be jointly bounded.
