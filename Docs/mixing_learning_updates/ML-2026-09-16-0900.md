## What is genuinely new

The shared base was checked through `ML-2026-09-15-1800`; previously covered reference-matching, stereo, grouping, compression and phase findings were not repeated. Two older but still significant DAFx papers were absent and were therefore added with publication date separated from discovery date.

The central new distinction is: **isolated-track tonal normalization, spectral overlap and psychoacoustic masking are not interchangeable**. A system may make one guitar resemble an average guitar spectrum without proving that the guitar occupies the correct role beside bass, vocal and drums. Likewise, a coarse overlap score can flag similar energy while ignoring level, timing, section, stereo position and audibility.

## Sources actually read

### Automatic Equalization for Individual Instrument Tracks Using Convolutional Neural Networks

Full eight-page DAFx-24 paper read: method, data, differentiable EQ, objective evaluation, listening test and limitations. [DAFx paper](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2024/papers/DAFx24_paper_27.pdf) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16691)

Bounded findings:

- The system first classifies the isolated source, then compares its time-averaged spectrum with an instrument-class target and predicts a static four-band parametric EQ: low shelf, two peaking bands and high shelf.
- The representation uses a 2048-sample Hann window, 50% overlap and 256 logarithmic frequency bins. These are analysis/model choices, not project defaults.
- The classifier covers 35 instrument classes and reports 79% test accuracy. Wrong or ambiguous classification can therefore select an unsuitable target before EQ begins.
- The best two-stage CNN reports 1.02 dB mean spectral error versus 1.35 dB for the cited baseline. This measures target reconstruction, not better masking control in a mix.
- In a blind 35-trial test with 26 internal listeners, all isolated samples were normalized to −23 LUFS and the automatic EQ was preferred roughly two-to-one, with about 14% undecided.
- The listening question referred to likely mix fit, but listeners heard isolated tracks. No full arrangement, section role, dynamic EQ or rock-specific masking test was performed.
- The paper's random-EQ and parameter bounds belong to model training. They are not accepted EQ ranges for Automixer.

### Improved Control for Selective Minimization of Masking Using Inter-Channel Dependency Effects

Full seven-page DAFx-08 paper read: cross-adaptive design, dependency mapping, masking proxy, controls, examples and future work. [DAFx paper](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2008/papers/dafx08_15.pdf)

Bounded findings:

- A user-selected master channel keeps unity gain while other channels are attenuated according to coarse spectral-class similarity.
- The actual correction is full-band gain, not frequency-selective EQ. It can therefore change musical balance even when the collision is local to one band or one phrase.
- The masking proxy uses a 1024-point FFT without a window and quantized bin comparison. The authors explicitly leave integration of a psychoacoustic model for future work.
- Results demonstrate the proposed dependency display and gain behavior; they do not include a listener study proving better mixes.
- Therefore spectral similarity may be a diagnostic feature, but it is not a sufficient masking label and cannot justify automatic whole-track attenuation.

## Knowledge and rule changes

Added **2 Source Cards, 9 Knowledge Cards and 7 rule candidates**. Main limits:

1. try role-aware level, arrangement and pan baselines before destructive EQ;
2. treat an instrument-class average spectrum as a proposal, never a target to flatten every track toward;
3. distinguish persistent tonal problems from intermittent collisions before choosing static or dynamic EQ;
4. stop automatic target selection when instrument classification or section role is uncertain;
5. require full-mix, mono and section checks; isolated-track preference is insufficient;
6. record FFT, window, hop, channel handling, activity mask and level normalization for every masking/spectral metric;
7. never grant vocal or lead permanent priority: the protected source follows the musical role in each section.

The only proposed numeric action range is a conservative internal A/B grid of `0/1/2/3 dB` maximum attenuation for a role-aware dynamic de-masking branch. Its origin is explicitly **project experiment design**, not either paper or a genre norm. Attack/release, Q and threshold remain source-dependent and unadopted.

## Handoff for mixing agents

Do not equate an isolated-track spectral target with de-masking. First establish the section role and test level/pan. Use static EQ for a stable tonal problem and only test bounded dynamic attenuation for an intermittent collision. Preserve the rhythm-guitar foundation in choruses; do not give vocal or lead automatic global priority. Every masking metric must expose its analysis settings and still pass a loudness-matched full-mix/mono listening test.
