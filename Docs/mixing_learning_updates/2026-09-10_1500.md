## Literature / scientific sources reviewed

### 1. Diff2Mix: Controllable Music Mixing via Diffusion Models and Differentiable Audio Effects
- Authors: Yisu Zong, Jinjie Shi, Joshua Reiss.
- Submitted: 2026-08-05.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05442
- New value: reference-conditioned style control is paired with explicit differentiable-console parameters, keeping the result interpretable and editable rather than only producing an opaque mix render.
- Learning impact: added candidate rules for explicit console control and multi-axis evaluation. Do not treat one objective metric as sufficient evidence of a better mix.

### 2. Rethinking Automatic Music Mixing as Sequential Stem Blending
- Authors: Yen-Tung Yeh, Chung-Jui Chan, Yun-Ning Hung, Yi-Hsuan Yang.
- Submitted: 2026-08-06.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05506
- New value: reframes automatic mixing as adding stems sequentially into a growing submix, conditioned on current mix context, and supports arbitrary input track counts.
- Learning impact: added an offline research candidate to compare sequential/context-conditioned blending with the existing parallel approach. Must test order robustness.

### 3. AILive Mixer: A Deep Learning based Zero Latency Automatic Music Mixer for Live Music Performances
- Authors: Devansh Zurale, Iris Lorente, Michael Lester, Alex Mitchell.
- Submitted: 2026-03-16; accepted to ICASSP 2026.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15995
- New value: explicitly targets live acoustic bleed and zero-latency constraints. Current published system predicts mono gains, not a full EQ/dynamics/spatial mix.
- Learning impact: added a bounded zero-latency, bleed-aware, gain-only baseline for HIL/live research. Do not over-generalize it into a full studio mixer.

### 4. Reverse Engineering of Music Mixing Graphs With Differentiable Processors and Iterative Pruning
- Authors: Sungho Lee, Marco A. Martínez-Ramírez, Wei-Hsiang Liao, Stefan Uhlich, Giorgio Fabbro, Kyogu Lee, Yuki Mitsufuji.
- Journal: Journal of the Audio Engineering Society 73(6), June 2025.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.17743/jaes.2022.0212
- Open preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15948
- New value: fit an over-complete differentiable mix graph, remove negligible processors, then fine-tune. The study reports preservation of full-console quality while eliminating roughly two-thirds of processors; the exact ratio is study-specific.
- Learning impact: added a candidate rule for processor pruning after full-fit optimization rather than assuming every effect is necessary.

## Next A/B / audio experiments

1. **Reference-style controller:** on one legal Cambridge-MT rock project, compare current rule-based/reference workflow vs an explicit-console style candidate inspired by Diff2Mix. Measure multiple axes; listening verdict is primary.
2. **Sequential blending:** test at least 4 different stem orders on the same song. Reject the method if mix quality changes materially with order.
3. **Bleed-aware live baseline:** on a bleed-heavy multitrack, compare static/manual gain vs zero-lookahead gain-only controller before attempting EQ/dynamics automation.
4. **Drum cleanup:** compare hard gating vs selective spill control on snare/toms; score ghost-note retention, transient consistency, cymbal chatter and full-mix punch.
5. **Rock vocal depth:** level-match plate returns while testing short pre-delay vs approximately 80 ms vs longer values; judge vocal forwardness, intelligibility and whether the reverb separates into an audible echo.
6. **Mix-graph pruning:** offline only, fit a richer candidate chain and prune negligible stages; compare full vs pruned chain blind and level-matched.
