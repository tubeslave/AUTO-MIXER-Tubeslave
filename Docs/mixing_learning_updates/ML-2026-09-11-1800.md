## What is genuinely new

Three sources absent from the accessible `master` index were read in full: MEGAMI, RelFx and StemFX. Together they change the reference-matching plan in three ways:

1. Automatic mixing should be treated as one-to-many: generate several bounded, coordinated candidates instead of assuming one deterministic optimum.
2. When no truly dry reference exists, compare the directional change from the current sound to the target, section by section, rather than only matching an absolute target descriptor.
3. Select a sparse effect topology and order before optimizing parameters. A universal chain can create distortion or modulation even when every module is nominally bypassable.

## Literature / implementations actually read

### MEGAMI — generative effect embeddings

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08040
- Official code: https://github.com/SonyResearch/MEGAMI
- Full HTML sections covering method, datasets, objective evaluation, listening test and limitations were read.
- The paper models a distribution of valid professional mixes, augments effect embeddings with log-RMS, crest factor, dynamic spread and stereo descriptors, and evaluates with distributional distances plus listening.
- Limits: 12 listeners, seven songs, wide song-dependent uncertainty, time-invariant track embeddings, no master-bus effects, partly internal data. Study scores are not project thresholds.

### RelFx — relative transformation without a dry reference

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10573v1
- Accepted at ISMIR 2026; arXiv DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2608.10573`.
- Full method, experiments, ablations and limitations were read.
- Same-section adjacent clips reduced content shortcuts. Crucially, some ablations improved retrieval while worsening style-transfer loss, so retrieval alone is not a valid acceptance gate.
- Limits: it models overall transformations, not source-specific effects inside mixtures; unseen real-world chain orders remain open; no broad rock-production listening study.

### StemFX — variable-length per-stem chain prediction

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15634v2
- Official code: https://github.com/barry-mir/stemfx
- Effect toolkit: https://github.com/barry-mir/multiafx
- Accepted at ISMIR 2026; arXiv DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2607.15634`.
- The paper's main practical result is structural: choosing which effects to use and in what order beat the tested universal fixed-chain baselines. Low-scoring fixed-chain outputs often contained distortion or modulation absent from the target.
- Limits: four stems, separator artifacts not quantified, random training chains may not reflect genre practice, 20-person study. Code was inspected but not run.
