- Stable ID: `arxiv:2609.35295`
- DOI: not stated on the arXiv record

- Venue/version: DAFx-26 proceedings paper; arXiv v1
- Proceedings date: 2026-09
- arXiv submission date: 2026-09-28
- Discovery date: 2026-09-29
- URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35295 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35295
- Licence: CC BY 4.0 on the arXiv record
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete_4_pages`
- Evidence type: simulation-based system-identification experiment
- Independence: primary paper; no independent reproduction found in this search
- Citation check: `partial`

Verified method and result:

1. Task A estimates six identifiable plate parameters from one 5 s, 44.1 kHz, unnormalised impulse response.
2. The authors explicitly retain amplitude because absolute level is inversely related to `mu`; peak normalisation would discard that information.
3. The posterior model is a neural spline flow conditioned on three spectrogram resolutions plus log-RMS. Training uses 60,000 simulator pairs; the paper reports 145 epochs, batch 128, Adam 1e-3 and about 26 A100 hours.
4. Per-observation refinement uses three rounds of 10,000 extra simulations and about 2.5 A100 hours per test observation.
5. On 50 simulated test IRs, reported global NMSE ×10^-3 is 2.17 offline, 3.80 refined, 0.47 after waveform-L2 selection, and 0.34 for an oracle selector. Refinement improves 40/50 cases and the median, but rare catastrophic failures worsen the mean.
6. The waveform reconstruction selector reduces average error but sometimes chooses the wrong parameter estimate.
7. Offline posterior calibration is checked on 500 held-out simulated plates and is slightly overconfident; refinement calibration is not established.

Limits: exact simulator/domain match; 50-example main test; no measured plate IR; no noise or model-mismatch stress test; no listening test; no musical-mix evaluation; no proof that a lower parameter error yields a preferred reverb.

## Knowledge cards

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-01

For parameter identification, normalisation is part of the measurement contract: if raw gain carries information about a target parameter, peak normalisation can make that parameter less identifiable. This does not imply that raw gain should drive creative reverb matching. Confidence: `high_within_simulated_task`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-02

Per-observation refinement can improve the median while worsening the mean through rare catastrophic failures; report tail failures and not only central tendency. Confidence: `high_within_reported_test`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-03

Lower waveform reconstruction error is an imperfect proxy for lower physical-parameter error. A selector must be evaluated against the actual target and its own failure cases. Confidence: `high_within_reported_test`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-04

Calibration of the offline posterior does not establish calibration of the refined posterior. Confidence: `high`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-05

Exact-simulator performance cannot be transferred to measured plates, noise, model mismatch, mix quality or listener preference without new validation. Confidence: `high`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-06

A drum-room role can be split into low-frequency weight and midrange ambience; filtering a room capture is a context-bound tonal decision, not a universal room-mic recipe. Confidence: `medium`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-07

Selective close-mic sends can define which hits excite a synthetic room independently of the dry-track faders. This is a routing hypothesis requiring gain, groove, bleed and tail checks. Confidence: `medium`.

### KC-ML-20260929-1500-08

The article's approximately 20% sustain reduction and approximately 70 Hz detector high-pass are one-session values. They require controlled A/B before reuse. Confidence: `medium_for_provenance_low_for_transfer`.
