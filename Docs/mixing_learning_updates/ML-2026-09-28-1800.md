## Source Cards

### `SRC-ML-20260928-1800-01` — A Stem-Agnostic Single-Decoder System for Music Source Separation Beyond Four Stems

- **Stable ID / source:** `arXiv:2406.18747`, ISMIR 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18747
- **Reading depth:** complete nine-page paper, including architecture, data/splits, query construction, training, inference, all result sections, limitations, ethics and artifact links.
- **Method:** Banquet combines a frozen or trainable OpenMIC PaSST query encoder with one Bandit encoder/decoder and FiLM-style conditioning. It predicts a complex time-frequency mask. The fully trainable variant has `24.9M` trainable parameters; the frozen-encoder variant has `13.5M`.
- **Data/protocol:** MoisesDB's 240 songs are split by genre into `144/48/48` train/validation/test songs. A ten-second clean stem excerpt with highest mean onset strength becomes a query. Training mixtures use six-second chunks; test tracks use six-second windows with `0.5 s` hop and Hann overlap-add. Different-song queries prefer the same genre and a different artist.
- **Results:** on the paper's VDBGP task the best Banquet variant reports median SNR `7.6/7.9/9.5/11.0/3.3/2.5 dB` for vocals/lead vocals/drums/bass/guitar/piano. Same-song and different-song query medians are within `0.2 dB`, but that is category-level separation evidence, not proof that the query can preserve or select a particular performer/timbre.
- **Long-tail boundary:** on the all-stem experiment no long-tail sample exceeds `5 dB` SNR; performance correlates strongly with target-track RMS (`Spearman rho 0.78–0.81`). The authors connect this to sparse data, low activity/level and overlap, and acknowledge genre/cultural imbalance.
- **Limited conclusion:** a single query-conditioned decoder can cover more stem labels without one decoder per label, and the published experiment supports useful VDBGP separation. It does not establish reliable long-tail extraction, unknown-source discovery, instance-specific identity or automatic mixing control.

### `SRC-ML-20260928-1800-02` — A conditioned UNet for Music Source Separation

- **Stable ID / source:** `arXiv:2512.15532v1` — https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15532
- **Status/date:** preprint submitted 17 December 2025; discovered earlier than this run and promoted now through complete reading.
- **Reading depth:** complete HTML paper, including rationale, SCNet/QSCNet construction, data, query pools, augmentation, training, metrics, both result tables and stated limitations.
- **Method:** QSCNet adds one FiLM module between the SCNet encoder and dual-path recurrent neck. It uses the same OpenMIC PaSST family and 768-dimensional query embedding as Banquet, but has `10.2M` parameters.
- **Data/protocol:** the authors reuse Banquet's `144/48/48` MoisesDB split, train on random mixtures of unrelated ten-second stem clips, sample instrument classes evenly, reject query clips with at least `20%` zero samples, train for 300 epochs on one A100 80 GB GPU, and select the best validation or EMA model.
- **Reported comparison:** on five common coarse stems, QSCNet reports Avg5 median SNR `8.5 dB` versus Banquet `6.9 dB`; on nine fine stems Avg9 is `6.3` versus `5.3 dB`. It reports gains for every listed stem and uses fewer than half Banquet's parameters.
- **Causal limitation:** architecture, segment length, mixture construction, loss, batch/training schedule, stem vocabulary and conditioning position differ. There is no one-factor ablation against Banquet, no listening test, no uncertainty interval and no public code/weights link in the paper. The authors explicitly say some gains may come from other network/training parameters.
- **Limited conclusion:** QSCNet is credible independent counterevidence to the claim that a conditioned UNet is unsuitable. Its reported SNR advantage is not isolated evidence that UNet topology or FiLM location caused the gain.
