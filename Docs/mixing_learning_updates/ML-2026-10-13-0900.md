### SRC-ML-20261013-0900-01 — Reference-Free Singing Pitch Correction via Music-Constrained Sequence Editing

- **Canonical ID:** `arXiv:2610.11524v1`
- **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2610.11524` (arXiv/DataCite registration shown as pending)
- **Publication state/date:** preprint submitted 2026-10-08; submitted to ICASSP 2027, not treated as accepted or peer reviewed
- **URLs:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11524>, <https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11524>, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.11524>, <https://dongb001.github.io/RF-SPC/>
- **Reading depth:** full experimental HTML + five-page PDF + full public project page
- **Citation check:** `partial`

The method factorises the decision into (1) whether a note should be changed and (2) which non-zero signed semitone offset to choose. A frozen MusicBERT encoder with lightweight adapters supplies context; a tonal prior derived from a 24-key posterior reranks offsets `0, ±1, ±2, ±3`. Timing is preserved and only pitch is edited. Training used automatically extracted MIDI from about 1,060 h / 16,000 professional Chinese and English pop recordings with synthetic note perturbations. Evaluation used a separate private paired amateur/professional set split by song: 20 validation songs (8,299 notes) and 80 held-out test songs (30,682 notes).

Reported exact-note accuracy rises from 73.25% input to 83.43%; BERT-APC reaches 79.44%. The full system repairs 46.81% of erroneous notes while changing 3.19% of initially correct notes. Hard key snapping is worse than input (71.76% accuracy; 9.62% harm). Song-level Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction report `p < 0.001` for the objective comparison with BERT-APC. Ablations show that the full system improves repair but does not minimise harm: BERT-APC and the flat-head/no-tonal variants have lower harm rates.

The subjective study used 30 vocally trained participants, each rating five randomly selected 8–12 s excerpts. The proposed method has the highest reported mean overall score (3.94), but the paper reports means rather than inferential statistics for these listener differences. All compared methods share SOME transcription and NSF-HiFi-GAN resynthesis, so renderer artefacts are common dependencies rather than evidence for the symbolic editor.

**Limits:** private paired evaluation data; professional performance is a useful reference, not absolute artistic truth; a cappella/reference-free domain rather than a dense rock mix; integer-semitone symbolic edits do not model microtonal intent, scoops, blue notes, vibrato, growl or formants; no public code/checkpoint/data release was found; the project page still contains placeholder author/affiliation text. Numerical tonal-prior settings are protocol-specific and are not project presets.
