## Sources actually read or rechecked

### `SRC-ML-20260924-0900-01` — MIDIBack

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28008>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28008v1`
- **Depth:** full text rechecked; data, target construction, perturbations, baselines, ablations and case study

The target is `round(tilde p_i)`: rounded pitch from the same GAME transcription used to construct model inputs, not an independent score. Training corruptions are global `±0.25–1.0`-semitone phrase shifts, a frozen learned detuner, and uniform `[-1,1]`-semitone noise. They do not cover note substitutions or boundary errors independently of the transcription stack.

All DDPC and BERT-APC baseline numbers are reproductions on MIDIBack's datasets and configuration. They are therefore useful within-protocol comparators, but not original published baseline evaluations. Without code, weights, split manifest or exact dependency settings, implementation parity is not independently verifiable.

The `80/10/10` split uses seed 42, but the grouping unit and stratification are not formalized. The text does not establish singer-, artist-, song-family-, or source-dataset-disjoint evaluation. The model's maximum sequence is 1,024 Octuple tokens; truncation and long-song segmentation behavior are not documented.

The case study shifts backing transcriptions by `±0.5` or `±1.0` semitone while fixing vocal notes to ground truth. Model outputs follow the manipulation. This demonstrates counterfactual sensitivity to accompaniment tokens, not that the new notes are musically correct or that the system performs human-like harmonic reasoning.

**Revised bounded conclusion:** MIDIBack supports accompaniment-aware symbolic hypothesis generation under simulated corruption. It does not yet validate independent musical targets, waveform correction, perceptual preference, or autonomous use.

### `SRC-ML-20260915-1200-01` — BERT-APC comparator

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20006v3>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2511.20006v3`
- **Depth:** targeted full-text comparison with MIDIBack

BERT-APC uses score-derived MIDI ground truth, evaluates an end-to-end TD-PSOLA correction path, and includes a listening test. MIDIBack reuses its learnable-detuner idea but replaces score targets with rounded automatic transcriptions. Thus the two works form a related/dependent method family, and MIDIBack's reproduced BERT baseline is not independent confirmation of its pseudo-target protocol.

## Knowledge-card revisions

1. Recovery against rounded transcription can measure consistency with the transcription stack rather than correctness against musical intent.
2. MIDIBack's BERT-APC comparison is a reimplementation under pseudo-target conditions, not BERT-APC's score-grounded audio evaluation.
3. The learned detuner makes BERT-APC a related method-family comparator, not independent confirmation.
4. Accompaniment improves synthetic global-shift recovery but worsens clean-note preservation, requiring a reject gate.
5. Following artificially shifted backing tokens proves sensitivity, not harmonic correctness.
6. GAME/MuScriptor versions, checkpoints and decoding are latent experimental factors missing from the reproducibility record.
7. The 1,024-token limit lacks a documented truncation/segmentation policy.
8. The mixed corpus lacks a public per-source split and rights manifest.
9. MuScriptor code is MIT, while checkpoints are CC BY-NC 4.0; code availability does not grant unrestricted weight use.
10. Public DAFx artifacts do not expose the participant implementation despite organizer receipt of code.
11. The 5,000-, 200-, and 16-IR plate results cannot be pooled.
12. Real-plate testing needs signal/perceptual endpoints because synthetic physical labels may not be uniquely valid.
