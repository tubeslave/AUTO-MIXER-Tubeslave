### Extracted techniques and context

1. **Keep the sound that defined the composition when reamping does not improve it.** At 4:23–5:40 the band compared multiple real amplifiers against the demo/plugin tones and retained the plugin because reamping was recreating, rather than improving, the established album sound. This supports an unchanged-demo comparator, not a blanket plugin-over-amp rule.
2. **Tone shaping was arrangement- and part-specific.** At 6:47–8:23 Mansoor describes a common rhythm setup: Amp 2, reduced compressor detail for a bright guitar, substantial attack from a drive stage, optional girth with a macro, cabinet 2 with a 57 around the shown 300–400 position, and optional 184/160 additions for warmth or low mids. Plugin names/positions are product-specific and must be verified visually before numeric transfer; no universal frequency or gain values were stated.
3. **Character may outweigh isolated neatness.** At 8:58–10:08 a miswired baritone sound that was nasal alone was retained because its combination with the other pass worked in context. At 10:00 the speaker explicitly says take tightness was secondary to the intended vibe.
4. **Edit delayed DI layers against musical context, not automatically to the grid.** At 10:44–11:24 he explains that delay before the amp makes timing errors repeat, but the DI can be nudged while preserving a favored take; exact grid placement is not always the musical target.
5. **Use a texture layer's spatial allocation to protect the center.** At 12:53–13:59 a delayed/distorted lead layer is widened and the center is cleared so the chorus vocal can remain central; a small EQ move is then used to tame the layer. No width or EQ amount was stated.
6. **Complementary amp voicings can be tested while holding cabinet choice similar.** At 14:19–14:42 he describes combining Amp 2 and Amp 3 for four-track parts, especially with similar cabinet settings; the voicings are described as 5150-like versus hot-rodded-Marshall-like. This is stylistic, product-specific evidence, not a universal quad-track recipe.
