### Method actually read

FDverb combines sparse time-domain early reflections with a stochastic STFT late tail. Early-reflection delays are drawn from a quadratic distribution between `pre_delay` and `tail_delay`; the first reflection is fixed at `pre_delay`. The pseudo-random generator is seeded from `pre_delay`, making the result repeatable but also making a pre-delay change alter the remaining tap pattern.

The tail uses 50% overlap, a sine analysis/synthesis window and per-bin attack/release envelopes. Detector magnitude is raised to `color` gamma; gamma=1 is the default. Attack is fixed to 10 ms. Decay can be tilted by frequency. Spectral noise is generated independently per bin and shaped by those envelopes.

Block size is a compound control: a larger block increases frequency resolution and tonal character but adds temporal smearing and latency; a smaller block produces a noisier tail with sharper time tracking. Algorithmic latency is `N/(2*fs)` before processing time; the paper's example is about 85 ms for N=8192 at 48 kHz. Early reflections can begin sooner and mask the delayed tail, but do not remove the latency or the lower bound on tail onset.

Alternative modes provide exponential, arithmetic/gated-like and freeze behaviour. `shift` and `drift` move spectral-bin/state indices linearly in frequency, so they do not preserve harmonic intervals and intentionally yield inharmonic tails. Brick-wall low/high cuts operate on the noise carrier.

Stereo processing runs one tail per channel. `width` controls noise correlation from fully correlated through uncorrelated to fully anti-correlated/side-only; it also controls the panning spread of early-reflection taps. `link` separately controls whether channel envelopes are independent or shared.

The plug-in supports AU/VST3, up to 32 early reflections, maximum 500 ms ER delay and FFT block sizes 2^5 through 2^14. A background thread and lock-free queues bridge DAW buffer sizes. Reported processing time is 0.011 ms/block at N=32 through 3.81 ms/block at N=16384; REAPER v7.76 reported under 2.6% for one instance on an M2 Pro at 48 kHz and 512-sample I/O. This is one host/machine condition, not a target-host real-time guarantee.

The v1.0→v1.1.0 comparison is six commits and changes early reflections, envelope processing, main DSP/UI code, presets and build configuration. The changelog states that ER decay became fixed rather than linked to tail decay, some presets changed, distorted/silent ER output was fixed, fractional drift was added, low tail-delay writeback was fixed, AU validation/sandbox declaration was fixed, macOS minimum version was corrected, bypass tail behaviour was fixed and extreme-tilt mode-switch silence was fixed.

- use v1.0 only for paper-parity analysis;
- use v1.1.0 only when the deployment/experiment manifest explicitly pins it;
- do not compare v1.0 and v1.1.0 as independent evidence;

1. `KC-ML-20261017-1200-01` — FDverb is a hybrid sparse-ER/STFT-noise-tail design; open implementation does not prove perceptual quality, naturalness or mix improvement. Confidence: high_method_and_scope_limit.
2. `KC-ML-20261017-1200-02` — Block size jointly changes spectral resolution, tonal/noise character, temporal smearing and latency; treating it as a single “quality” knob is unsafe. Confidence: high_method.
3. `KC-ML-20261017-1200-03` — ERs can precede and perceptually bridge a long-latency tail, but they do not remove `N/(2*fs)` tail latency or the minimum tail-delay constraint. Confidence: high_method.
4. `KC-ML-20261017-1200-04` — Because `pre_delay` seeds the ER PRNG, changing it also changes the later tap pattern; a pre-delay A/B is not otherwise tap-pattern controlled. Confidence: high_implementation_description.
5. `KC-ML-20261017-1200-05` — Width can reach anti-correlated/side-only tails and also spreads ER taps; mono/downmix and correlation checks are mandatory. Confidence: high_method_plus_safety_gate.

7. `KC-ML-20261017-1200-07` — Shift/drift are linear-bin operations and produce inharmonic tails; they are creative effects, not transparent pitch modulation. Confidence: high_method.
