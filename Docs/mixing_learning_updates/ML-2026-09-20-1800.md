### SRC-ML-20260920-1800-01 — DDSPMM-Guided Diffusion for Synthesis Parameter Estimation

- Takemoto, K.; Nakamura, T.; Saruwatari, H.
- Stable identifier: `arXiv:2609.14427v1`; submitted 2026-09-13 and marked by the authors as accepted for APSIPA Annual Summit and Conference 2026.
- [arXiv abstract/status](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14427) · [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14427) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14427)
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete`; method, equations, training setup, all results, figures, conclusion and references read.

The method replaces unconstrained frame-wise fitting with conditional reverse diffusion over source-wise F0, loudness and 16-dimensional timbre trajectories. It conditions on instrument class and aligned MIDI pitch, and at every reverse step adds the gradient of the multi-scale spectral reconstruction loss through fixed DDSP source synthesizers. The reported system uses 1000 diffusion steps and a late-rising guidance schedule with `lambda_end=3.0`, `omega=4.0`; the authors state that strong early guidance destabilized preliminary runs.

The evidence supports a narrow conclusion: on four URMP mixtures of two or three classical harmonic instruments, the prior consistently reduced loudness and MFCC errors relative to unconstrained DDSPMM. It did not improve every criterion. For clarinet/violin, F0 MAE worsened from `9.82` to `12.8` cents and Zimtohrli fell from `3.77` to `3.71`; in other mixtures the perceptual metric improved. Therefore smoother or more likely trajectories are not a monotonic guarantee of pitch accuracy or perceptual similarity.

Important limitations:

- Evaluation covers four named mixtures, classical bowed strings/woodwinds, an aligned score and independent 12-second chunks; it does not establish robustness for rock drums, distorted guitars, bleed, dense arrangements or section boundaries.
- DDPM pretraining uses 652 twelve-second segments derived from the same isolated-instrument recordings used to train the DDSP autoencoder in the foundational work. The paper does not give a piece/source-disjoint map between those recordings and the URMP evaluation mixtures, so leakage risk cannot be ruled out.
- The stochastic reverse process is not accompanied by a seed-sensitivity or repeatability analysis.
- Multi-scale magnitude loss is computed on the summed mixture. A good mixture fit can redistribute error between sources and therefore does not prove source identity or non-target invariance.
- Zimtohrli, parameter MAEs and informal listening are not a controlled preference test. No phase/mono, raw gain, true peak, transient, tail, compute or real-time measurements are reported.
- The `lambda_end`, exponent, iteration count and initialization values are method-specific experimental settings, not mixing presets.

### SRC-ML-20260920-1800-02 — foundational DDSP mixture model

- Kawamura, M.; Nakamura, T.; Kitamura, D.; Saruwatari, H.; Takahashi, Y.; Kondo, K., ICASSP 2022.
- Stable identifiers: `arXiv:2202.00200`; DOI `10.1109/ICASSP43922.2022.9746399`.
- [open full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.00200v1) · [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00200) · [author demo page](https://sarulab-audio.github.io/DDSP_Mixture_Model/)
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete`; method, initialization, training/evaluation setup and complete results read. Demo audio was not downloaded or analyzed.

The foundational model sums outputs of fixed pretrained DDSP source synthesizers and optimizes source-specific F0, loudness and timbre parameters against a mixture-level multi-scale spectral loss. Its score-informed version substantially stabilizes several parameter estimates relative to separation followed by DDSP analysis, but the comparison is limited to two-source classical mixtures at 16 kHz. The paper's audible-superiority statement is based on author listening, not a controlled study. It also shows that estimated parameters can be negative or otherwise leave the natural source manifold, motivating the newer trajectory prior.

This source clarifies why “no explicit source separation” is not the same as “perfect source isolation”: the inverse problem is underdetermined at mixture level, and its source-wise decomposition inherits the renderer and score priors. It also confirms that the DDSP autoencoder training pool comprised 35 of 44 URMP pieces; the new paper does not disclose whether its four URMP evaluation mixtures are disjoint from that pool.

## Revised conclusions

1. The DAFx demo's cited “submitted” method now has a stable full text and author-reported acceptance at APSIPA ASC 2026; it is one work/version family, not independent confirmation.
2. The DDPM prior has evidence for reducing loudness/timbre-trajectory error in the tested setting, but not for universal source-edit quality: F0 and Zimtohrli do not improve in every mixture.
3. “Smoother” must be tested against intentional vibrato, attacks and phrasing. Temporal plausibility can become over-regularization.
4. Frozen non-target parameters remain insufficient evidence of unchanged non-target audio because the initial analysis-by-synthesis decomposition is approximate and the mixture loss can trade errors among sources.
5. A production claim now requires four gates: source/piece-disjoint evaluation, unchanged reconstruction control, target/non-target residuals, and loudness-matched controlled listening.
6. The warm-init companion's embedded audio remains excluded from reusable Reference Cards until file-level provenance and rights are explicit.
