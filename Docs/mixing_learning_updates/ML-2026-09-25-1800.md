## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1500-01` — synth-JDF, updated

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29320>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.29320v1`
- **Depth this run:** full-text re-review of data, controls, metric table, inference sweeps and limitations

The strongest causal comparison is not JDF against the different encoder-conditioned baseline, but JDF against `Unif-ParamOnly`: both use the shared-token DiT, while `Unif-ParamOnly` is trained only on the conditional inversion edge. On Surge real audio, MSS is `7.68` for JDF and `11.35` for `Unif-ParamOnly`; the authors therefore have evidence that training on the full time square matters. This is still a bundled change in training support/objective, not a direct proof that one mathematical component alone causes the gain.

The real-audio marginal remains unsupported as a useful addition: JDF and JDF-A are essentially tied on off-manifold MSS (`7.68` versus `7.69`). The test pool mixes Freesound single events with a proprietary production library, and the paper notes that many one-shots contain silent tails. Results should therefore be stratified by source family, silence fraction and transient/sustained character before they are generalized.

The favourable `tau≈0.1` region comes from the same reported inference sweep used to discuss performance. A separate tuning split for choosing `tau` is not described. Treat it as a post-hoc hypothesis and lock it on validation before a held-out test.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1800-01` — foundational symmetric inversion comparator

- **Title:** *Audio synthesizer inversion in symmetric parameter spaces with approximately equivariant flow matching*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07199>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2506.07199v1`
- **Publication date:** 2025-06-08
- **Discovery depth:** full HTML, including task contract, data, models, OOD results, appendices and repository link
- **Relation:** same lead author, same Surge/Param2Tok lineage; foundational comparator, not independent confirmation

This paper deliberately studies on-manifold inversion to avoid the confound of projecting arbitrary audio onto a synthesizer manifold. It establishes the permutation-symmetry motivation and the generative Param2Tok baseline later used by synth-JDF. Its out-of-distribution NSynth/FSD50K results show degradation even before the later joint-density extension.

Cross-paper numeric comparison is unsafe. The earlier Surge `Full` task includes fixed Chorus, Delay and Reverb II routing and 165 parameters; synth-JDF says it uses a similar active set but disables audio effects. Solver, model and data contracts also differ (`100` RK4 steps in the older work versus `20` Euler steps in synth-JDF). The earlier result validates the lineage, not the later off-manifold claim independently.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1500-02` — NoteSep, updated

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29071>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.29071v1`
- **Depth this run:** full-text re-review of ASO equations, compute, editing protocol, real transfer and artifact statement

The paper explicitly says ASO falls back to raw note magnitudes with mixture phase when all ownership weights are zero, trading off perfect mixture consistency for less leakage. The earlier card is corrected: ASO can reduce independently estimated-note inconsistency, but it is not an exact waveform-sum constraint.

The ablation improves median SI-SDR from `5.09 dB` for NoteGrab Selective to `7.39 dB` for NoteSep, while median target gain remains `0.83` and off-target energy is `0.11` versus `0.12`. This supports better allocation, not transparent reconstruction. Editing uses stored note estimates and never subtracts a target estimate from the input mixture. The reported unedited `2f` score (`71.23`) is therefore a reconstruction metric, not a null or listener proof.

Compute scales with the number of queried notes: a two-second, 16-query input needs 16 NoteGrab passes plus one ASO pass; the paper reports `454.7/32.7 ms` and `840.1/828.5 MiB` peak allocation for separator/ASO on an NVIDIA A10. Dense passages and live use need separate latency and memory validation.

The selective HPSS path applies a high-pass cutoff `max(30, f0−60)` Hz only under a lower-octave-overlap condition. That number is a study-specific preprocessing rule and can remove target low-frequency energy; it is not a production EQ setting.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1500-03` — additive CLAP representations, updated

- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27187>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.27187v1`
- **Depth this run:** full-text re-review of grouping, holdouts, baselines, metric table and limitations

The procedure groups clips by exact source-label set and averages their embeddings before fitting source contributions. It therefore predicts a combination mean and removes within-combination clip variation from the target. Repeated holdouts also move combinations back to training when necessary so that every held-out source remains represented in training. The correct claim is generalization to unseen combinations of known labels, not unseen-source discovery.

On FSD50K, CLAP beats both permutation and label-overlap baselines across the reported panel; for example CKA is `0.60±0.02` versus `0.21` and `0.52`. On the smaller CHiME-Home test, CKA is `0.76±0.06` versus `0.73` and `0.62`, a small margin over permutation. Dataset tags are semantic, correlated and potentially incomplete; the contribution vectors need not be physically independent sources.

### `SRC-ML-20260925-1800-02` — independent compositionality comparator

- **Title:** *Evaluating Compositional Structure in Audio Representations*
- **Primary:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13685>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2603.13685v1`
- **DOI:** `10.1109/ICASSP55912.2026.11465018`
- **Publication date:** 2026-03-14 (arXiv v1); ICASSP 2026
- **Depth:** full HTML, including synthetic data generator, A-COAT/A-TRE definitions, controls, statistics, results and conclusion
- **Independence:** different authors and controlled synthetic protocol; not a natural-recording replication

This comparator builds controlled ten-second, 32-kHz synthetic scenes from one to four normalized DX7-like sources. A-COAT tests consistency of embedding differences when the same one-to-three sources are added to two base scenes; A-TRE predicts embeddings from timbre, pitch, rate and amplitude attributes.

Its key warning is structural: the linear Downsample baseline achieves a perfect A-COAT score (`1.00±0.01`) while performing poorly on A-TRE (`0.23±0.16`). CLAP reaches only `0.39±0.20` on A-COAT and `0.90±0.05` on A-TRE. A high additive score can therefore reflect preservation of signal linearity without useful semantic factorization, and a high reconstructibility score can coexist with weaker additive transformations. The paper itself leaves extension beyond synthetic data and linkage to downstream performance for future work.

## Revised conclusions

- **Revised:** `Unif-ParamOnly` controls much of the architecture difference, so synth-JDF has stronger evidence for full-time-square training than the 15:00 summary stated. It still does not isolate every objective component or validate musical usefulness.
- **Revised:** `tau≈0.1` is a post-hoc sweep result until validation selection and locked held-out testing are shown.
- **Corrected:** NoteSep/ASO is not perfectly mixture-consistent; the method explicitly permits a fallback that sacrifices perfect consistency.
- **Narrowed:** CLAP additivity concerns averages of known semantic label combinations. It is not evidence for per-clip physical source arithmetic or mix-control parameters.
- **Independence:** the 2025 synth paper is same-family evidence. The ICASSP compositionality benchmark is independent but synthetic and tests a different notion of composition.

## Knowledge cards

1. A same-architecture conditional-edge control is stronger causal evidence than comparison with a differently conditioned encoder baseline.
2. Full-time-square JDF training is supported as a bundled objective/support change; the unpaired-real marginal is not.
3. A hyperparameter selected from a displayed test sweep must be reselected on validation and locked before held-out evaluation.
4. Synth-inversion metrics cannot be compared across papers when active parameters, effects, solver and data contracts differ.
5. Source-family, silence-ratio and transient/sustain strata are necessary when an off-manifold pool mixes Freesound and proprietary one-shots.
6. NoteSep ASO reduces ownership conflicts but explicitly does not guarantee perfect mixture consistency.
7. Stored-estimate editing reconstructs unchanged material; a high reference metric does not replace an unedited-sum null.
8. Note-level inference cost scales with query count and can become material in dense arrangements.
9. The `max(30, f0−60)` Hz selective high-pass is a paper preprocessing rule, not a production EQ preset.
10. CLAP reconstruction predicts mean embeddings of label combinations, not individual clips or isolated physical sources.
11. Combination-disjoint evaluation with all sources known does not test unseen-source generalization.
12. A perfect linear A-COAT baseline shows that additive geometry and useful semantic decomposition are distinct properties.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1800-01` — full-time-square training ablation

- **Variant B:** JDF, same shared-token architecture trained over the full time square.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1800-03` — ASO sum-conservation audit

- **Baseline A:** independently summed NoteGrab Selective estimates.
- **Variant B:** the same estimates after ASO.
- **Only changed factor:** ASO ownership/reallocation.
- **Controls:** rights-cleared score-aligned phrase, identical note set/timing/gains; no edit. Raw sums and separate loudness-matched blind copies.
- **Measure:** sample-domain and STFT residual to the input, target gain, off-target energy, SI-SDR, octave confusion, onset/tail error, mono/phase and peaks.
- **Protect:** unchanged notes, fundamentals, attacks and ambience.

### `EXP-ML-20260925-1800-04` — CLAP sensitivity to a real mix control

- **Baseline A:** a rights-cleared fixed stem mix.
- **Variant B:** the same mix with one preregistered source fader changed by `+3 dB`; this number is an experimental probe, not a recommended mix move.
- **Only changed factor:** one source level.
- **Controls:** identical timing, pan, processing, encoder revision and preprocessing. Preserve calibrated raw signals; make separate BS.1770-matched copies only for blind audibility/preference.
- **Measure:** CLAP embedding delta direction/magnitude, explicit source level, integrated/short-term loudness, crest, waveform difference, peaks and blind detection/preference.
- **Protect:** no processing decision from CLAP alone.
- **Success:** repeatable monotonic response distinguishable from total-loudness change across multiple songs/sections.
- **Cancel:** response is smaller than seed/preprocessing variance, non-monotonic or contradicted by calibrated measurements.
