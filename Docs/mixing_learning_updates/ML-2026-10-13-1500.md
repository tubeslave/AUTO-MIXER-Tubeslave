## Source Card

### SRC-ML-20261013-1500-01 — Keyframe Time Stretching via Extrema Sampling

- **Author:** Matthew Nielsen
- **Canonical ID:** `DAFx26_paper_21`
- **Venue/date:** 29th International Conference on Digital Audio Effects (DAFx26), 1–4 September 2026
- **URL:** <https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_21.pdf>
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0
- **Discovered:** 2026-10-13
- **Reading depth:** full eight-page paper
- **Citation check:** `partial`
- **Code:** <https://github.com/heavylight-industries/capicola>

The method stores timestamped local extrema as keyframes, uses extrema density to choose splice length, and reconstructs/stretches audio by content-adaptive overlap-add. It is explicitly positioned as a low-cost creative effect for embedded hardware, not as a transparent restoration algorithm.

The hardware evaluation targets a 480 MHz STM32 H7/Daisy platform at 48 kHz. The paper estimates about 34 cycles/sample offline and 61 live for the proposed method, versus about 4 for OLA, 850 for a phase vocoder, 690 for FFT-WSOLA and 4,100 for time-domain WSOLA. These are operation-count estimates, not measured end-to-end latency, and the authors note that desktop/vectorised implementations narrow the comparison.

The baseline evaluation uses ten TSM-toolbox clips and ratios 0.5, 1.2 and 1.8. At unity with the documented `0.001/-60 dB` analysis threshold, mean STOI is 0.91, LAT deviation 0.09 and spectral-contrast loss 3.08 dB. Material dependence is large: singing voice reaches 0.92/0.15/4.54, while glockenspiel reaches 0.75/0.41/3.84. These numbers describe that implementation/corpus; the threshold is not a project preset.

A 15-listener webMUSHRA-like test used five clips and omitted a hidden reference because durations changed. Averaged across ratios, keyframe scores were 51.1 (bongo), 42.5 (castanets/violin), 48.8 (drum solo), 31.1 (singing voice) and 46.8 (indie pop). WSOLA scored 54.4, 73.3, 55.9, 76.0 and 54.2 respectively; the paper states WSOLA led on four of five clips. The proposed method's local-attack-time deviation was poor at 0.5x (0.875), closer to WSOLA at 1.2x (0.520 versus 0.489), and slightly lower at 1.8x (0.502 versus 0.531).

The decisive limitation is representation loss before stretching: sparsification reduces spectral contrast, and sparse/silent intervals before transients can create repeated segments. Compression at 0.5x underperforms. The listener sample is small and no inferential statistics are reported. Therefore the paper does **not** justify a transparent vocal/full-mix timing default or an automatic engine choice.
