## Source cards

### `SRC-ML-20260923-0900-01` — SpanSynth-Edit

- Primary source: [Synthesis and editing of multi-instrument audio mixtures using scalar-quantised latents with MIDI Span conditioning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25546), arXiv `2609.25546v1`, DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2609.25546`, submitted 22 September 2026; authors mark it as submitted to ICASSP 2027.
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete`, including methods, experiments, ablations, appendices and limitations.
- Core method: frozen HeartCodec at 48 kHz mono, 128-dimensional scalar-quantised latents at 25 Hz, MIDI Span conditioning, and a 480.8M-parameter conditional-flow DiT. Reported training used about 660 hours from 17 instrumental dataset families.
- Evaluation: synthesis and 30/50/70-percent editing spans on Slakh, plus POP909 piano; objective measures combine perceptual embeddings, FAD/MSS and YourMT3+ transcription metrics. No controlled human listening test is reported.
- Important result: objective audio-quality and similarity scores usually improve over the selected baselines, but instrument/note adherence is not uniformly best. FlowEdit improves some FAD/MSS results while reducing other quality and requested-note measures.
- Metric decomposition matters: on the Slakh+drums condition, the paper attributes a much larger transcription-score loss to the evaluator and codec roundtrip than to scalar quantisation alone. Therefore generated-note scores need evaluator and codec ceilings beside them.
- Timing result is limited: the model is insensitive to 1–5 ms MIDI shifts and responds more clearly at 10–40 ms; this does not establish sample-accurate timing control.
- Limits for mixing: mono only; no unchanged-note waveform-preservation test, stereo/pan/phase test, seam/click test, true-peak/loudness test, transient audit, long-tail audit, rock-specific evaluation or listening preference test. Baselines also differ in whether they receive isolated stems or mixtures.

### `SRC-ML-20260923-0900-02` — MambaVoice

- Primary source: [MambaVoice: Lightweight Audiovisual Singing Voice Separation via a Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26635), arXiv `2609.26635v1`, DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2609.26635`, submitted 22 September 2026 and marked accepted at ISMIR 2026.
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete`.
- Method: 16 kHz complex-ratio-mask separator with eight non-uniform bands, facial-landmark ST-GCN gating and a two-Mamba/two-Transformer backbone; 16.2M parameters.
- Results: 14.18 dB SDR on the 50-percent-interference Acappella condition versus 13.76 dB for VoViT; under 100-percent interference, MambaVoice is lower than VoViT (11.11 versus 11.82 dB). On URSing, mean/median SDR are 5.58/10.18 dB, exposing a wide, skewed result distribution.
- The paper correctly narrows its preliminary listening result: 15 experienced participants rated five randomized four-second excerpts; confidence intervals for MambaVoice and VoViT overlap, so the models are comparable rather than one being perceptually better.
- The quoted 3.93 ms runtime is separation-network forward-pass time for a four-second input and excludes landmark extraction, chunk accumulation and tracking. It is not end-to-end latency.
- Mixing limit: visually anchored target identity can be a useful separator hypothesis, but SDR/SIR and five short excerpts do not authorize vocal-stem editing; leakage, additivity, target swapping through silence, phase/mono, boundaries, peaks and downstream mix preference remain untested.

- Positive controls: strict config and state loading, finite-input/output checks, exact retention of latent codes outside the generated mask, exact retention of waveform samples outside the applied interval, float WAV output and a warning when peak exceeds 1.
- Deployment hazards: every input is averaged to mono; the requested boundary is expanded outward to the 25 Hz/40 ms latent grid; replacement uses a hard splice without a crossfade; public runs draw fresh randomness but the CLI has no seed option and `run.json` records no seed; the default Hugging Face revision is mutable `main`.
- The implementation preserves audio only outside the rounded interval. Ordinary editing resynthesizes all notes inside it, including notes whose MIDI was unchanged. The toy FlowEdit equality test is useful numerical plumbing, not an audio-quality or real-checkpoint null test.
- Tests cover small-component parity, serialization and sample preservation outside the interval, but not real-checkpoint perceptual quality, stereo behavior, clicks, gain continuity, transients, peaks or seed robustness.
