### SRC-ML-20260920-1500-01 — Audio-to-Audio via Diffusion Warm Initialization

- Andrade, C.; Schlecht, S. J., DAFx-26, 8 pages, CC BY 4.0.
- Stable identifier: `arXiv:2606.18968`; the DAFx proceedings PDF is the canonical reviewed version for this card.
- [DAFx full text](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_50.pdf) · [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18968) · [companion repository](https://github.com/CristobalAndrade/Audio-to-Audio-via-Diffusion-Warm-Initialization)
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete`; method, experiments, metrics, results, discussion and limitations read.

The method starts a pretrained Stable Audio Open reverse-diffusion trajectory from a guide signal. `tau_init` controls how much of the reverse path is skipped, while `lambda` controls explicit noise injection. The detailed experiment is oboe-to-piano timbre transfer with 100 inference steps, guidance scale 30, 50 outputs per configuration for pitch-set Jaccard distance and 100 outputs for FAD against 100 unconditional piano generations.

The main useful result is a trade-off, not a preset: later initialization preserves more guide structure but aligns less with the target distribution. Additional noise (`lambda=1`) increased pitch deviation and informally heard hallucinated musical events without a material FAD advantage. The paper's `tau_init≈0.8`, `JD<0.6` and `FAD<0.7` are Stable-Audio/Open, prompt, CFG and task-specific observations from informal listening, not universal thresholds or a controlled preference result.

Important limitations:

- FAD at `tau_init=0` remains non-zero because transfer uses CFG 30 while the reference distribution uses CFG 7; the metric therefore mixes timbre fit with inference-policy mismatch.
- The detailed quantitative study covers two timbre-transfer directions; MIDI-to-real and enhancement claims are mainly example-based.
- Human-voice guides perform worse; unwanted sources, especially drums, often persist or become distorted rather than being removed.
- FAD and pitch-set distance do not test raw gain, true peak, transients, mono compatibility, event hallucination or musical preference.
- Initialization, guidance and noise require empirical retuning for another diffusion model.

### SRC-ML-20260920-1500-02 — Diffusion-Based Music Audio Editing System Using Differentiable Digital Signal Processing Mixture Model

- Takemoto, K.; Nakamura, T.; Saruwatari, H., DAFx-26 demo paper, 3 pages, CC BY 4.0.
- Stable URL: [official DAFx card](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/pU4J3deBDpTamQ8VW4smzg) · [full text](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_demo_69.pdf)
- Reading depth: `full_text_complete` for the complete demo paper.

The system takes a mixture plus MIDI score, aligns them using memory-restricted multiscale DTW, estimates source-wise F0/loudness/timbre trajectories with a diffusion model guided by a multi-scale spectral loss through a DDSP mixture renderer, and then regenerates only the selected source's parameters while leaving the other estimated parameter trajectories unchanged.

The useful design principle is explicit edit scope: choose the target source and editable condition, freeze non-target parameter trajectories, and retain an unchanged reconstruction control. This is not yet evidence of transparent source-wise mix editing. The demonstration uses two-flute mixtures and shows flute-to-violin conversion plus note-level phrasing changes. It reports no controlled listening test, no numeric fidelity table, no non-target null residual, and no phase/transient/peak/tail evaluation. A score is required and the model is limited to harmonic pitched sources represented by the pretrained DDSP autoencoder.

## Revised conclusions

1. Warm initialization is a controllable candidate mechanism, not a universal audio repair rule. Its useful operating point is model-, CFG-, prompt- and task-dependent.
2. Lower FAD does not prove preservation: the reference and transfer CFG differ, and FAD does not detect extra notes, attacks, gain or clipping.
3. Additional diffusion noise is not automatically beneficial; in the studied case it increased hallucinated musical events without meaningful distributional gain.
4. “Without explicit source separation” in the DDSP mixture demo does not prove that an unedited source is acoustically unchanged. Frozen estimated parameters still pass through an imperfect analysis-by-synthesis renderer.
5. A source-wise editing claim needs two independent gates: reconstruction fidelity before any edit, then off-target residual after the selected edit.
