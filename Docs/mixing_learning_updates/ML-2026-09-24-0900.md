## Sources actually read

### `SRC-ML-20260924-0900-01` — MIDIBack

- **Title:** *MIDIBack: Harmony-Aware Singing Pitch Correction via Joint Vocal-Accompaniment Symbolic Modeling*
- **Primary URL:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28008>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28008v1`
- **Publication / discovery:** submitted `2026-09-23`; discovered `2026-09-24`
- **Depth:** full HTML read: method, data, perturbations, tables, ablations, case study, conclusion
- **Status:** preprint; no public implementation verified

MIDIBack predicts note-level vocal pitch from joint vocal/accompaniment OctupleMIDI. Vocal and backing transcriptions are produced by GAME and MuScriptor; madmom supplies timing metadata; an Adversarial-MidiBERT backbone predicts corrected vocal pitch while accompaniment tokens remain an invariant anchor.

The target is the rounded pitch center of the source transcription, not a human score. Training corruptions are simulated: phrase-level global outshift of `±0.25–1.0` semitone, a learned GRU detuner, and uniform noise up to `±1` semitone. The corpus comprises 2,356 multitrack songs / 66.2 vocal hours from five sources and uses an `80/10/10` split with seed 42, but the paper does not document song-, singer-, artist-, or source-dataset-disjoint grouping.

The complete system reports `78.6%` overall note RPA. Accompaniment is especially important for synthetic global outshift: the paper's ablation falls from `81.5%` to `35.8%` when accompaniment is removed. The same context also increases clean-note changes: clean RPA is `91.3%` for the complete system versus `94.6%` without accompaniment. That is a useful over-correction warning, not merely a headline improvement.

Audio is reduced to 22.05-kHz mono for transcription and evaluation remains symbolic: RPA, pitch-class RPA, MAE, correlation, and corruption rate. No end-to-end waveform correction/resynthesis listening test is reported. The authors explicitly describe the work as recovery from simulated corruptions rather than a perceptual validation of vocal tuning. Moises AI support and Moises-related data/components are disclosed; this does not invalidate the work, but independent replication is absent.

**Bounded conclusion:** harmonic context is useful evidence for disambiguating synthetic note-wide pitch shifts. It is not evidence that MIDIBack is an autonomous, transparent, or preferred audio pitch-correction engine.

### `SRC-ML-20260924-0900-02` — phase-aware plate parameter estimation

- **Title:** *A Multi-Resolution Spectrogram Approach for Estimating the Physical Parameters of a Plate Reverb*
- **Primary URL:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28320>
- **Stable ID:** `arXiv:2609.28320v1`; DAFx-26 paper
- **Publication / discovery:** submitted `2026-09-23`; discovered `2026-09-24`
- **Depth:** full HTML read: preprocessing, architecture, evaluation, ambiguity study, timing, limitations
- **License stated on arXiv:** CC BY 4.0

The method uses a roughly 11.7-M-parameter ResNet-18 over three spectrogram resolutions plus an optional phase-derived fourth channel. The input impulse response is downsampled by 30 to 1,470 Hz so the estimator focuses on the first 20 isolated modes. This is a deliberate low-modal-overlap system-identification regime, not a full-band reverb-quality analysis.

In the authors' local 200-IR evaluation, adding phase reduces output-location ambiguity: the x/y NMSE terms move from `0.0035/0.0033` to `0.0033/0.0017`, while total mean NMSE changes from `0.0061` to `0.0058`. The abstract also reports `0.02920` over a 5,000-IR validation set. These numbers are from different protocols and must not be merged into one performance claim. Reported inference for a five-second IR is about `6.47 ms` on an M2 MacBook Air, but the model is still evaluated on synthetic simulator-matched data.

**Bounded conclusion:** phase can reduce a specific coordinate ambiguity in synthetic low-mode plate inversion. It does not establish perceptual improvement, real-plate robustness, or a deployable full-band reverb rule.

### `SRC-ML-20260924-0900-03` — official DAFx challenge comparator

- **Title:** *The 1st DAFx Challenge: Physical and Modal Parameter Estimation for Plate Reverberation*
- **Primary URL:** <https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_challenge_76.pdf>
- **Stable ID:** `DAFx26_challenge_76`
- **Publication / discovery:** DAFx-26 proceedings; discovered `2026-09-24`
- **Depth:** full eight-page report read

The official report shows that both challenge tasks use synthetic impulse responses from the same damped Kirchhoff–Love generator and parameter ranges available to participants. The hidden test comprises only 16 IRs from a secret seed. The organizers explicitly warn that this setup may reward dense interpolation of a known generator/range rather than a genuinely general inverse solver; proposed future tests include out-of-range cases, noise, free-edge boundaries, and a physical plate.

The plate-spectrogram method above is entry `#8-A`, ranked eighth in Task A, with official mean NMSE `0.006476`, median `0.003`, and surface-density mean/median SMSE `0.674/0.515`. The official result is close to the authors' local 200-IR mean, but not to the separate 5,000-IR validation number. Stronger challenge methods reach near-machine precision on this matched benchmark, which further limits any state-of-the-art claim for `#8-A`.

The report also clarifies the data contract: scientific evaluation requires absolute-amplitude displacement from NPZ; peak-normalized WAV is only for convenient listening and destroys information needed for surface density and modal gains. Task B additionally shows that modal-gain estimation is difficult and that ranking changes with the metric.

**Bounded conclusion:** this is a useful controlled system-identification benchmark, but it is not an independent real-world confirmation of the simulator-trained estimator or of musical reverb quality.

### `EXP-ML-20260924-0900-01` — accompaniment-guided target notes in a fixed correction engine

- **Baseline A:** vocal-only target-note predictor feeding one fixed, bounded conventional pitch-correction engine.
- **Variant B:** the same predictor/engine with accompaniment tokens available; accompaniment context is the only changed factor.
- **Material:** rights-cleared multitracks with human note annotations; stratify clean, globally shifted, chromatic/blue-note, vibrato, and intentionally dissonant phrases.
- **Controls:** identical transcription, boundaries, engine parameters, stems, seed set, gain, render path, and correction limits. Preserve raw renders for safety; make separate integrated-loudness-matched blind copies for preference.
- **Measurements:** target-note accuracy, clean-note corruption, cents error, onset/timing, formant proxy, consonant/HF residual, sample/true peak, mono/stereo residual, and blind preference.
- **Protected qualities:** lyric intelligibility, singer identity, expressive intonation, vibrato, consonants, timing, stereo, and headroom.
- **Success:** repeatable improvement on genuinely ambiguous notes without higher clean-note corruption or protected-quality loss.
- **Cancel:** split leakage, target derived from the evaluated transcription, increased clean-note edits, audible artifacts, or non-repeatable preference.
